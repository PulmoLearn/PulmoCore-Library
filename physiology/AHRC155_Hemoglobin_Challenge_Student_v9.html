Before you move the curve: why is it S-shaped?

Hemoglobin has four oxygen-binding heme sites. Binding the first O₂ makes the next O₂ easier to bind: this is positive cooperativity. As hemoglobin gains oxygen, its affinity increases. When O₂ is released in tissues, subsequent O₂ molecules become easier to release.

Lower portion · low PO₂

Many hemoglobin molecules have released most of their oxygen. At very low PO₂, saturation approaches zero.

Steep middle · unloading zone

Cooperative interactions make relatively small PO₂ changes cause large saturation changes. This helps supply tissues as PO₂ decreases.

Upper plateau · loading reserve

At higher PO₂, most sites are occupied. A moderate fall in alveolar/arterial PO₂ produces a smaller change in saturation.

P50 explained: P50 is the PO₂ at which 50% of available hemoglobin oxygen-binding sites are occupied. Normal adult P50 is approximately 26–27 mm Hg. Right shift = higher P50 (lower affinity); left shift = lower P50 (higher affinity). P50 is not the amount of oxygen being delivered.

Try this first: move PO₂ from 100 → 60 → 40 → 20 mm Hg. Watch where the saturation changes most rapidly. Then keep PO₂ fixed at 40 while changing temperature or pH.
