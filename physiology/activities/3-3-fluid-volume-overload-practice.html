Fluid Volume Overload: Why Did the Fluid Move?

Edema is a consequence, not one diagnosis. Use four questions throughout this activity: Did pressure push fluid out? Did low protein weaken the pull inward? Did the barrier become leaky? Did lymphatic drainage fail? Then connect fluid location to cardiopulmonary consequences.

Build the Normal Capillary-Exchange Map

→
Capillary hydrostatic pressure

Pressure inside the capillary pushes water outward toward the interstitium.

←
Plasma oncotic pressure

Albumin and other plasma proteins create an inward force that favors vascular retention.

⇄
Capillary permeability

An intact barrier limits protein movement. Barrier injury allows protein-rich fluid to cross more readily.

↩
Lymphatic drainage

Returns excess filtered interstitial fluid and escaped proteins back to the circulation.

Memory framework: edema can begin as a pressure problem, protein problem, barrier problem, or drainage problem.

Why Can the Body Retain More Fluid When Edema Is Already Visible?

The kidney responds to effective arterial perfusion, not simply to how swollen the patient looks.

RAAS

Renal perfusion / distal Na delivery ↓→Renin ↑→Angiotensin II→Vasoconstriction + aldosterone→Na⁺ and water retention ↑

ADH

ADH ↑→Collecting-duct water permeability ↑→Water reabsorption ↑

Because water can be retained without an equivalent amount of sodium, excessive ADH can contribute to dilutional hyponatremia.

Natriuretic peptides

Atrial/ventricular stretch ↑→Natriuresis + water loss + vasodilation

These hormones oppose excessive volume/pressure loading and counter RAAS.

Why heart failure is confusing

Low forward cardiac output can reduce effective renal perfusion while venous pressure and total-body fluid are already high. RAAS/ADH can therefore remain active during visible congestion.

Trace Pulmonary Edema From Fluid to Hypoxemia

Interstitial fluid ↑→Diffusion distance ↑+Lung stiffness ↑→Compliance ↓→Elastic work ↑
Alveolar fluid ↑→Ventilated volume ↓whilePerfusion may continue→Low V/Q / shunt-like units ↑→Oxygenation ↓
ComplianceFluid-laden and unstable lung units need more pressure to expand.
SurfactantAlveolar fluid can dilute/disrupt surfactant, increasing surface tension and instability.
DiffusionFluid increases the distance oxygen must cross; oxygen is affected earlier than CO₂ because CO₂ diffuses more readily.
Work of breathingStiffer lungs increase elastic work and favor a rapid, shallow breathing pattern.
One process, two failures: pulmonary edema can simultaneously make the lung harder to inflate and make gas exchange worse.

Positive Pressure: Respiratory Benefit, Hemodynamic Cost

Spontaneous inspiration

Intrathoracic pressure becomes more negative→RA pressure relative to peripheral veins ↓→Venous-return gradient ↑

Positive-pressure breathing

Intrathoracic pressure ↑→Pressure around RA/thoracic veins ↑→Venous-return gradient ↓→RV preload may ↓
A fluid-overloaded patient can still be preload sensitive. Interstitial edema may coexist with limited effective circulating volume or low forward flow.
Effects can move in opposite directions: positive pressure may recruit unstable alveoli and improve SpO₂ while simultaneously reducing venous return, preload, cardiac output, and blood pressure.
