Trace One Red Blood Cell Through the Heart

Arteries and veins are named by direction relative to the heart, not oxygen content. Pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood away from the RV; pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood to the LA.
Right heartVena cavae → RA → tricuspid → RV
To lungsPulmonic valve → pulmonary arteries
From lungsPulmonary veins → LA → mitral → LV
To bodyAortic valve → aorta → systemic circulation
Valve rule: valves open and close passively when pressure gradients reverse. Papillary muscles/chordae stabilize the AV valves; they do not pull them open.

Build the Normal Cardiac Conduction Pathway

SA nodeInitiates the normal impulse and atrial depolarization.
AV nodeDelays conduction so atrial contraction can finish before ventricular systole.
His / bundle branchesCarry activation rapidly into the ventricular conduction system.
Purkinje systemDistributes activation through the ventricles for coordinated depolarization.
Normal AV delay is useful. A prolonged PR means slower atria-to-ventricle conduction; if every P is still followed by QRS, activation may remain one-to-one.

Four Phases of the Cardiac Cycle

Pressure changes first. Valve positions change when pressure gradients reverse. Ventricular volume changes only when an inflow or outflow valve is open.
Ventricular fillingAV openSemilunar closed

Ventricular volume rises; most filling is passive.

Isovolumetric contractionAll valves closedS1

Pressure rises while volume stays constant.

EjectionSemilunar openAV closed

Ventricular volume falls as blood is ejected.

Isovolumetric relaxationAll valves closedS2

Pressure falls while volume stays constant.

Heart sounds are transition markers: S1 follows AV closure; S2 follows semilunar closure.

Rate, Filling, Atrial Contribution & Coronary Perfusion

Atrial contribution

Most filling is passive. Atrial contraction contributes the final portion of EDV and matters more when ventricular compliance is low or filling time is short.

Coronary perfusion

LV contraction compresses intramyocardial vessels during systole, so left-coronary flow occurs mainly during diastole.

Heart-rate / diastolic-time demonstration

Try it: drag HR higher. Watch relative filling/coronary-perfusion opportunity shrink. This is conceptual, not an exact timing equation.

Relative diastolic opportunity: 100%

Higher rate does not raise output forever. At marked tachycardia, shortened diastole can reduce EDV and stroke volume enough to lower cardiac output while also reducing coronary-perfusion time.

Electrical Activity and Mechanical Output Can Separate

Use this chain: impulse formation → conduction → ECG event → chamber response → pressure → valve/flow → perfusion.
Conduction failureAtrial impulses can continue while fewer activate the ventricles, lowering effective ventricular rate.
Mechanical failureA QRS can occur without enough pressure generation for a useful stroke volume or pulse.
Artifact / mismatchIf the monitor changes but pulse/BP do not, verify leads and signal quality first.
Timing failureLoss of atrial contraction or extreme tachycardia can reduce filling despite ongoing depolarization.
