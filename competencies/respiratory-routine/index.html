Respiratory Routine

Build a consistent bedside respiratory assessment routine, then use the findings to decide what the patient needs next.

Teach

Teach

Use the Same Clinical Routine Every Time

A routine creates consistency. The patient findings determine whether you continue routine monitoring, intervene, reassess, or escalate.

1

PREPARE

Know Why You Are Entering

Review the order and relevant chart information. Gather only the tools needed for the planned assessment.

Think: What am I expected to assess, and what equipment supports that assessment?
2

CONNECT

Start With the Patient

Perform hand hygiene, introduce yourself, use two patient identifiers, explain the assessment, and position the patient appropriately.

3

MEASURE

Collect Reliable Baseline Data

Measure pulse and respiratory rate without cueing the patient to alter breathing. Apply pulse oximetry correctly and judge whether the signal is believable.

4

INSPECT & LISTEN

Look Before You Interpret

Observe breathing pattern, effort, symmetry, color, posture, and general appearance. Auscultate systematically and compare side-to-side.

5

INTERPRET

Combine the Findings

Do not treat respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, breath sounds, or appearance as isolated numbers. Ask whether the findings agree with one another and with the patient's condition.

What did I observe?Are the measurements reliable?Do the findings fit together?Does the patient need action?What must be reassessed?
6

CLOSE THE LOOP

Finish the Encounter Completely

Return the patient to a safe position, clean equipment as appropriate, perform hand hygiene, document the assessment, and communicate clinically significant findings.

Patient 1 is guided. The interface will coach you through the routine and explain why each step matters. Later patients will remove those supports.
