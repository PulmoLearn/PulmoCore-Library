Critical Care Transport & Oxygen Planning

Transporting a critically ill patient is not simply moving the bed. It is a temporary ICU on wheels. This lesson focuses on choosing the right equipment, calculating oxygen needs, verifying portable ventilator settings, maintaining airway rescue readiness, monitoring during transport, and troubleshooting when equipment or patient stability changes.

Learning Objectives

  1. Identify when a critically ill patient is ready, not ready, or conditionally ready for transport.
  2. Select transport equipment for oxygenation, ventilation, airway rescue, suction, monitoring, and emergency backup.
  3. Calculate oxygen cylinder duration and plan a safe oxygen reserve for transport.
  4. Verify portable ventilator settings and alarm readiness before disconnecting from the bedside ventilator.
  5. Monitor oxygenation, ventilation, airway security, and patient tolerance throughout transport.
  6. Troubleshoot common transport problems including low oxygen supply, circuit disconnection, high-pressure alarms, low volume, secretion obstruction, and battery failure.
  7. Document transport settings, events, interventions, patient response, and handoff communication.
Equipment focus: This lesson does not re-teach transport physiology. It teaches the bedside workflow: prepare equipment, verify settings, calculate oxygen, monitor continuously, and respond to problems quickly.

Transport Go / No-Go Decision

FIRST DECISION: SHOULD THIS PATIENT LEAVE?

Before collecting equipment, decide whether the patient is stable enough to transport. A transport should not begin until the destination, route, equipment, personnel, and backup plan are clear.

Ready to Transport
Airway secure · oxygenation stable on current support · hemodynamics acceptable · transport monitor available · oxygen reserve calculated · suction/BVM ready · destination prepared.
Conditional / Delay Briefly
Need to replace low cylinder · suction setup missing · portable vent not checked · battery not charged · route not clear · patient needs preoxygenation or secretion clearance before departure.
Do Not Transport Yet
Unstable airway · worsening hypoxemia · severe hemodynamic instability · no backup airway/ventilation plan · inadequate oxygen supply · uncorrected equipment failure · destination not ready.

Oxygen Cylinder Planning

HIGH-YIELD: NEVER TRANSPORT WITHOUT OXYGEN RESERVE

Oxygen duration must be calculated before transport. Use the appropriate cylinder factor, subtract the safe residual pressure, and divide by total flow. For ventilators, use the estimated oxygen consumption from the transport ventilator or facility policy when available.

CYLINDER DURATION
Duration (min) = [(Cylinder pressure − Safe residual pressure) × Cylinder factor] ÷ Flow (L/min)
Common factors: E cylinder ≈ 0.28 · H cylinder ≈ 3.14
Step 1
Estimate total trip time: leave ICU → destination → procedure wait → return. Add delay time.
Step 2
Calculate cylinder duration using total oxygen flow or ventilator oxygen consumption.
Step 3
Plan reserve. A tank that lasts “just long enough” is not safe. Replace low tanks before departure.

Portable Ventilator Setup

The portable ventilator must be treated as a new ventilator connection. The RT verifies the ordered settings, alarms, power, oxygen source, circuit, and patient response before leaving the bedside.

Verification PointWhat to ConfirmCommon Error
ModePortable ventilator mode matches intended support or has approved equivalentAssuming bedside mode names translate exactly to transport vent modes
VT / pressureOrdered tidal volume or inspiratory pressure entered correctlyEntering mL/kg target incorrectly or failing to account for pressure-control differences
RR, PEEP, FiO₂Settings copied/ordered and verified on patientForgetting PEEP or using lower FiO₂ during transfer than at bedside
AlarmsHigh pressure, low pressure/disconnect, low volume, apnea, oxygen/battery alarms activeSilenced or default alarms too broad to detect deterioration
HumidificationHME or transport humidification strategy appropriate for time and secretionsUsing HME despite thick/bloody secretions or aerosol therapy needs

Monitoring During Transport

Transport monitoring must answer two questions continuously: “Is the airway still secure?” and “Is the patient still being oxygenated and ventilated?”

Oxygenation
SpO₂ trend, color, FiO₂, oxygen supply pressure, visible chest rise, work of breathing if spontaneous.
Ventilation
ETCO₂ waveform/value, ventilator exhaled VT, RR, minute ventilation, chest rise, breath sounds if deterioration occurs.
Airway Security
ETT/trach depth marking, securement device, circuit tension, HME/filter obstruction, condensation, accidental disconnect risk.
Patient Tolerance
HR/rhythm, BP, agitation, dyssynchrony, diaphoresis, cyanosis, sudden change in respiratory pattern or mental status.
ETCO₂ transport pearl: A present waveform helps confirm ongoing ventilation through the artificial airway. Sudden loss of waveform may indicate disconnection, apnea, airway displacement, cardiac arrest/low perfusion, or equipment failure. Always assess the patient first.

Transport Troubleshooting

STABILIZE FIRST, THEN TROUBLESHOOT

When a transport problem occurs, quickly assess the patient, manually ventilate if needed, call for help, and correct the equipment issue.

ProblemLikely CauseImmediate RT Action
High-pressure alarmSecretions, kinked tubing, biting, bronchospasm, water in circuit, patient coughingAssess patient/chest rise; check tubing; suction if needed; manually ventilate if unstable; call for help
Low-pressure / disconnect alarmCircuit disconnect, leak, cuff leak, loose adapter, oxygen supply failureReconnect circuit; check cuff and adapters; switch to BVM if ventilation compromised
SpO₂ droppingOxygen depletion, disconnect, worsening lung condition, secretion obstruction, displaced airwayIncrease FiO₂ if available; check cylinder pressure; assess airway; suction; manually ventilate; return to ICU if unstable
ETCO₂ suddenly absentDisconnect, apnea, sensor issue, airway displacement, cardiac arrest/low perfusionAssess pulse/chest rise; check connections and sensor; manually ventilate; call emergency response as needed
Battery alarmPortable ventilator/monitor battery lowPlug into power immediately if available; switch to spare battery; prepare BVM backup
Portable suction not workingBattery dead, tubing disconnected, canister full, suction not turned onTroubleshoot connections/canister; replace device if available; return to unit if suction needed and unavailable

Special Transport Situations

MRI Transport
Only MRI-conditional equipment enters MRI zones. Standard cylinders, regulators, ventilators, monitors, beds, and pumps may be unsafe. Confirm MRI-compatible oxygen source, ventilator, monitor, suction, and emergency plan before leaving.
Isolation / Infection Control
Use appropriate PPE, cover/contain patient secretions, coordinate route if needed, prevent circuit disconnections, and avoid contaminating elevators/hallways. Bring extra supplies to avoid borrowing equipment mid-transport.
Procedural Destination
Before leaving: destination oxygen source available, wall suction present, power available, staff expecting patient, transfer surface ready, and there is space for the ventilator, monitor, pumps, and RT access to the airway.

Handoff, Documentation & Debriefing

Transport documentation creates a safety record: how the patient was supported, what changed, and what was done.

Document Before Departure
Baseline SpO₂/ETCO₂/HR/BP · ventilator settings · oxygen cylinder pressure/duration · airway depth/securement · suction and BVM readiness.
Document During / After
Transport destination · times out/back · settings used · patient response · complications · alarms · interventions · oxygen used · handoff communication.
Handoff
Report airway type/depth, vent settings, oxygen source, sedation/comfort issues, suction needs, recent alarms, and what to do if the patient deteriorates.
Debrief When Needed
After adverse events or near misses, briefly identify what happened, what worked, what equipment failed/missing, and what should change before the next transport.

Summary & Board-Prep Reminders

Before Transport
Confirm destination, route, personnel, airway security, portable vent, oxygen reserve, suction, BVM, monitor, battery, and handoff plan.
Oxygen Math
Duration = [(psi − residual) × cylinder factor] ÷ flow. Always plan reserve for delays.
During Transport
Monitor SpO₂, ETCO₂, ventilator pressures/volumes, airway depth, circuit integrity, HR/BP, patient appearance, and oxygen supply.
If Trouble Occurs
Assess patient first. Manually ventilate if needed. Check airway/circuit/oxygen/power. Suction if indicated. Call for help and return to ICU if unstable.
