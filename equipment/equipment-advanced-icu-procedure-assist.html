Advanced ICU Procedure Assist — ETCO₂, Bronchoscopy, Apnea Test, Lines, Transport & Debriefing

Advanced ICU procedures are not just “watch the provider.” The RT prepares oxygenation, ventilation, suction, monitoring, emergency equipment, and documentation pathways so procedures can be performed safely. This lesson focuses on equipment choice, setup, stop criteria, troubleshooting, transport readiness, and post-event debriefing.

Learning Objectives

  1. Select and set up appropriate ETCO₂, exhaled CO₂, or exhaled CO monitoring equipment for common ICU scenarios.
  2. Troubleshoot mainstream and sidestream capnography problems using patient assessment and equipment checks.
  3. Prepare bronchoscopy equipment, monitoring, suction, oxygen, ventilator adjustments, and post-procedure checks.
  4. Assist with apnea testing by preparing oxygenation equipment, baseline data, monitoring, stop criteria, and documentation.
  5. Prepare respiratory support and monitoring for arterial or venous catheter insertion procedures.
  6. Use a critical care transport checklist to prevent oxygen, power, ventilator, monitoring, and suction failures.
  7. Participate in adverse-event debriefing to identify equipment, communication, and process changes.

The RT Role in Advanced ICU Procedure Assist

PROCEDURE ASSIST = AIRWAY + OXYGENATION + MONITORING + EMERGENCY READINESS

For advanced ICU procedures, the RT often does not perform the invasive procedure, but the RT is responsible for keeping the patient ventilated, oxygenated, monitored, and ready for rapid escalation.

Before the Procedure
Verify order and patient identifiers · review oxygen/ventilator status · gather suction and airway rescue supplies · confirm monitoring · prepare procedure-specific equipment · participate in time-out.
During the Procedure
Monitor SpO₂, ETCO₂, HR, BP, ventilator pressures, work of breathing · maintain oxygen and ventilation · manage alarms · suction as needed · speak up if the patient destabilizes.
After the Procedure
Reassess breath sounds, SpO₂, ETCO₂, airway position, ventilator settings, secretion burden, cuff pressure if applicable, and patient tolerance. Return equipment to safe baseline.
Escalation Rule
If the patient is unstable, the procedure pauses unless the provider explicitly directs otherwise. Stabilize oxygenation and ventilation first, then continue troubleshooting.

ETCO₂ and Exhaled Gas Analysis Equipment

NBRC GAP: BOK-I.E.10 — EXHALED GAS ANALYSIS

Exhaled gas monitoring gives immediate information about ventilation, perfusion, airway placement, and equipment function. The RT must choose the correct monitoring device and recognize when the number or waveform is wrong because of equipment setup rather than patient physiology.

Mainstream ETCO₂
Sensor sits directly between the airway and circuit. Fast response. Common during intubation, mechanical ventilation, transport, and CPR. Watch for added weight, dead space, secretion contamination, and calibration/warm-up needs.
Sidestream ETCO₂
Small sample line pulls gas to a monitor. Useful for nonintubated patients and procedural sedation. Watch for water/secretions in the sample line, low sampling flow, nasal cannula displacement, and delay in waveform response.
Exhaled CO / CO₂
CO monitoring may be used for smoke inhalation or carbon monoxide exposure screening. CO₂ monitoring may be used for ventilation trend, CPR quality/ROSC clues, and airway verification. Device-specific calibration and sampling matter.

Procedural sedationSidestream nasal/oral sampling cannulaSample line connected and cannula positionedLow ETCO₂ from cannula displacement or mouth breathing without oral scoop

Critical reminder: A flat capnogram is an emergency until proven otherwise. Check patient, airway, circuit, sensor connection, and perfusion. Never assume it is “just the monitor” before assessing the patient.

Bronchoscopy Assist: Equipment and Workflow

PROCEDURE ASSIST: BRONCHOSCOPY

Bronchoscopy can be diagnostic or therapeutic. From the RT equipment perspective, the priorities are oxygenation, ventilation, suction, specimen handling, airway access, and rapid response to desaturation or bronchospasm.

Core Equipment
Bronchoscope tower/light source or disposable scope system · suction source and tubing · specimen traps · saline syringes · bite block · oxygen source · BVM · airway rescue supplies.
Ventilated Patient Setup
Confirm ETT/trach size can accommodate bronchoscope · increase FiO₂ per protocol/order · monitor peak pressures and exhaled VT · use swivel adapter if available · maintain PEEP when possible.
Monitoring
Continuous SpO₂, HR/rhythm, BP, ventilator pressures, exhaled volume, ETCO₂ if available, patient tolerance, cough/bronchospasm, and sedation level.

Bronchoscopy Procedure Traps

  • Scope partially obstructs the ETT. Expect increased resistance, higher pressures, lower exhaled volume, and possible CO₂ retention during the procedure.
  • Desaturation can happen quickly. Increase FiO₂ before the procedure if ordered/protocol allows, but continue monitoring because preoxygenation does not eliminate risk.
  • Specimen traps must be connected correctly. Incorrect trap placement or failure to label specimens can invalidate the procedure result.
  • Post-procedure assessment is not optional. Reassess breath sounds, oxygenation, ventilator settings, secretion burden, and airway position after the scope is removed.

Apnea Test Assist: Equipment, Monitoring, and Stop Criteria

NBRC GAP: BOK-I.C.13 / BOK-I.D.14 — APNEA TEST

The apnea test is performed by the provider as part of brain death evaluation according to facility policy. The RT's role is to prepare oxygenation equipment, ensure baseline data are available, support the provider during ventilator disconnection or apnea oxygenation, monitor closely, and speak up when stop criteria occur.

Before Test
Confirm provider order and policy · verify prerequisites per facility protocol · preoxygenate as ordered · obtain baseline ABG/PaCO₂ if required · confirm hemodynamic stability · prepare oxygen insufflation or CPAP/apnea oxygenation setup.
Equipment
Oxygen source and flowmeter · sterile catheter or apnea oxygenation adapter per protocol · pulse oximeter · ECG/BP monitoring · suction · BVM · ventilator ready to reconnect · ABG supplies/arterial line access if present.
During Test
Observe chest/abdomen for respiratory effort · monitor SpO₂, BP, rhythm, ETCO₂ if used · time test · be ready to reconnect ventilator immediately if stop criteria occur.
Stop Criteria Examples
Significant desaturation · hemodynamic instability · arrhythmia · provider-directed termination · equipment failure. Always follow facility policy and provider direction.
PROCEDURAL PRINCIPLE
Prepare for the test, but prepare even harder for rapid reconnection and stabilization.

Arterial or Venous Catheter Insertion Assist

NBRC GAP: BOK-III.H.7 — ASSIST WITH ARTERIAL OR VENOUS CATHETER INSERTION

The RT does not usually insert arterial or central venous catheters, but often supports respiratory stability while the provider performs the procedure. The equipment focus is oxygen delivery, suction, monitoring, ventilator adjustments, patient positioning, and readiness for deterioration.

Documentation focus: Chart respiratory status before/during/after, oxygen or ventilator changes, patient tolerance, complications, and communication with provider/RN.

Critical Care Transport Equipment Readiness

TRANSPORT = PORTABLE ICU

Transporting a critically ill patient requires anticipating every support the room normally provides: oxygen, ventilator power, suction, monitoring, medications/infusions, airway rescue, and enough reserve to return safely if delayed.

Oxygen Reserve
Calculate expected duration plus safety reserve · check tank pressure · ensure correct regulator · verify flow/ventilator oxygen connection · bring backup cylinder if needed.
Ventilator / BVM
Transport ventilator charged and set · circuit secure · alarms on · battery checked · manual resuscitator with PEEP valve if needed · mask and airway adapters available.
Monitoring / Suction
Portable pulse oximetry, ECG, BP, ETCO₂ if indicated · suction source with catheter/Yankauer · ensure tubing length and battery/power are adequate.

Transport Failure Prevention Checklist

  • Before leaving: Confirm destination, route, oxygen supply, ventilator battery, monitor battery, suction, emergency airway supplies, and personnel roles.
  • During movement: Watch tubing tension, avoid circuit disconnects, keep oxygen cylinder visible/secured, and monitor SpO₂/ETCO₂ trends.
  • At destination: Reconnect to wall oxygen/power when possible, confirm ventilator settings, reassess breath sounds and airway security.
  • Before return: Recalculate oxygen reserve. Delays in imaging/procedural areas are common.

Debriefing Following Adverse Patient Events

NBRC GAP: BOK-III.G.4 — DEBRIEFING

Debriefing after an adverse event is not about blame. It is a structured way to identify what happened, what equipment or communication issues contributed, and what should change before the next patient encounter.

What Went Well?
What equipment worked? Who recognized the problem? What communication helped? What should be repeated next time?
What Was Difficult?
Was equipment missing? Was the monitor hard to see? Was oxygen reserve insufficient? Did roles become unclear?
What Will We Change?
Update checklist · restock transport bag · label adapters · clarify who brings suction · add ETCO₂ check · revise handoff.
What Must Be Documented?
Objective event facts · interventions · patient response · provider notification · equipment failures · safety report if required by policy.

Summary & Board-Prep Reminders

ETCO₂ / Exhaled Gas
A flat waveform is an emergency until patient, airway, circuit, sensor, sampling line, and perfusion are assessed. Match device to clinical scenario: mainstream for airway/ventilation, sidestream for sedation/nonintubated use.
Bronchoscopy
Prepare suction, oxygen/BVM, airway rescue, specimen traps, ventilator adapters, and monitoring. Watch for desaturation, high pressures, low exhaled volume, bronchospasm, and secretion obstruction.
Apnea Test
The RT prepares oxygenation and rapid reconnection. Monitor continuously. Significant desaturation, instability, arrhythmia, or equipment failure means stop and stabilize per protocol/provider direction.
Transport & Debrief
Transport is a portable ICU. Check oxygen reserve, battery, suction, monitoring, airway rescue, and tubing security. Debrief adverse events to fix equipment and process gaps.
