Invasive Lines, A-Line Sampling & Pressure Monitoring Equipment

Respiratory therapists often obtain arterial blood samples from arterial lines and assist during ICU line procedures. This lesson focuses on equipment selection, setup, sampling workflow, pressure monitoring safety, waveform troubleshooting, and when to escalate concerns.

Learning Objectives

  1. Identify arterial line, central venous line, and pressure monitoring equipment used in ICU procedure assist.
  2. Prepare and check a pressure monitoring system, including transducer, flush solution, pressure bag, stopcocks, and monitor cable.
  3. Explain how to level and zero a transducer and why incorrect leveling creates inaccurate pressures.
  4. Recognize waveform problems such as overdamping, underdamping, air bubbles, clot, kink, and flush system failure.
  5. Perform a safe arterial line blood sampling workflow using closed or open sampling systems.
  6. Document oxygen/ventilator settings, sampling source, patient response, and line complications.
  7. Describe the RT support role during arterial or venous catheter insertion without crossing sterile role boundaries.
Boundary reminder: This lesson is equipment/procedure support focused. Local policy determines whether the RT may draw from an arterial line, assist with line placement, or manage transducer equipment independently.

Line Types RTs Commonly Encounter

Arterial Line
Invasive catheter placed in an artery, commonly radial, femoral, or brachial. Used for continuous blood pressure monitoring and repeated arterial blood sampling. RT relevance: ABG sampling, waveform reliability, oxygenation/ventilation decisions.
Central Venous Catheter
Catheter inserted into a large central vein, such as internal jugular, subclavian, or femoral. Used for medications, vasoactive infusions, volume access, CVP monitoring if connected. RT relevance: procedure assist, oxygenation during insertion, sterile support, line safety awareness.
Pulmonary Artery Catheter
Advanced hemodynamic catheter used in select ICU patients. RT relevance: recognize it as invasive monitoring, avoid dislodgement during airway care/transport, understand that waveform changes require clinician attention.
Safety priority: Any disconnection, bleeding, sudden waveform loss, neurovascular change, or line contamination is an immediate escalation issue.

Pressure Monitoring Setup

SETUP SEQUENCE
  1. Verify provider order, patient identity, line type, and facility policy for RT role.
  2. Inspect insertion site and dressing without contaminating the sterile field.
  3. Confirm tubing is connected from catheter to transducer with no open stopcocks.
  4. Prime flush tubing completely; remove all air bubbles from tubing, stopcocks, and transducer.
  5. Inflate pressure bag to the standard pressure, commonly 300 mmHg, or per policy.
  6. Connect transducer cable to the monitor and select the correct invasive pressure channel.
  7. Level the transducer at the phlebostatic axis before zeroing.
  8. Zero the transducer to atmospheric pressure using the stopcock open to air, then close it to air and reopen to the patient.
  9. Verify waveform, numeric pressure, flush response, and alarm settings.
Never ignore air: Air bubbles dampen the waveform and can create inaccurate pressure readings. Remove air before interpreting the pressure or drawing a sample.

Leveling and Zeroing

Leveling
Align the transducer with the phlebostatic axis, approximately the 4th intercostal space at the mid-axillary line for heart-level reference. If the transducer is too low, pressure reads falsely high. If too high, pressure reads falsely low.
Zeroing
Zeroing tells the monitor what atmospheric pressure is. Turn the stopcock off to the patient and open to air; press zero on the monitor; then close to air and reopen to the patient. Zeroing does not fix an incorrectly leveled transducer.

Transducer below heart levelFalsely high pressureRaise transducer to phlebostatic axis
Transducer above heart levelFalsely low pressureLower transducer to phlebostatic axis

Arterial Waveform Reliability

Before acting on a number or interpreting an ABG trend, confirm that the monitoring system is reliable.

Normal Waveform
Sharp upstroke, clear systolic peak, dicrotic notch, and stable diastolic runoff. Numeric pressure should match clinical picture and cuff trend reasonably.
Overdamped
Flattened waveform, absent dicrotic notch, falsely low systolic and falsely high diastolic. Causes: air bubbles, clot, kink, loose connection, catheter against vessel wall.
Underdamped
Exaggerated sharp waveform with ringing; systolic falsely high and diastolic falsely low. Causes: overly stiff tubing, excessive tubing length/resonance, system artifact.

Arterial Line Blood Sampling Workflow

PROCEDURE FOCUS

Follow facility policy and the line type in use. Closed sampling systems are preferred when available because they reduce blood waste and contamination risk.

  1. Verify the order, patient identity, current oxygen delivery or ventilator settings, and sample timing.
  2. Perform hand hygiene and use appropriate PPE.
  3. Inspect the line, waveform, flush system, stopcocks, and insertion site before sampling.
  4. Pause or note incompatible infusions only if applicable and allowed by policy; do not manipulate unrelated lines.
  5. Withdraw the dead space/discard volume or pull blood into the closed sampling reservoir per policy.
  6. Attach heparinized ABG syringe or sampling syringe using aseptic technique.
  7. Collect the sample without introducing air.
  8. Flush the line according to policy until the line is clear and waveform returns.
  9. Return discard volume if using a closed system and policy allows.
  10. Remove air bubbles from the ABG syringe, cap it, mix gently, label at bedside, and transport/analyze promptly.
  11. Reassess waveform, distal perfusion, bleeding, and patient tolerance.
  12. Document sample source, oxygen/vent settings, time, complications, and notification of abnormal results.

Arterial or Venous Catheter Insertion Assist

The RT role may include oxygenation/ventilation support, monitoring, equipment readiness, sterile support, and connection of pressure monitoring equipment after placement. The provider inserts the catheter unless facility scope and credentialing allow otherwise.

Before Procedure
Verify order and consent if applicable · confirm patient identity · prepare oxygen/BVM/suction · ensure monitor is active · gather transducer setup · position patient as requested · identify respiratory risk.
During Procedure
Maintain oxygenation and ventilation · avoid contaminating sterile field · hand supplies only as directed · monitor SpO₂, ECG, BP, and patient response · communicate respiratory deterioration immediately.
After Procedure
Connect pressure tubing if directed · level and zero transducer · confirm waveform · secure tubing to prevent pulling · inspect distal perfusion · document support provided and complications.
Escalate immediately: loss of pulse distal to arterial line, expanding hematoma, uncontrolled bleeding, sudden neurologic change, severe pain, contaminated sterile field, air in line, accidental disconnection, or respiratory instability during insertion.

Summary & Procedure Reminders

Before sampling
Verify order, patient, oxygen/vent settings, line source, waveform, flush system, and insertion site.
Before trusting a pressure
Level at phlebostatic axis, zero to air, remove air bubbles, verify pressure bag and waveform.
Before assisting insertion
Know your role, maintain oxygenation/monitoring, protect sterile field, prepare pressure setup, escalate complications.
High-yield safety points: Transducer below heart = falsely high pressure · transducer above heart = falsely low pressure · overdamped waveform often means air/clot/kink/loose connection · document exact oxygen/vent settings with ABG results · compromised distal perfusion is urgent.
