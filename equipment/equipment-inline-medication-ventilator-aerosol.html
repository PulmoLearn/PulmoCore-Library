Inline Medication Delivery During Mechanical Ventilation

Medication delivery through a ventilator circuit is not just “connect a nebulizer and turn it on.” The RT must select the correct device, protect the circuit, remove or bypass incompatible components, monitor the patient, and troubleshoot poor delivery without interrupting ventilation.

Why Inline Medication Delivery Is Different

In a spontaneously breathing patient, aerosol medication is delivered directly through a mouthpiece or mask. In a mechanically ventilated patient, the medication must travel through a pressurized breathing circuit, around adapters, through humidification equipment, and into an artificial airway. Every circuit component can either help or block medication delivery.

Primary Goal
Deliver medication while maintaining oxygenation, ventilation, circuit integrity, and airway safety.
Main Risk
Aerosol particles can obstruct HMEs or filters, increase airway resistance, and reduce delivered medication dose.
Board Trap
Never aerosolize through an HME. Remove or bypass the HME during treatment.

Circuit Placement Principles

Follow manufacturer and facility guidance, but use these core principles when checking setup.

Keep Circuit Closed
Use inline adapters when possible to avoid circuit breaks, derecruitment, and infection risk.
Position for Delivery
The medication must enter the inspiratory flow path and should not be trapped by an HME, filter, or pooled condensate.
Mind Humidification
Heated humidity can affect aerosol behavior, but do not remove required humidification unless following a protocol or device-specific instruction.

HME and Filter Safety

VERY HIGH-YIELD

An HME is a passive humidifier and can act like a filter barrier to aerosol particles. Aerosol therapy through an HME can clog the element, increase circuit resistance, trigger alarms, reduce medication delivery, and increase the patient's work of breathing.

Never do this
Do not run SVN, mesh aerosol, or MDI aerosol through an HME.
Correct action
Remove or bypass the HME during treatment. Reinstall after treatment if still appropriate and not contaminated/obstructed.

Inline SVN / Jet Nebulizer Setup

  1. Verify order, medication, dose, patient identity, and contraindications.
  2. Assess baseline breath sounds, HR, SpO₂, ventilator settings, peak pressure, and alarms.
  3. Inspect circuit for HME, filters, leaks, and condensate.
  4. Remove or bypass HME if present.
  5. Place nebulizer/adapter in the inspiratory circuit per facility/manufacturer instructions.
  6. Add medication to nebulizer cup and connect driving gas if required.
  7. Start nebulization and monitor ventilator response, aerosol output, and patient tolerance.
  8. When complete, remove or close adapter, restore circuit, verify alarms, and reassess patient.
Jet nebulizer warning: A jet nebulizer may add flow to the circuit. Depending on ventilator design, this can affect measured volume, delivered pressure, trigger sensitivity, or alarms. Stay at bedside during setup and early treatment.

Inline MDI Setup

  1. Confirm the MDI medication and ordered number of puffs.
  2. Attach MDI canister to the inline spacer/adapter.
  3. Shake the MDI before use.
  4. Coordinate actuation with the beginning of inspiration when possible.
  5. Allow several ventilator breaths between puffs so medication can distribute.
  6. Monitor for HR increase, tremor, improved breath sounds, pressure changes, or adverse response.
  7. Remove MDI canister, close adapter, and reassess circuit integrity.
MDI Advantage
Fast, compact, does not add external driving gas flow.
MDI Risk
Poor timing or adapter misuse can reduce dose delivery.
Safety Rule
HME still must not remain in the aerosol pathway.

Summary & Board-Prep Reminders

Remove the HME
Do not deliver aerosol through an HME or incompatible filter.
Keep the circuit safe
Use inline adapters, avoid unnecessary circuit breaks, and close all ports.
Monitor the ventilator
Watch pressure, volume, alarms, expiratory flow, and patient response.
