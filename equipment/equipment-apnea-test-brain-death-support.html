Apnea Test & Brain Death Determination Support

The apnea test is a provider-directed component of brain death determination. The RT role is equipment preparation, safe oxygenation support during ventilator disconnection or CPAP-based testing, close monitoring, rapid rescue readiness, ABG coordination, and clear documentation.

Learning Objectives

  1. Identify the RT role during provider-directed apnea testing for brain death determination.
  2. Select the correct oxygenation, suction, monitoring, and ABG equipment for apnea test support.
  3. Differentiate oxygen insufflation and CPAP-based apnea test support setups.
  4. Verify readiness criteria before ventilator disconnection or apnea test initiation.
  5. Monitor oxygenation, hemodynamics, respiratory effort, and safety conditions during the test.
  6. Recognize stop criteria and immediately reconnect/rescue the patient when needed.
  7. Document baseline data, setup method, ABG values, patient response, complications, and communication.

What the RT Does — and Does Not Do

RT Procedure Support
Prepare equipment, preoxygenate, support apneic oxygenation, ensure suction/BVM readiness, monitor continuously, draw or coordinate ABGs per facility policy, reconnect to the ventilator if the test is aborted, and communicate changes immediately.
Provider Responsibility
Brain death determination is a medical diagnosis and facility-policy-driven process. The RT does not independently decide to perform the test, interpret brain death, or continue testing when instability develops.
Board-Style Framing: For NBRC questions, focus on safe procedure support: correct setup, adequate preoxygenation, ABG timing, monitoring, and stopping the test when oxygenation or hemodynamics become unsafe.

Critical Trap: The apnea test assesses absence of spontaneous respirations while CO₂ rises. Do not provide ventilatory breaths during the observation period unless the test is aborted or the provider directs rescue ventilation.

Procedure Flow: RT Bedside Sequence

  1. Confirm provider order, team presence, facility protocol, and communication plan.
  2. Verify baseline vital signs, SpO₂, ventilator settings, and baseline ABG timing.
  3. Preoxygenate with 100% O₂ as directed and verify SpO₂ is stable.
  4. Prepare oxygenation method: insufflation catheter, T-piece/CPAP, or approved ventilator CPAP setup.
  5. Prepare rescue equipment: suction, BVM with O₂, and ventilator reconnection path.
  6. Begin test only when provider indicates readiness.
  7. Observe continuously for chest/abdominal movement, SpO₂, HR/rhythm, BP, and visible distress/instability.
  8. Obtain endpoint ABG or coordinate ABG at the ordered time/target.
  9. Reconnect to ventilator or provide rescue ventilation immediately if abort criteria occur.
  10. Document method, times, values, patient response, complications, and team communication.

Monitoring During the Test

Oxygenation
Watch SpO₂ continuously. Falling SpO₂ may require immediate termination depending on threshold and facility policy.
Hemodynamics
Track BP, HR, and rhythm. Hypotension or arrhythmia may make the test unsafe.
Respiratory Movement
Observe chest and abdominal motion. Any spontaneous respiratory effort must be communicated to the provider immediately.

ABG Collection and Result Evaluation

The endpoint ABG demonstrates whether PaCO₂ reached the required threshold or rose enough from baseline according to facility policy. The RT may draw the ABG, coordinate with lab/nursing, or assist depending on site policy.

Baseline Data
Record baseline PaCO₂, pH, PaO₂, FiO₂, ventilator settings, SpO₂, and time before the apnea test begins.
Endpoint Data
Record endpoint ABG time, PaCO₂, pH, PaO₂, oxygenation method, test duration, patient response, and whether the test was completed or aborted.

Documentation and Communication

  • Order/protocol followed and provider present or directing the procedure.
  • Baseline vital signs, SpO₂, ventilator settings, FiO₂, and ABG values.
  • Preoxygenation method and duration per protocol.
  • Oxygenation method used during the apnea test.
  • Start and stop times.
  • Observed spontaneous respiratory effort: present/absent as directed by provider documentation workflow.
  • Endpoint ABG values and time drawn.
  • Complications, abort criteria, rescue actions, and patient response.
  • Provider notification and handoff details.

Summary & NBRC Reminders

RT Role
Prepare, oxygenate, monitor, coordinate ABG collection, rescue if unstable, and document. The provider determines brain death.
Equipment Priority
Oxygen source, apnea test oxygenation setup, continuous monitoring, suction, BVM, and ventilator reconnection readiness.
Stop Criteria
Desaturation, hypotension, arrhythmia, spontaneous respiratory effort, equipment failure, or inability to safely oxygenate.
NBRC Must-Know: Do not continue an apnea test through instability. Support oxygenation without providing ventilatory breaths unless the test is aborted. Document setup, times, ABGs, patient response, and communication.
