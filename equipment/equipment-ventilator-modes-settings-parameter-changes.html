Ventilator Modes, Ordered Settings & Parameter Changes

This lesson is the bedside bridge between a ventilator order and safe equipment operation. You will practice identifying which settings are active in each mode, entering ordered parameters, checking measured values, monitoring patient response, and recognizing when a parameter change creates a new safety problem.

Learning Objectives

  1. Translate a ventilator order into the correct screen selections and verify that the active settings match the ordered support strategy.
  2. Differentiate active controls in A/C, SIMV, CPAP, PSV, volume control, and pressure control modes.
  3. Identify common order-entry and equipment-screen errors before connecting or changing support.
  4. Perform ordered parameter changes and monitor the patient’s response using measured values and bedside assessment.
  5. Recognize when a setting is inactive, unsafe, mismatched to the mode, or likely to create alarms or dyssynchrony.
  6. Document ventilator mode, ordered settings, measured values, patient response, alarms, and provider communication after changes.
Scope Reminder: This is an equipment/procedure lesson. Deep ventilator physiology, lung-protective strategy, and full waveform interpretation belong in the separate didactic and troubleshooting lessons.

Start With the Order — Then Verify the Screen

PROCEDURE FOUNDATION

Ventilator operation begins by comparing three things: the provider order, the screen selections, and the measured patient response. A correct order entered into the wrong mode or an inactive setting can create a dangerous mismatch.

1. Read the Order
Mode · FiO₂ · VT or pressure control · RR · PEEP · flow or I-time · pressure support if spontaneous breaths are allowed · alarm limits.
2. Enter and Confirm
Do not assume the screen label means the same thing across all ventilators. Confirm patient category, circuit type, humidification, mode, and active parameters.
3. Verify Measured Values
After connection or a change, compare set values with measured exhaled VT, minute ventilation, peak pressure, plateau if ordered, SpO₂, ETCO₂, RR, and patient comfort.
High-Risk Error: Entering a tidal volume into a mode where VT is not the controlled variable, or entering pressure support in a mode where the patient is not triggering spontaneous breaths. Students must learn what the mode actually controls.

Screen Check: Set Values vs Measured Values

Students should verify both set and measured values. The ventilator may be set correctly, but the patient may not be receiving what you expect because of a leak, patient effort, resistance, compliance change, disconnection, or alarm condition.

Expected Match
In volume control, set VT should generally match exhaled VT closely unless there is leak, circuit issue, or measurement problem. Pressure may vary.
Expected Variation
In pressure control or PSV, set pressure may be stable but exhaled VT varies. Always monitor exhaled VT and minute ventilation after pressure changes.

Initial Settings: Equipment Entry Workflow

ORDER-TO-SCREEN
  1. Select patient category if required: adult, pediatric, neonatal. This may affect default alarms, flow sensors, circuit type, and allowed ranges.
  2. Confirm patient height/IBW source when tidal volume is ordered based on predicted body weight.
  3. Select the ordered mode exactly as the facility ventilator labels it.
  4. Enter core support settings: FiO₂, PEEP, VT or pressure control, RR, flow or I-time, pressure support if ordered.
  5. Set safety alarms before leaving the bedside: high pressure, low pressure/disconnect, low exhaled VT, low minute ventilation, apnea, high RR, oxygen alarm.
  6. Connect and observe. Verify chest rise, breath sounds, exhaled VT, pressure trend, SpO₂, ETCO₂ if available, and patient-ventilator comfort.

Parameter Changes: What Changes What?

Parameter changes should be tied to the equipment variable being adjusted and the patient response being monitored. In class, students may learn the physiology behind each change. In the equipment lesson, they need the bedside workflow: change one thing, verify the screen, monitor response, document.

Monitoring After a Change

Oxygenation Response
SpO₂ trend · FiO₂ requirement · PEEP tolerance · skin color · work of breathing · ABG PaO₂ when ordered.
Ventilation Response
Exhaled VT · minute ventilation · total RR · ETCO₂ trend · PaCO₂ when ordered · patient synchrony.
Safety Response
PIP/plateau trend · BP/HR response · alarms · air trapping signs · new leak · comfort/anxiety · sedation level.
Document: time of change, previous setting, new setting, order or protocol source, pre/post SpO₂, RR, HR/BP if relevant, measured VT/VE/pressures, patient tolerance, and who was notified if response was abnormal.

Common Equipment-Side Problems After Mode or Setting Changes

  • Low exhaled VT after switching to pressure control: pressure target may be too low, compliance worsened, resistance increased, or leak present. Assess patient and circuit; notify provider if ordered target no longer achieves adequate ventilation.
  • High pressure alarm after increasing VT: volume is being delivered against higher resistance or lower compliance. Check for biting, secretions, kink, bronchospasm, or compliance issue.
  • Apnea alarm in CPAP/PSV: patient is not initiating breaths. Return to a mode with backup ventilation and call for help according to protocol.
  • Patient “air hungry” after setting change: inspiratory flow, trigger sensitivity, pressure support, rise time, or sedation/comfort may be contributing. Assess bedside before changing settings.
  • Low pressure / disconnect alarm: check circuit connections, cuff leak, inline suction cap, nebulizer adapter, humidifier chamber, and patient airway connection.
  • New air trapping after RR increase: expiratory time may be inadequate. Check flow-time waveform if available and assess for auto-PEEP risk.

Summary & Board-Prep Reminders

Volume Control
Set volume; monitor pressures. A high-pressure alarm after a VT increase requires patient/circuit assessment.
Pressure Control
Set pressure; monitor exhaled VT and VE. If compliance, resistance, or leak changes, ventilation changes.
PSV/CPAP
Patient must initiate breaths. Watch RR, VT, VE, comfort, and apnea backup status.
After Any Change
Verify screen, assess patient, check measured values, review alarms, document response, and escalate abnormal findings.
