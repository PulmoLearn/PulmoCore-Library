The Next Decision

Which Value Matches the pH?

First classify the pH. Then identify whether PaCO₂ (respiratory) or HCO₃⁻ (metabolic) is responsible for that direction.

PaCO₂ moves opposite pH.
High CO₂ → lower pH (respiratory acidosis). Low CO₂ → higher pH (respiratory alkalosis).
HCO₃⁻ moves with pH.
Low HCO₃⁻ → lower pH (metabolic acidosis). High HCO₃⁻ → higher pH (metabolic alkalosis).
Reference ranges: pH 7.35–7.45 · PaCO₂ 35–45 mm Hg · HCO₃⁻ 22–26 mEq/L.
Today’s focus: name the primary process. You will learn to classify compensation in Step 3.

Step 2 Takeaway

Match the pH to Its Primary Driver

Respiratory acidosis: low pH + high PaCO₂
Respiratory alkalosis: high pH + low PaCO₂
Metabolic acidosis: low pH + low HCO₃⁻
Metabolic alkalosis: high pH + high HCO₃⁻

Sometimes both PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻ are abnormal. That does not automatically mean there are two primary disorders. Step 3 will teach you to identify compensation with the pH +1 / pH +2 framework.
