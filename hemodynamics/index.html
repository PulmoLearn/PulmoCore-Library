Facilitator notes · suggested 40–50 minute live session
  1. 5 min: Start with direction of flow. Ask students to name the tricuspid, pulmonic, mitral, and aortic valves in sequence.
  2. 8 min: Select Conduction. Trace SA → AV delay → His → bundle branches → Purkinje, then find P and QRS.
  3. 12 min: Step through all seven phases; stop at both isovolumetric phases and ask why volume is unchanged.
  4. 8 min: Integrate ECG with mechanical timing and valve clicks (S1, S2). Emphasize the QRS precedes the rise in ventricular pressure.
  5. 8–12 min: Use the blood-drop tour and valve challenge, then ask prediction questions; use Next question for live polls. Optional: connect to the bedside pulse and ECG monitoring.

Teaching distinction: ECG is a surface recording of summed electrical activity; it does not directly demonstrate perfusion, valve opening, mechanical contraction, or a pulse.
