C1: Select the SBT method, duration, and oxygen condition.
Aligned competencies: 3.3.1, 3.3.2, 3.3.3, 3.3.4

An SBT tests spontaneous breathing with minimal or no assistance

Common approaches include a T-piece, CPAP, or low pressure support. Current guidance supports conducting an SBT with or without pressure support according to protocol. Low pressure support is commonly around 5-8 cm H2O; excessive assistance may hide the work the patient will face after extubation.

ElementCurrent Unit 3 approach
DurationUsually 30-120 minutes; longer is not automatically better.
Pressure assistanceLow pressure support or no pressure support according to protocol.
PEEP/CPAPLow level when used; enough to match the chosen protocol without recreating full support.
FiO2Maintain the pretrial requirement rather than increasing it to make the trial pass.

The test condition must remain interpretable

If pressure support or FiO2 is increased because the patient is failing, stabilize the patient—but recognize that the original SBT has ended or changed.

What you are expected to know

  • Define an SBT.
  • Select a protocol-appropriate low-support or unsupported method.
  • Use a usual duration of 30-120 minutes.
  • Maintain FiO2 rather than masking oxygenation intolerance.
C2: Monitor the SBT and recognize success or failure.
Aligned competencies: 3.3.5, 3.3.6, 3.3.7, 3.3.8

Monitor the whole response

DomainSigns of toleranceSigns of intolerance
Breathing patternStable rate and VT without escalating effortSustained tachypnea, falling VT, accessory use, paradox, diaphoresis.
OxygenationStable SpO2 on unchanged FiO2Clinically important desaturation or increasing requirement.
HemodynamicsStable heart rate, rhythm, and blood pressureMarked change, ischemia, dysrhythmia, hypotension or severe hypertension.
Neurologic/comfortAlert enough, comfortable, cooperativeAgitation, anxiety with distress, somnolence, declining mental status.
Gas exchangeAcceptable pH/PaCO2 for the patient when measuredWorsening acidemia or hypercapnia with clinical intolerance.

Failure criteria should represent sustained or clinically important change, not one brief fluctuation. A transient rise in rate after turning may be acceptable if it resolves and all other domains remain stable. Conversely, acceptable SpO2 does not prove success when the patient is developing rapid shallow breathing and fatigue.

What you are expected to know

  • Monitor respiratory, oxygenation, hemodynamic, neurologic, and comfort domains.
  • Differentiate a transient isolated change from sustained intolerance.
  • Stop an unsafe trial.
  • Recognize that success requires sustainable integrated performance.
C3: Determine why the SBT failed.
Aligned competencies: 3.3.9, 3.3.15

Failure is a diagnostic event

After restoring support, identify the dominant load rather than simply recording “failed SBT.” The cause determines what should be corrected before the next attempt.

Failure mechanismCluesNext evaluation
Excess respiratory loadBronchospasm, secretions, auto-PEEP, high resistance, poor complianceWaveforms, holds, breath sounds, airway patency, mechanics.
Cardiovascular loadHypertension, tachycardia, crackles, positive fluid balance, ischemiaFluid status, cardiac function, perfusion, ECG.
Muscle weakness/fatigueFalling VT, rapid shallow pattern, weak pressures, malnutritionStrength, electrolytes, nutrition, rest, rehabilitation.
Neurologic/sedationPoor drive, somnolence, agitation or deliriumMedication and neurologic assessment.
Gas-exchange burdenHypoxemia, rising dead space, worsening diseaseABG, imaging, oxygenation and ventilation mechanism.

Document a mechanism and a plan

State the failure pattern, the most likely cause, what will be corrected, and when readiness will be reassessed.

What you are expected to know

  • Classify SBT failure as respiratory, cardiac, neuromuscular, neurologic, or gas-exchange related.
  • Use waveforms and auto-PEEP when obstruction is suspected.
  • Identify fluid/cardiac load when crackles and hemodynamic stress emerge.
  • Create a targeted correction and reassessment plan.
C4: Support the patient after failure and repeat assessment appropriately.
Aligned competencies: 3.3.10, 3.3.11, 3.3.15

Rest without creating avoidable delay

After failure, restore a stable, comfortable, non-fatiguing level of ventilatory support. Correct reversible causes, minimize unnecessary sedation, and repeat readiness assessment and SBT according to protocol—generally at least daily once appropriate. Leaving the patient unsupported to build endurance can worsen fatigue.

After failurePurpose
Restore supportReverse distress and permit respiratory-muscle recovery.
Treat the mechanismReduce the load that caused failure.
Reassess responseConfirm the intervention improved the relevant physiology.
Repeat timely SBTAvoid prolonged ventilation after the barrier is corrected.
CommunicateAlign RT, nursing, medical, therapy, and airway plans.

What you are expected to know

  • Select non-fatiguing support after a failed trial.
  • Correct the identified mechanism before repeating.
  • Repeat readiness assessment/SBT without unnecessary multi-day delay.
  • Document tolerance, failure cause, intervention, and next planned assessment.
C5: Compare SBT-based liberation with gradual support reduction.
Aligned competencies: 3.3.12, 3.3.13, 3.3.14, 3.3.16

A timely SBT answers the central question directly

Pressure-support reduction may be useful as the patient improves, but the patient does not need to reach zero support before an SBT. Routine gradual SIMV-rate reduction is not preferred because it can prolong liberation. When readiness criteria are met, conduct the SBT rather than continuing small reductions solely out of habit.

StrategyAppropriate roleImportant distinction
Daily readiness screen + SBTPrimary protocolized assessment of liberation potentialDirectly evaluates spontaneous breathing.
Pressure-support reductionSelective transition or comfort strategyShould not delay an SBT once ready.
SIMV-rate reductionNot the routine preferred approachMay prolong liberation.
Successful SBTEvidence that ventilatory support may be stoppedDoes not prove airway patency/protection.

What you are expected to know

  • Explain why SBT-based assessment supports timely liberation.
  • Use pressure-support reduction selectively.
  • Avoid presenting SIMV-rate reduction as preferred.
  • Proceed from successful SBT to airway and extubation assessment.
End-of-reading check: You should be able to select and conduct an SBT, recognize success or failure, determine the mechanism of failure, and plan the next step without delaying liberation.
