How to use this guide

This guide identifies the knowledge and decisions students should be able to make after Lesson 1.3. Quiz alignments are shown as Q#. No Unit Competency Exam E# is assigned here because a current Unit 1 exam was not used to create this package.

Clinical sequence: Know the patient → calculate PBW → choose VT and rate → protect timing → select FiO₂ + PEEP → choose flow/Ti/trigger → apply settings → read PIP/VTE/total rate/V̇E → set alarms → reassess.

C5 · Establish Safe Initial Invasive Settings and Alarms

Calculate PBW from height and sex and use PBW rather than actual body weight to establish tidal-volume boundaries.
Assessment alignment: Q1 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Use 6-8 mL/kg PBW as a common initial VT range when appropriate and recognize 10 mL/kg PBW as a ceiling/check rather than a routine target.
Assessment alignment: Q1, Q4 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Estimate initial V̇E at about 10% of PBW in L/min and pair VT with rate rather than selecting either in isolation.
Assessment alignment: Q2 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Decode AC-VC, AC-PC, SIMV-VC, and SIMV-PC by separating breath organization from the controlled variable.
Assessment alignment: Q7 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Recognize that pressure-control pressure is selected and delivered VTE varies with mechanics and timing.
Assessment alignment: Q6 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Use a typical VC inspiratory flow around 50-60 L/min as a reasonable starting point when mechanics are not obstructive.
Assessment alignment: Q8 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Distinguish set VT from VTE, set rate from total rate, selected pressure from PIP, and calculated V̇E from measured V̇E.
Assessment alignment: Q6, Q10 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Set practical initial alarm limits from monitored values: high pressure about PIP +10, low pressure 5-10 below PIP, and low VTE about 10% below expected/measured VTE.
Assessment alignment: Q10 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms
Verify patient, airway, circuit, humidification, oxygen source, monitored parameters, and active alarms during the first minutes after initiation.
Assessment alignment: Q10 · 2027 RTE: invasive ventilation / patient data / alarms

C6 · Adapt Settings to Disease and Presentation

Match the initial plan to the dominant physiology: normal lungs, pump failure, obstruction, severe asthma, low compliance/ARDS, severe hypoxemia, or post-arrest state.
Assessment alignment: Q3, Q4, Q5, Q9, Q12 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
For COPD, protect expiratory time with a lower rate and shorter Ti; a larger safe VT may be preferable to a high rate when V̇E must be maintained.
Assessment alignment: Q2, Q3 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
For COPD or severe asthma in VC, consider higher inspiratory flow (often 60-100 L/min) to shorten Ti and extend Te.
Assessment alignment: Q3, Q8 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
For ARDS, use lower lung-protective VT near 6 mL/kg PBW and support oxygenation with FiO₂ plus PEEP; do not force VT upward to satisfy a V̇E shortcut.
Assessment alignment: Q4 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
For neuromuscular pump failure with normal oxygenation, provide adequate PBW-based ventilation without unnecessary FiO₂ escalation.
Assessment alignment: Q5 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
Select FiO₂ according to actual oxygen need; a stable normal-lung patient may begin near 0.21, while severe hypoxemia may require substantially more.
Assessment alignment: Q9 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
After ROSC, use FiO₂ 1.0 until oxygenation can be measured reliably, then titrate to avoid unnecessary hyperoxia.
Assessment alignment: Q12 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
Use CPAP when oxygenation/recruitment is the primary noninvasive need and spontaneous ventilation/airway protection remain adequate.
Assessment alignment: Q11 · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
Increase bilevel inspiratory assistance by increasing IPAP while holding EPAP constant when ventilation is inadequate and NIV remains safe.
Assessment alignment: Lesson practice · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
Escalate from NIV when airway protection, vomiting, secretions, cooperation, hemodynamics, or clinical deterioration make noninvasive support unsafe.
Assessment alignment: Lesson practice · 2027 RTE: support oxygenation/ventilation / decision making
