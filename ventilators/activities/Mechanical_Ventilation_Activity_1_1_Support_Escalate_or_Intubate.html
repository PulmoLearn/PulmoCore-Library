Your repeatable clinical routine

LOOK → IDENTIFY → MATCH → REASSESS → ACT

LOOKStable, working harder, tiring, or immediately unsafe?
IDENTIFYOxygenation, ventilation, respiratory pump, airway protection, or more than one?
MATCHChoose support that addresses the physiology and is safe for the patient.
REASSESSPatient, work of breathing, mental status, gas exchange, hemodynamics, and device response.
ACTContinue, modify, change modality, or escalate.
Patient first. A normal oxygen saturation does not prove ventilation is adequate. A backup rate does not protect an unsafe airway. A lower respiratory rate may represent fatigue rather than improvement.
Quick Reference & Acronym Refresher — open anytime
PaO₂Partial pressure of arterial oxygen. Helps assess arterial oxygenation.
PaCO₂Partial pressure of arterial carbon dioxide. Helps assess ventilation.
FiO₂Fraction of inspired oxygen. The oxygen concentration being delivered.
A–a gradientAlveolar–arterial oxygen gradient. A widened gradient suggests impaired transfer from alveoli to arterial blood.
CPAPContinuous positive airway pressure. One continuous pressure level during spontaneous breathing.
NIVNoninvasive ventilation. Positive-pressure support without an endotracheal tube or tracheostomy.
IPAPInspiratory positive airway pressure. The higher bilevel pressure during inspiration.
EPAPExpiratory positive airway pressure. The lower pressure maintained during expiration.
S/T modeSpontaneous/Timed mode. Patient-triggered support with timed backup breaths if triggering becomes inadequate.
VCVital capacity. A bedside measure of the largest volume exhaled after a maximal inspiration.
MIP / NIFMaximal inspiratory pressure / negative inspiratory force. Measures inspiratory muscle strength.
Pressure supportIPAP − EPAPThe pressure difference that provides inspiratory assistance during bilevel NIV.
