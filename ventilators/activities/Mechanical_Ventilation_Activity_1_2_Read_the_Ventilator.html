Your ventilator-reading routine

PATIENT → TRIGGER → CONTROL → MECHANICS → TIMING → GRAPHICS

PATIENTWhat is the patient doing at the bedside?
TRIGGERDid the patient or machine start the breath?
CONTROLIs volume/flow or pressure the primary inspiratory target?
MECHANICSDid compliance or resistance change the cost of the breath?
TIMING + GRAPHICSDid inspiration and expiration happen when they should, and do the waveforms match the patient?
Patient first. A waveform is evidence, not the patient. Use ventilator data to explain what you see at the bedside rather than treating the screen as the diagnosis.
Quick Reference & Formula Refresher — open anytime
PIPPeak inspiratory pressure. Reflects pressure needed to overcome resistive and elastic loads during a flow-delivered breath.
PplatPlateau pressure. Measured during an inspiratory hold with flow stopped; approximates static respiratory-system pressure in a passive patient.
PEEPPositive end-expiratory pressure. Baseline pressure at the end of expiration.
Static complianceCstat = exhaled VT ÷ (Pplat − PEEP)Lower compliance means a stiffer respiratory system.
Airway resistanceRaw ≈ (PIP − Pplat) ÷ flow in L/sA larger PIP-to-Pplat difference suggests greater resistive pressure when flow is comparable.
Time constantτ = resistance × complianceDescribes how quickly a lung unit fills and empties.
Total cycle timeTCT = 60 ÷ respiratory rateInspiratory time and expiratory time must fit within each breath cycle.
TriggerWhat starts inspiration: patient effort or machine timing.
CycleWhat ends inspiration and allows expiration to begin.
