Part 1 · Direct practice

Choose the Best Complete Setup

Each case gives you complete sample ventilator settings. Choose the best overall starting set. For invasive cases, do not choose an answer just because it says AC or SIMV; compare the resulting VT, rate, timing, PEEP, and FiO₂. Mode should become the deciding factor only when the patient actually needs a different support type, such as CPAP versus invasive ventilation.

Use the same reasoning every time: PBW → VT + minute ventilation → disease-specific rate/timing → FiO₂ + PEEP → flow/trigger → alarms → reassess.
Mode reminder: AC and SIMV tell you how mandatory breaths are organized. VC and PC tell you what controls the mandatory breath. In pressure-control options, ΔP is written above PEEP and the resulting VT is shown in parentheses. Newly intubated, sedated simulator patients use invasive full-support modes; pressure support is saved for later spontaneous-breathing/liberation work.

Part 3 · Best-answer discrimination

Close Calls: Which Setup Is Best?

These five cases are intentionally harder. More than one option may look reasonable or fall near an acceptable range, but one set better fits the patient's physiology, safety priorities, and RTE-style decision making.

Do not stop at “could this work?” Compare the remaining options for the safest VT, most appropriate rate/timing, oxygenation strategy, and disease-specific priority.
