1 · Activate prior knowledge

Bring First-Semester Knowledge Forward

You have already learned how to assess breathing, interpret blood gases, provide oxygen, maintain an airway, and describe oxygen transport. Mechanical ventilation adds a new question: what level of support does this patient need?

Pulmonary assessmentRespiratory rate and pattern, accessory-muscle use, mental status, cough, secretions, breath sounds, and trend.
Respiratory physiologyOxygenation, alveolar ventilation, V/Q relationships, shunt, diffusion, respiratory drive, and muscle function.
Foundations of careOxygen devices, positioning, suctioning, airway adjuncts, bag-mask ventilation, and patient safety.
New decisionChoose oxygenation support, ventilatory assistance, an immediate bridge, or a protected invasive airway.

2 · Classify the problem

Name What Is Failing Before Choosing a Device

Oxygenation failurePaO₂ or SpO₂ is inadequate for the delivered FiO₂. Ventilation may still be adequate. Think V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion impairment, or insufficient inspired oxygen.
Ventilatory failureEffective alveolar ventilation is inadequate. PaCO₂ rises and acute respiratory acidosis may develop. Look for shallow breathing, fatigue, reduced drive, obstruction, or muscle weakness.
Respiratory-pump failureThe drive, nerves, muscles, or chest wall cannot sustain ventilation. VC and inspiratory strength may deteriorate before SpO₂ or ABG values become dramatic.
Airway-protection failureThe patient cannot protect against aspiration or clear secretions. An acceptable PaO₂ or PaCO₂ does not make an unsafe airway safe.
Clinical distinction: respiratory distress describes increased effort; respiratory failure means the system cannot maintain adequate gas exchange, ventilation, or airway safety. A distressed patient may still compensate—until the work becomes unsustainable.
Do not interpret a quieter patient automatically as improvement. A falling respiratory rate accompanied by shallower breaths, reduced alertness, or weaker effort can represent fatigue.
3 · Apply oxygen physiology

Use the Oxygenation Tool That Answers the Question

These calculations are not interchangeable. Choose the one that clarifies the clinical problem.

ToolQuestion it helps answerInterpretation
A–a gradientIs arterial oxygen lower than expected for the calculated alveolar oxygen?A widened gradient supports impaired transfer from alveoli to arterial blood.
P/F ratioHow severe is hypoxemia relative to FiO₂?A falling ratio indicates worsening oxygenation even if PaO₂ appears unchanged.
CaO₂How much oxygen is carried in arterial blood?Hemoglobin-bound oxygen dominates content; severe anemia can lower content despite a reassuring saturation.
DO₂How much oxygen is delivered to tissues each minute?Delivery depends on both arterial content and cardiac output.
P/F = PaO₂ ÷ FiO₂
CaO₂ = (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)
DO₂ = CaO₂ × cardiac output × 10

Enter FiO₂ as a decimal. SaO₂ is also entered as a decimal in the CaO₂ equation. Use the convention specified by your program when rounding.

5 · Decide whether NIV is safe

An NIV Indication Does Not Automatically Make the Patient a Candidate

Features supporting a trialSpontaneous breathing, cooperation, an interface that can seal, sufficient airway protection, manageable secretions, relative hemodynamic stability, and a reversible problem that can be reassessed closely.
Features limiting a trialApnea, active vomiting, inability to protect the airway, severe secretion burden, unsuitable facial anatomy or trauma, inability to cooperate, severe instability, or immediate need for intubation.

Altered mental status requires interpretation. Hypercapnic somnolence may improve with effective ventilation in a closely monitored patient who can still protect the airway. Progressive loss of airway control is different.

6 · Provide an immediate bridge

Bag-Mask Ventilation Must Produce Effective Breaths

Use bag-mask ventilation when the patient needs assisted breaths immediately—during apnea, respiratory arrest with a pulse, severe inadequate ventilation, or preparation for definitive airway management.

Open the airwayPosition the head when appropriate and use an oropharyngeal or nasopharyngeal airway when indicated.
Create a sealIf one rescuer cannot maintain a seal and jaw lift, use a two-person technique with a two-handed mask hold.
Confirm ventilationLook for visible chest rise, appropriate breath delivery, improving clinical response, and available capnography.
More force is not the first answer. Poor chest rise can result from airway obstruction, poor position, mask leak, secretions, or high resistance. Excessive pressure increases gastric inflation and aspiration risk.

7 · Follow the response

Starting Support Creates the Next Assessment

Define what should improve before initiating support. Then compare the patient’s actual response with that prediction.

If ventilation improvesWork of breathing and respiratory rate should become more sustainable, exhaled volume may improve, PaCO₂ should trend toward the patient-specific goal, and acute acidemia should improve.
If oxygenation improvesSpO₂ and PaO₂ should improve at the same or lower FiO₂. A P/F trend can reveal improvement that an isolated PaO₂ cannot.
If the strategy failsExpect worsening effort, gas exchange, acidosis, mental status, hemodynamics, secretion management, or interface tolerance.
If the airway failsLoss of airway protection or clearance can require invasive support even when oxygenation or PaCO₂ appears better.

8 · Carry-forward framework

Use the Same Reasoning Sequence Every Time

1 · RecognizeWhat changed in assessment, gas exchange, respiratory mechanics, airway safety, or perfusion?
2 · ClassifyIs the problem oxygenation, ventilation, respiratory pump, airway protection, oxygen content, delivery, or a combination?
3 · CalculateWould A–a gradient, P/F, CaO₂, or DO₂ clarify the problem?
4 · InterveneWhich support directly addresses the identified problem and remains safe?
5 · PredictWhat should improve if the strategy works?
6 · ReassessContinue, modify, or escalate based on the response and emerging risks.
Carry this framework forward: connect each assessment finding to the physiologic problem, choose support that addresses that problem, predict the response, and reassess the trend.
