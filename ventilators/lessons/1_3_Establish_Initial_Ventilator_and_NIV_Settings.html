What You Will Be Expected to Do

This lesson prepares you to establish an initial plan—not to memorize one universal group of settings. You will calculate the breath size and rate, select the settings that primarily support ventilation and oxygenation, adapt timing to mechanics, and establish immediate safety limits.

RTE reasoning: calculations supply the starting numbers. Clinical decision making asks whether those numbers fit the mode, disease, gas-exchange problem, timing needs, and patient safety.
1 · Start with patient size

Distinguish Predicted Body Weight from Ideal Body Weight

Adult ventilator tidal volume is based on predicted body weight (PBW) because PBW is used as a proxy for lung size. Ideal body weight (IBW) is a height- and sex-based estimate of ideal body mass used for broader clinical purposes. The terms are sometimes used loosely in older material, and their values may be close, but they describe different clinical ideas.

TermWhat it estimatesHow to use it here
PBWA height- and sex-based lung-size proxyUse the exact PBW formula for PBW-based VT selection
IBWA height- and sex-based estimate of ideal body massUse when the question specifically asks for IBW or an IBW estimate
Actual body weightThe patient's measured massDo not substitute it for PBW when selecting adult lung-protective VT
Exact PBW formulas
Male PBW = 50 + 2.3 × (inches over 60)
Female PBW = 45.5 + 2.3 × (inches over 60)
NBRC RTE test tip · Quick IBW estimate
Start with 50 kg + 2 kg for every inch over 60 inches. Then add 2 kg for a male or subtract 2 kg for a female.
Quick IBW = 50 + 2(inches − 60) + 2 for males or − 2 for females.
This is an estimation shortcut, not a replacement for the exact PBW formula when the stem asks for PBW.
Compare the results: for a 5 ft 9 in male, exact PBW is 50 + 2.3(9) = 70.7 kg. The quick IBW estimate is 50 + 2(9) + 2 = 70 kg. The values are close, but the label and calculation requested by the question still matter.
Picture the patient

Actual weight can change while the lung-size estimate does not

Patient AA 5 ft 5 in adult female weighs 62 kg. Her PBW is 45.5 + 2.3(5) = 57 kg.
Patient BA 5 ft 5 in adult female weighs 140 kg. Her PBW is still 57 kg because the formula uses the same height and sex.

2 · Size the breath

Multiply PBW by the Selected mL/kg Target

Target VT (mL) = PBW (kg) × target mL/kg
Conventional protective rangeUse the prescribed target within approximately 6–8 mL/kg PBW when appropriate.
ARDS or vulnerable lungsBegin near 6 mL/kg PBW and reassess pressures, gas exchange, and delivered volume.
Usable settingRound the calculated result to a setting the ventilator can deliver without drifting outside the intended range.
Picture the patient

One PBW can create a target, a range, or a lung-protective starting point

For PBW 66 kg, 6 mL/kg is 396 mL and 8 mL/kg is 528 mL. A usable 6 mL/kg setting might be 400 mL. In ARDS, start near the lower target instead of selecting 8–10 mL/kg to correct carbon dioxide.

Do not use actual weight in obesity. A 150 kg patient with PBW 66 kg does not receive 900–1,200 mL simply because actual weight is large.

3 · Build the ventilation plan

Connect Tidal Volume, Total Rate, and Minute Ventilation

V̇E (L/min) = exhaled VT (L) × total RR (/min)
Initial RR = desired V̇E ÷ selected VT (L)
Unit check: convert milliliters to liters before multiplying or dividing. A VT of 430 mL is 0.430 L. Use total respiratory rate when calculating measured minute ventilation because patient-triggered breaths also contribute.
Picture the patient

The calculation creates a candidate rate—not permission to ignore timing

If desired V̇E is 8.6 L/min and selected VT is 0.430 L, the calculated rate is 20/min. That may be reasonable for restrictive disease, but a patient with severe obstruction may not have enough time to exhale at that rate. Preserve the safe VT, then evaluate whether the calculated rate fits the mechanics.

Carry forward: desired VT, desired V̇E, and calculated RR should agree mathematically. The disease process decides whether the combination is physiologically tolerable.

Picture the patient

Severe pneumonia requires more than a tidal-volume number

A patient with PBW 68 kg, severe hypoxemia, and full-support needs might begin with assist-control volume control, VT near 410 mL, an appropriate calculated rate, high initial FiO₂, PEEP for recruitment, flow that meets demand, a usable trigger, and patient-specific alarms. Each setting has a distinct job.

Initial FiO₂ is severity dependent. High FiO₂ can be appropriate during unstable or severely hypoxemic initiation, but it should be reduced promptly when reliable oxygenation data permit. A patient with normal oxygenation does not automatically require FiO₂ 1.00.

5 · Build a pressure-control plan

Select Pressure and Time, Then Measure the Resulting VT

TargetedInspiratory pressure is applied for the selected inspiratory time.
ResultingFlow and VT change with compliance, resistance, effort, and timing.
Required follow-upCompare exhaled VT with the PBW-based target and titrate cautiously.
Picture the patient

The ventilator cannot promise a volume from a pressure alone

For PBW 60 kg, the 6–8 mL/kg range is 360–480 mL. The clinician selects a moderate pressure above PEEP and an appropriate inspiratory time. If exhaled VT is 320 mL, pressure or time may need cautious adjustment; if VT is 560 mL, the delivered breath is excessive even if the displayed pressure seems acceptable.

Confirm the display convention. Some ventilators display inspiratory pressure above PEEP; others emphasize total inspiratory pressure. The pressure difference available for inflation must be understood before comparing settings.

6 · Let physiology constrain the plan

Restrictive and Obstructive Disease Need Different Timing Priorities

Compare two patients

The same calculated VT does not create the same complete plan

ARDS / restrictive hypoxemiaUse VT near 6 mL/kg PBW, an adequate rate to support V̇E, and an FiO₂–PEEP strategy appropriate for severe oxygenation failure. Monitor pressures and hemodynamics.
COPD or severe asthmaUse protective VT, a cautious rate, higher inspiratory flow when using volume control, and prolonged expiration. Avoid chasing a normal PaCO₂ by creating air trapping.
At a fixed RR: higher VC flow → shorter inspiratory time → more time available for expiration
Bring it forward: respiratory rate fixes total cycle time. Delivering the selected volume faster shortens inspiratory time; the remaining cycle time becomes available for exhalation. Flow and I:E are linked, not independent decorations on the settings screen.
Do not interpret this as “high flow fixes obstruction.” Higher flow can protect expiratory time, but obstruction still requires bronchodilation, secretion management, monitoring for incomplete exhalation, and reassessment.

8 · Establish immediate safety

Use Both Calculated Alarm Rules and Practical Guardrails

Alarm questions can be framed two ways. Some ask you to calculate limits from the patient's stable values. Others use common adult guardrail defaults intended to catch large changes without constant nuisance alarms. At the bedside, the device, mode, facility policy, and patient determine which limits are appropriate.

AlarmCourse / RTE calculationCommon adult guardrailClinical distinction

Picture the bedside

Recognize what the question is asking

For PIP 24 cm H₂O, VT 500 mL, V̇E 8.0 L/min, and total RR 18/min, patient-specific calculation rules produce high pressure near 34; VT near 425–450 and 550–575 mL; V̇E near 6.8–7.2 and 8.8–9.2 L/min; and RR near 8 and 28/min. A common broad adult setup might instead use high pressure 40, VT 200–800 mL, low V̇E 3 L/min, and RR 8–30/min. Both patterns exist; use the wording and clinical purpose to decide which approach is being tested.

Current-practice distinction: the 2024 AARC patient–ventilator assessment guideline supports systematic assessment but does not prescribe one universal numeric alarm formula. Use manufacturer instructions and facility policy, then individualize alarms to values that would trigger meaningful evaluation or action for that patient.
Alarm settings are not treatment settings. Raising a limit does not correct obstruction, worsening compliance, coughing, biting, water in the circuit, or a kinked tube. It only changes when the device warns you.

Carry-forward framework

Build Every Initial Plan in the Same Order

1 · SizeCalculate PBW and select a protective VT.
2 · VentilateChoose desired V̇E, solve for RR, and check timing.
3 · Oxygenate and deliverSelect FiO₂, PEEP/EPAP, flow or Ti, and trigger.
4 · ProtectSet alarms, position safely, and prepare to reassess actual response.
Relatable clinical sequence

The numbers become a plan only after they agree with the patient

A calculated VT may be safe, but the paired rate may be too fast for obstruction. A pressure-control setting may look reasonable, but measured VT may be outside the PBW target. Bilevel pressures may both rise, but the pressure-support difference may stay unchanged. Finish the calculation, then ask what each setting actually does.

Core reasoning: the math creates the starting plan; disease mechanics constrain it; monitored delivery and patient response determine whether it should remain.
