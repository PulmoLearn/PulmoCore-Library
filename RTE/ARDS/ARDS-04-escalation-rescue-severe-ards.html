Escalation & Rescue in Severe ARDS

Severe acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) can remain dangerous after lung-protective ventilation and prolonged prone positioning. Recheck equipment and reversible causes, then report the severity and response. An inhaled pulmonary vasodilator may briefly improve oxygenation in a rescue situation. Selected patients with persistent severe failure should be considered early for venovenous extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VV-ECMO) evaluation. The arterial oxygen tension (PaO₂) divided by inspired oxygen fraction (FiO₂) helps describe severity.

What should change when support fails?

Reassess and recommend change

Verify → optimize → bridge → refer

Confirm true refractory failure

Check the endotracheal tube, circuit, secretions, pneumothorax, fluid status, and hemodynamics. Document arterial oxygen tension (PaO₂), fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO₂), their ratio, acid-base status, and the trend on current support.

Verify optimized standard care

Confirm a protective tidal volume and plateau pressure, appropriate positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), and an adequate prolonged prone trial when feasible. Treat the underlying cause. Do not correct severe hypoxemia by giving unsafe large breaths.

Understand bridge therapies

Inhaled nitric oxide or an inhaled prostacyclin may temporarily improve oxygenation in selected rescue situations. A better oxygen number alone does not show a survival benefit or remove the need for escalation; reassess response and adverse effects.

Request VV-ECMO evaluation

VV-ECMO can support gas exchange outside the body while protective ventilation continues. Current guidance suggests it for selected severe ARDS after optimized care, especially with a very low PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio or severe refractory acidosis. Early consultation lets an experienced center assess reversibility, timing, and risks.

Core rule: persistent life-threatening hypoxemia despite protective ventilation and proning calls for urgent multidisciplinary review and early rescue-center discussion. A transient response to an inhaled drug is a bridge, not a complete plan.
Referral cue: a PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio below about 80 despite optimized care is one severe-failure threshold used when considering VV-ECMO evaluation. It is a consultation cue, not an automatic order to place cannulas.
Severe ARDS rescue decisions

Review each concept

Repeat the arterial gas and check timing, FiO₂, oximeter signal, and ventilator settings. A changed ratio has meaning only alongside the support being delivered and the patient’s examination.
An obstructed tube, mucus plug, pneumothorax, new edema, or circuit failure can worsen oxygenation. Identify and address correctable problems before labeling hypoxemia refractory.
Maintain low tidal volume by predicted body weight and the plateau-pressure limit while planning rescue. Large breaths may worsen ventilator-related lung injury.
Review whether a prolonged prone session was given safely and whether oxygenation improved or declined. A brief turn is not equivalent to a full evidence-based prone treatment.
PEEP helps maintain open air spaces but can affect blood pressure. Evaluate its oxygenation benefit, pressure effects, circulation, and protocol fit rather than escalating it blindly.
Inhaled nitric oxide or prostacyclin can sometimes improve ventilation-to-blood-flow matching and oxygenation temporarily. Monitor response and adverse effects; routine use has not shown a survival benefit in ARDS.
VV-ECMO moves blood through an external gas-exchange circuit and returns it to the veins. It supports oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange without directly providing circulatory pump support.
Very severe hypoxemia or refractory severe hypercapnic acidosis despite optimal management may prompt VV-ECMO consultation. A potentially reversible cause, early course, and the overall risk profile matter.
A center capable of VV-ECMO should be involved early when severe failure persists, especially if transfer may be needed. Share the trend, current support, prone response, and known contraindications.
After any adjustment, check PaO₂/FiO₂, pH, PaCO₂, pressures, blood pressure, and complications. Recommend the next change from response, not from the name of a rescue therapy.
Integrated RTE-style rescue case

Transient improvement, persistent severe failure

An adult with early, potentially reversible severe ARDS has persistent hypoxemia:
  • Tube and circuit are patent; no new pneumothorax or correctable plug is found
  • Protective ventilation, PEEP review, and a prolonged prone session are documented
  • PaO₂ is 54 mm Hg on FiO₂ 0.90, a ratio of 60
  • A short inhaled-vasodilator trial briefly improves PaO₂, then it falls again
