Recognizing Restrictive & Mixed Patterns

Spirometry can suggest restriction, but it cannot confirm it because spirometry does not measure total lung capacity (TLC). A preserved or increased FEV₁/FVC ratio with a reduced FVC raises concern for restriction; a reduced TLC confirms the restrictive component. When obstruction is also present and TLC is reduced, the pattern is mixed.

Socratic pattern string

Suspected, confirmed, mixed—or something else?

Interpretation framework

Do not call restriction until TLC proves it

Possible restriction on spirometry

Reduced FVC with a preserved or increased FEV₁/FVC ratio suggests a restrictive pattern, but low FVC may also reflect poor technique, early termination, air trapping, or another process.

Confirmed restriction

Your PulmoLearn sources explicitly state that restriction is confirmed by a reduced TLC. Spirometry alone cannot directly measure TLC or residual volume.

Mixed pattern

A reduced FEV₁/FVC ratio identifies obstruction. If TLC is also reduced, the data support both obstructive and restrictive components.

Obstruction + low FVC + normal TLC

This does not confirm restriction. Your source says a low FVC in an obstructed patient with nonreduced TLC may reflect air trapping instead.

The word “possible” matters.

Preserved ratio + low FVC = possible restriction. Reduced TLC = confirmed restrictive component. Low ratio + reduced TLC = mixed physiology.

Restrictive & mixed-pattern decisions

Review each topic

Early termination, submaximal inspiration, poor effort, leak, or another invalid maneuver can falsely lower FVC and create the appearance of restriction. Correct poor technique before classifying the pattern.
A preserved or increased FEV₁/FVC ratio with reduced FVC is the classic spirometric clue for possible restriction. It is not confirmation because spirometry cannot measure TLC.
Your current PulmoLearn teaching states directly: true restriction is confirmed by low total lung capacity. PFT-04 will go deeper into TLC, RV, FRC, RV/TLC, and measurement methods.
A reduced FEV₁/FVC ratio establishes an obstructive component. If TLC is also reduced, a restrictive component is present too. Together, that supports a mixed obstructive-restrictive pattern.
A reduced ratio with low FVC is indeterminate from spirometry alone. Your source explicitly says this can be mixed disease or severe obstruction with air trapping. Lung-volume testing is needed to separate them.
This RTE review evaluating chart/history/laboratory/imaging/monitoring data before acting. Interpret the PFT pattern alongside symptoms, smoking/exposure history, prior diagnoses, imaging, neuromuscular or chest-wall conditions, and prior studies rather than assigning a disease from one number.
Your PulmoLearn sources recommend lung-volume testing when restriction is suspected or when reduced FVC with obstruction raises concern for air trapping versus mixed disease.
A restrictive physiologic pattern does not by itself identify the cause. It may occur with intrinsic parenchymal disease, obesity/chest-wall limitation, neuromuscular weakness, or other processes. The supplied lesson set emphasizes physiologic classification first, with disease correlation afterward.
