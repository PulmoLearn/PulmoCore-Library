Lung Volumes, Air Trapping & Hyperinflation

Lung-volume testing answers questions that spirometry cannot. Use TLC to confirm restriction, RV and RV/TLC to identify trapped gas, FRC and TLC to recognize hyperinflation, and the measurement method to understand why different techniques may report different volumes in severe obstruction.

Socratic lung-volume string

What does the volume pattern actually mean?

Interpretation framework

Use the right variable for the right question

TLC: “Is total lung size reduced?”

Your source defines TLC as all gas in the lungs after maximal inspiration. Reduced TLC is the defining lung-volume abnormality supporting restriction.

RV: “How much remains after maximal exhalation?”

RV cannot be measured by simple spirometry. Increased RV supports air trapping because more gas remains after expiration.

FRC: “Where does the respiratory system rest?”

FRC = ERV + RV and is the resting equilibrium volume after a normal exhalation. Static hyperinflation raises this resting operating volume.

RV/TLC: “How much of total capacity is trapped at end expiration?”

Increased RV/TLC supports a larger trapped-gas fraction even when TLC itself is not reduced.

Do not diagnose restriction from low FVC alone.

Your source repeatedly emphasizes that low FVC can reflect restriction, severe air trapping, poor effort, or another process. TLC is what confirms true restrictive volume loss.

Lung-volume patterns & methods

Review each topic

TLC = VC + RV. Your source states that TLC is reduced in restriction and may be increased in hyperinflation. A low TLC confirms a restrictive component; a high TLC can support static hyperinflation when the rest of the pattern fits.
Residual volume is the gas that remains after maximal exhalation. Obstruction with incomplete emptying commonly raises RV. Because RV cannot be exhaled, simple spirometry cannot directly measure it.
FRC = ERV + RV. It is the volume at which inward lung recoil balances outward chest-wall recoil and also serves as an oxygen reservoir between breaths. Your source says static hyperinflation commonly raises FRC.
The source pattern for obstruction with air trapping is RV ↑ and RV/TLC ↑, with FRC potentially increased and VC potentially reduced. This helps explain why a low VC/FVC does not always mean restriction.
Static hyperinflation means the resting lung volume is chronically elevated. Your source describes increased FRC and often increased TLC at rest, caused by loss of recoil and chronic air trapping shifting the operating volume upward.
Dynamic hyperinflation occurs when the next breath begins before exhalation is complete. End-expiratory lung volume progressively rises, creating intrinsic PEEP. This is a dynamic breathing-time problem and is distinct from chronic static hyperinflation.
Body plethysmography measures thoracic gas volume, including trapped gas. Your source notes that it is often preferred when severe obstruction is suspected and may report a larger lung volume than gas-dilution techniques because poorly communicating gas is still present in the thorax.
Helium dilution estimates communicating lung volume and may underestimate trapped or poorly ventilated gas that does not mix well with helium. Nitrogen washout also estimates communicating volume and may underestimate poorly ventilated or noncommunicating regions.
When plethysmography reports a larger volume than helium dilution or nitrogen washout in severe obstruction, your source says the difference suggests gas that is present in the thorax but communicates poorly with the mouth.
Obstruction with air trapping: RV ↑, RV/TLC ↑, FRC may ↑, VC may fall. Static hyperinflation: FRC and often TLC ↑. Restriction: TLC ↓, VC usually ↓, RV may be normal or ↓. Mixed pattern: obstructive airflow plus confirmed TLC reduction.
Integrated RTE-style lung-volume case

Use the method and pattern together

Patient with severe obstructive physiology:
  • FEV₁/FVC is reduced
  • FVC is mildly reduced
  • RV and RV/TLC are increased
  • Plethysmography reports a larger TLC than helium dilution
  • TLC by plethysmography is not reduced
