PFT Equipment, Calibration & Quality Control

A perfect patient maneuver is still unusable if the measurement system is wrong. Before testing, verify the spirometer or pneumotach setup, calibration/verification status, filters and connections, infection-control readiness, and quality-control status. If QC fails, correct the equipment problem before patient data are reported.

Patient problem—or measurement-system problem?

QC framework

Verify the device before trusting the patient result

1. Confirm device readiness.

Follow manufacturer and facility procedures for setup, warm-up, environmental entry, calibration verification, maintenance, cleaning, and infection-control components.

2. Verify volume accuracy.

Your PulmoLearn source uses a precision 3-L syringe to verify spirometer volume measurement before patient testing and after maintenance or transport.

3. Challenge the system at different flows.

The source teaches slow, medium, and fast syringe strokes because a device can behave differently across the flow range encountered during patient testing.

4. Stop if QC fails.

Do not “fix” failed QC by repeating the patient. Check tubing, syringe seal, filter, sensor, and connections; repeat verification according to policy; remove from service if the problem persists.

Valid PFT data require both good patient technique and reliable equipment.

Your student notes explicitly state that a quality-control failure can invalidate otherwise well-performed testing.

Source boundary: one older PulmoLearn lesson gives a specific ±3.5% syringe tolerance (2.895–3.105 L). Because the current AHRC notes emphasize manufacturer/facility verification requirements rather than one universal number, this lesson teaches that source-specific numeric range as a legacy/local example—not as a universal rule for every device.
Equipment & quality-control decisions

Review each topic

Spirometry systems measure volume and/or flow. Pneumotach-based systems infer flow from a pressure difference across a known resistance and integrate flow to determine volume. The registry specifically requires spirometer/pneumotach operation to be reviewed at the level needed to recognize setup and sensor problems.
Your PulmoLearn equipment lesson uses a precision 3-L syringe to verify volume accuracy before patient testing. Attach it firmly with no patient mouthpiece, inject the full syringe volume, and use the device/manufacturer verification procedure. The older PulmoLearn lesson teaches checks at slow, medium, and fast flows.
The older PulmoLearn equipment lesson states a ±3.5% acceptance range for a 3-L syringe, corresponding to 2.895–3.105 L. Treat that as source-specific teaching. For actual equipment, follow the current manufacturer and laboratory policy for the device in use.
If verification fails, first inspect the measurement pathway: syringe connection, tubing, adapters, filter, sensor/pneumotach connection, and evidence of leaks. A patient repeat cannot correct a leaking or out-of-tolerance system.
Your PulmoLearn source places a bacterial/viral filter between patient and spirometer to reduce cross-contamination. Replace patient-specific disposable components as required. A clogged or wet filter can increase resistance and falsely lower measured flows.
Portable devices still require correct patient-data entry, calibration/verification status, proper mouthpiece/filter setup, leak-free connections, and infection-control readiness. “Portable” does not mean exempt from QC.
In a full PFT laboratory, the same principle applies across spirometers and other lung-testing systems: verify setup, required calibration/verification, maintenance status, environmental inputs, and QC before reporting patient results.
Your AHRC notes are explicit: do not bypass failed QC. Withhold patient reporting, troubleshoot and correct the equipment problem, document the failure/corrective action, and repeat testing when appropriate.
Record failed QC, troubleshooting, component replacement, test interruption, and repeat testing according to laboratory policy. A corrected device problem should be traceable rather than silently ignored.
Age, standing height, sex, units, and selected reference equation affect predicted values. The measurement system can be mechanically accurate yet still produce misleading interpretation if the patient inputs are wrong.

Do not report a patient test through failed QC

Morning setup:
  • Portable spirometer powers on normally
  • 3-L verification is out of the facility's acceptable range at fast flow
  • Slow and medium checks are acceptable
  • Filter is damp and the tubing connection feels loose
