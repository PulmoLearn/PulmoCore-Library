Tracheostomy vs Laryngectomy Airway Emergencies

Identify which airway anatomy the patient has before directing oxygen or ventilation. A tracheostomy and a total laryngectomy both involve a neck stoma, but they are not the same emergency airway.

Anatomy-first emergency framework

Before oxygenating or ventilating, identify the airway type

Tracheostomy patientThe patient has a tracheostomy tube/stoma into the trachea. The source AA-05 pathway emphasizes knowing upper-airway patency, stoma maturity, and the patient-specific rescue plan.
Total laryngectomy patientYour LindseyJones RTE source explicitly states that after laryngectomy the patient is not breathing through the mouth because normal upper-airway humidification is bypassed.
Emergency decisionThe AA-06 registry requires directing emergency oxygenation/ventilation to the correct airway, not simply to the most familiar interface.
Device recognitionThe 2027 NBRC outline explicitly lists both tracheostomy tube and laryngectomy tube under artificial-airway management.
Exam move

When you see a neck stoma, do not assume the mouth/nose is the correct rescue route. First identify whether the patient has a tracheostomy or a total laryngectomy, then direct oxygen/ventilation to the airway that actually reaches the lungs.
