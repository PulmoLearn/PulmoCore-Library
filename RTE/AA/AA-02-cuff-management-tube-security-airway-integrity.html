What does the cuff finding mean?

Interpretation framework

Do not treat every leak with more air

<20 cmH₂O + audible leakPoor seal, lower delivered/exhaled volume, increased aspiration risk. Increase into target using a manometer, then reassess.
20–30 cmH₂OCourse target range. The goal is an adequate seal without excessive wall pressure.
>30 cmH₂OExcess tracheal-wall pressure and mucosal ischemia/injury risk. Reduce toward target while maintaining a seal.
Leak at target pressureDo not blindly add air. Check tube depth, cuff integrity, tube size, circuit, ventilator volumes/pressures, and the patient.
Use the whole airway picture. Cuff pressure + tube depth + bilateral breath sounds + exhaled CO₂ + ventilator data + patient response belong together.

The source specifically teaches a cuff manometer rather than estimating cuff status from the pilot balloon alone.
Low pressure can cause an audible leak, reduce effective delivered/exhaled tidal volume, and increase aspiration risk.
Pressure above the course target increases tracheal mucosal ischemia, ulceration, necrosis, and later stenosis risk.
Investigate displacement, cuff damage, inappropriate tube size, circuit leak, or another airway problem instead of overinflating.
Transport, repositioning, procedures, traction, or inadequate securement can change airway depth. Reassess the external mark and tube security after movement.
Repeat bilateral breath sounds, exhaled CO₂, volumes/pressures, oxygenation, depth, and patient response after cuff or tube adjustments.
