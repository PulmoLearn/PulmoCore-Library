Emergency decision framework

Patient first. Patency next. Replacement only when it is safe.

1. Assess the patientBreathing, color, responsiveness, work of breathing, air movement, oxygenation, and whether ventilation is effective.
2. Check trach patencyAttempt suction. Inability to pass a catheter is a major obstruction/displacement clue.
3. Remove an obstructed inner cannula when applicableYour source specifically includes this step in possible obstruction.
4. Provide oxygen / manual ventilation supportDo not let device troubleshooting delay gas exchange support.
5. Replace when the emergency plan allowsSame-size trach first; one size smaller if needed in the source home-emergency pathway.
6. Verify ventilationReplacement is not successful until effective gas movement is confirmed.
Never force a tube into resistance.

Your PulmoLearn source explicitly warns that blindly forcing a tracheostomy tube can injure the airway or create a false passage—placement into soft tissue instead of the tracheal lumen.

Source-bounded approach: assess the patient, inspect/manage the trach using the established emergency pathway, and support oxygenation/ventilation.
Your PulmoLearn lesson defines accidental decannulation as unexpected removal or displacement of a tracheostomy tube.
Source emergency response: call for help, position the patient, attempt the same-size replacement, use one size smaller if needed, confirm ventilation, and follow the established emergency pathway.
