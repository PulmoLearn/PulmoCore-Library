Exercise Assessment & Monitoring

The six-minute walk test (6MWT) records how far a person walks in six minutes under a standard protocol. Pair the distance with symptoms, heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation measured by pulse oximetry (SpO₂). Record the lowest SpO₂ during walking, not only the reading at the end. If exercise oxygen needs to be adjusted, assess that in a separate titration test.

What does the walk reveal?

Exercise assessment framework

Baseline → standardized walk → response → next test

Prepare a repeatable walk

Use the prescribed test protocol on a flat, measured course, ideally at least 30 meters long. Check the clinical history, resting pulse, blood pressure, SpO₂, breathlessness, walking aids, and prescribed oxygen setup. Explain that the goal is to walk as far as possible in six minutes, resting if needed.

Measure during and after

Track distance, SpO₂ through the walk when possible, heart rate, symptoms, and the reason for any stop. Record the lowest reliable SpO₂, which may occur before the end. Use standardized time prompts and do not pace the patient.

Respond to warning symptoms

Watch for chest pain, dizziness, staggering, intolerable breathlessness, or marked oxygen decline. Stop and assess according to the test protocol; get clinical help when indicated. A suspect oximeter value also needs a signal and patient check.

Interpret and titrate separately

Compare walk distance and symptoms with prior tests only when the course, oxygen setup, and other conditions are documented. A fall in SpO₂ with exercise calls for evaluation of activity oxygen needs. Keep oxygen flow fixed during a distance-outcome 6MWT; use a separate, monitored exercise titration under the order or protocol.

Core test rule: document the distance, lowest valid SpO₂, symptoms, heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen setup so the result can guide a safe plan.
Comparison warning: an end-of-test SpO₂ can miss an earlier nadir. A longer distance after an oxygen-flow change is not directly comparable with an earlier test unless the difference in support is considered.
Walk-test and oxygen checkpoints

Review each concept

Review relevant diagnoses, recent symptoms, walking ability, prescribed oxygen, medications, and prior test conditions. Measure resting heart rate, blood pressure, SpO₂, and perceived breathlessness before starting.
A 6MWT uses a flat measured course, ideally at least 30 meters. Explain the aim and use standard time prompts. Avoid walking ahead and setting the pace; note any aid or oxygen device that affects comparison.
Record full laps and the final partial lap so the total distance is accurate. Note stops and the reason for an early end. Distance is the main outcome, but it is not the only clinically useful result.
Monitor oxygen saturation during walking when possible and record the lowest valid reading, plus baseline and end values. Check pulse signal and the patient's condition if a reading seems unreliable.
Ask about breathlessness and fatigue, track heart rate, and note chest pain, lightheadedness, or other concerning symptoms. Obtain post-test blood pressure and recovery measurements as the protocol directs.
Chest pain, dizziness, staggering, or intolerable breathlessness warrants stopping and immediate assessment. Follow the test protocol for major oxygen decline and arrange needed care rather than urging the patient to finish.
When distance is the measured outcome, keep oxygen flow and delivery setup constant throughout that walk. Record the source, flow, and device. Changing flow during the walk changes the test conditions.
If exertion causes low SpO₂, perform a separate supervised exercise oxygen assessment under an order or protocol. Check baseline and response to each allowed support change, along with symptoms and activity; document the effective setup for the clinician.
Compare distance, SpO₂ nadir, symptoms, heart rate, stops, and oxygen conditions with the prior study. A similar distance may hide worse desaturation, and a higher end SpO₂ may hide a lower mid-walk value.
Communicate test conditions and results, symptoms, any safety stop, and a separate oxygen titration response. Explain the next step to the patient; update a prescription only through the authorized care plan.
Integrated RTE-style exercise case

Read more than the final number

A patient walks 310 meters in a standardized 6MWT on the ordered oxygen setup:
  • Resting SpO₂ is 95%
  • The lowest reliable SpO₂ during walking is 85%
  • SpO₂ recovers to 92% at the end; breathlessness rises
  • The patient has no chest pain or dizziness and is stable after rest
