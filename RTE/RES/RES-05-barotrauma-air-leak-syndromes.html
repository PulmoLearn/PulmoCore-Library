Barotrauma & Air-Leak Syndromes

Positive-pressure ventilation can force gas outside the normal alveolar-airway pathway. The location of escaped air determines the syndrome, the bedside clues determine urgency, and the care-plan change should reduce further pressure injury while treating any immediately dangerous pleural complication.

Air-leak framework

Recognize location → identify urgency → change the plan

Pneumothorax

Pleural air disrupts negative intrapleural pressure and can collapse part or all of the affected lung.

Tension pneumothorax

Your source treats sudden hypoxemia, unilateral absent breath sounds, hypotension/bradycardia, tracheal/mediastinal shift, and worsening ventilator mechanics as emergency tension-pattern clues.

Pneumomediastinum

Air is present in the mediastinum. Your PulmoLearn source includes it among air-leak findings to identify on imaging and correlate with clinical status.

Subcutaneous emphysema

Air tracks into subcutaneous tissue; your source specifically directs assessment for palpable crepitus and imaging evidence of subcutaneous air.

Pulmonary interstitial emphysema (PIE)

Your neonatal air-leak source includes PIE as a positive-pressure-associated air-leak syndrome and links severe air-leak physiology with high-frequency ventilation pathways.

Bronchopleural fistula (BPF)

A persistent airway-to-pleural leak may present with ongoing lost exhaled volume and persistent chest-drain bubbling.

The care-plan recommendation depends on urgency.

Stable air-leak findings call for reassessment, imaging/chest-drain evaluation, and pressure-limiting ventilation. Tension physiology requires immediate emergency escalation and decompression support rather than waiting for routine testing.
