High-Frequency Ventilation Fundamentals

High-frequency ventilation uses very small delivered volumes at very rapid rates. The exam-critical task is to separate oxygenation controls from ventilation controls and recognize when HFOV or HFJV may be useful.

High-frequency framework

Separate recruitment from small-volume ventilation

HFOV

Very small oscillatory volumes are delivered around a relatively constant mean airway pressure.

HFJV

Rapid, high-velocity pulses are delivered through a specialized adapter while exhalation remains passive.

Oxygenation

Your source states that oxygenation depends largely on lung recruitment and mean airway pressure plus FiO₂.

Ventilation

Your source states that ventilation depends on delivered oscillatory/jet volume, amplitude or pressure, frequency, and patient mechanics.

HFOV key control

Mean airway pressure is the primary recruitment/oxygenation control. Amplitude is a major determinant of oscillatory VT and CO₂ clearance.

HFJV key controls

Jet PIP, rate, inspiratory time, and background PEEP influence ventilation and lung volume.

Think: MAP = lung volume/oxygenation; amplitude = CO₂ clearance.

That pairing is explicitly supported by the supplied PulmoLearn neonatal source and is the most important HFOV discriminator for RTE-style questions.

HFOV delivers very rapid oscillatory pressure changes around a set mean airway pressure.
The source’s central idea is that lung recruitment can be managed relatively independently from very small-volume ventilation.
When lung volume is inadequate on HFOV, your scenario source says to increase mean airway pressure cautiously while reassessing oxygenation and hemodynamics.
MAP is not primarily the CO₂-control knob.
Your source states that amplitude strongly influences oscillatory tidal volume and CO₂ removal.
If PaCO₂ rises while oxygenation and lung volume are acceptable, the scenario’s first adjustment is increased oscillatory amplitude with reassessment.
The supplied source says amplitude and frequency influence CO₂ clearance, but the retrieved material does not spell out a universal adult/neonatal bedside rule for how every frequency change alters VT/PaCO₂. This lesson therefore avoids inventing a simplistic one-direction frequency rule.
HFJV delivers very short, high-velocity pulses through a specialized circuit/adapter.
Your source specifically notes its usefulness when severe air leak or pulmonary interstitial emphysema is present, or when very short inspiratory pulses with passive exhalation are desirable.
HFOV may help when conventional ventilation cannot maintain oxygenation without excessive pressure or volume.
HFJV may help in selected patients with significant air-leak physiology.
Your source says advanced technology does not replace the goal of effective gas exchange with the least injury.
