Prone Positioning, Recruitment & Advanced Oxygenation Strategies

When severe hypoxemia persists despite lung-protective ventilation and appropriate FiO₂/PEEP, selected advanced oxygenation strategies may improve recruitment and ventilation-perfusion matching. The RT must know the rationale, preparation, monitoring, risks, and how to judge whether the intervention actually helped.

Advanced oxygenation framework

Indication → safety → intervention → physiologic response

1. Confirm refractory disease.

Your ARDS source says severe disease may remain hypoxemic despite high FiO₂, PEEP and lung-protective ventilation. Advanced strategies are not automatic first steps.

2. Understand the physiology.

Your pediatric/ARDS source says proning improves ventilation-perfusion matching and reduces/recruits dorsal dependent atelectasis.

3. Protect airway and devices.

Your adult ARDS source specifically assigns the RT to secure the airway/tubes, monitor pressure points and reassess oxygenation during proning.

4. Balance recruitment against harm.

PEEP/recruitment can open unstable units, but excessive pressure can overdistend the lung and reduce venous return.

5. Reassess after every maneuver.

Trend SpO₂, ABG/PF ratio, PEEP/FiO₂ need, plateau pressure, compliance, synchrony and hemodynamics.

6. Stop escalating blindly.

If oxygenation does not improve or pressure/hemodynamics worsen, the strategy is not succeeding simply because more pressure was applied.

Recruitment is a physiologic response, not a setting.

The desired response is better oxygenation or mechanics without unacceptable overdistention or circulatory compromise.

Source boundary: the registry requires lung-recruitment maneuvers, but the retrieved PulmoLearn source set does not provide one universal pressure/time recruitment-maneuver protocol. This lesson therefore teaches selection, monitoring and response criteria without inventing a specific maneuver recipe.
