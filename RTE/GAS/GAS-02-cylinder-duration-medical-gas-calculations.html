What information belongs in the calculation?

Most errors happen before the arithmetic: wrong cylinder size, wrong factor, wrong flow, or no reserve plan.

Core formula

Cylinder duration is pressure converted to usable volume

The source lesson uses the formula below and these cylinder factors.

Cylinder duration (min) = Pressure (psig) × Cylinder factor ÷ Flow (L/min)
D = 0.16Portable cylinder
E = 0.28Common transport cylinder
G = 2.41Large cylinder
H/K = 3.14Large stationary cylinders
Memory rule

Pressure × factor ÷ flow. Identify the cylinder letter first. A correct formula with the wrong factor still gives the wrong answer.

Calculated duration 112 min → conservative planned-use limit ≈ 75 min.
  • Duration is inversely related to flow
  • If flow doubles and pressure is unchanged, duration is cut in half
  • Use the flow the patient is actually likely to need—not the lowest possible number
A patient whose oxygen requirement is rising needs a larger reserve than the original calculation suggests.
  • D 0.16
  • E 0.28
  • G 2.41
  • H/K 3.14
  • Using H/K factor for an E cylinder creates a dramatically exaggerated duration
Identify cylinder → read pressure → confirm flow → select factor → calculate → interpret.
  • Supply exceeds the entire expected time with meaningful reserve
  • Backup source is available when risk is high
  • Calculated duration barely covers expected time
  • Patient is unstable or oxygen need may increase
  • No backup exists for foreseeable delays
