Which specialty-gas concept fits the case?

Start with the physiologic target: pulmonary vascular resistance—not bronchospasm or fixed upper-airway obstruction.

Core framework

Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators target the pulmonary circulation

The source set is most detailed for inhaled nitric oxide; inhaled epoprostenol is represented more briefly as an aerosolized prostacyclin pulmonary vasodilator.

Inhaled nitric oxide (iNO)Selective gaseous pulmonary vasodilator. The source material associates it with decreased pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), improved V/Q relationships, and pulmonary-hypertension physiology.
Inhaled epoprostenolAerosolized prostacyclin used as an inhaled pulmonary vasodilator. The supplied review material names pulmonary hypertension and ARDS rescue therapy as uses.
Neither is a bronchodilatorThese therapies target pulmonary vascular tone. They are not the same treatment as albuterol for bronchospasm or heliox for turbulent upper-airway resistance.
Reassess the care planEvaluate oxygenation, clinical response, pulmonary-hypertension physiology, delivery-device function, and adverse effects before continuing or changing therapy.
RTE decision rule

If the problem is high pulmonary vascular resistance / pulmonary hypertension physiology, think inhaled pulmonary vasodilator. If the problem is bronchospasm or fixed airway narrowing, choose the therapy aimed at that problem instead.

The source exam material treats a decrease in systemic blood pressure as NOT one of the expected physiologic responses of inhaled nitric oxide.
  • Aerosolized prostacyclin
  • Used as an inhaled pulmonary vasodilator
  • Pulmonary hypertension
  • ARDS rescue therapy
  • Unlike iNO, epoprostenol is delivered as an aerosolized medication rather than as a nitric-oxide gas stream
The supplied PulmoLearn sources do not provide enough detail here to teach a dose table or a detailed epoprostenol adverse-effect list, so this lesson intentionally does not invent one.
  • NO can form NO₂ when exposed to oxygen
  • NO₂ is a pulmonary toxicant
  • The supplied review activity states that levels >10 ppm can cause cellular damage, pulmonary edema, and death
  • Unexpected high NO₂ is a device/delivery safety problem, not something to ignore because the ordered NO setting looks correct
Verify delivery setup and monitoring, protect the patient from continued unsafe exposure, and correct the system problem.
  • Patients on iNO require monitoring for methemoglobinemia
  • MetHb impairs normal oxygen carriage
  • Co-oximetry/hemoximetry is the direct measurement framework already taught in GX-05
If the clinical picture and oxygen measurements become inconsistent during iNO therapy, include methemoglobinemia in the reassessment.
  • Abrupt termination can cause rebound pulmonary hypertension
  • The neonatal competency guide also warns about rebound during weaning
  • Worsening pulmonary-hypertension physiology
  • Rebound hypoxemia
Wean rather than suddenly stopping when discontinuing iNO, and observe the physiologic response during the wean.
  • Sample-line occlusion
  • Pump failure
  • High pressure in the sample line
  • Leaks around sensors
  • The review activity teaches use of a filter at the distal end of the sample line during nebulizer therapy to prevent aerosol from clogging the monitoring system
The supplied review notes mention a dry-sample-gas alarm for low relative humidity and a service-advisory alarm that may be corrected by cycling device power.
