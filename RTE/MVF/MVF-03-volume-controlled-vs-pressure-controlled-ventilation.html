Volume-Controlled vs Pressure-Controlled Ventilation

Volume control fixes the delivered volume target and allows airway pressure to change with mechanics. Pressure control fixes inspiratory pressure and time, so tidal volume changes when compliance, resistance, effort, or leak changes.

Volume controlSet tidal volume + flow pattern; pressure varies.
Pressure controlSet inspiratory pressure + time; tidal volume varies.

What is fixed—and what is allowed to change?

Core comparison

Volume control: fixed volume, variable pressure. Pressure control: fixed pressure, variable volume.

Volume controlYour source states the ventilator targets a specific tidal volume. Airway pressure rises or falls as compliance and resistance change.
Pressure controlYour source sets inspiratory pressure above baseline and holds that pressure; the volume delivered varies with lung mechanics.
When compliance worsensIn volume control, the pressure required for the same volume rises. In pressure control, the same pressure produces less tidal volume.
When resistance risesVolume control tends to show higher peak pressure while volume remains targeted unless a safety pressure limit interrupts delivery. In pressure control, peak pressure stays near the set target while exhaled VT may fall.
Exam discriminator

Ask one question first: Which variable is guaranteed? If volume is targeted, monitor pressure. If pressure is targeted, monitor exhaled tidal volume and minute ventilation.
