Breath Variables: Trigger, Limit, Cycle & Baseline

Every mechanical breath can be described by four questions: What starts it? What variable is controlled or limited during inspiration? What ends inspiration? What pressure remains during exhalation before the next breath?

Primary: III.C.3a Invasive mechanical ventilationNo shared mapping in current crosswalk
TriggerStarts inspiration and ends exhalation.
Limit / controlA variable that reaches or is held at a set value during inspiration.
CycleEnds inspiration and starts exhalation.
BaselinePressure held during exhalation before the next inspiration.

Which variable is doing what?

Four-variable framework

Trigger → Limit → Cycle → Baseline

TriggerYour source defines the trigger as the variable that begins inspiration. Patient effort may trigger by pressure, flow, or volume; a mandatory machine breath is time-triggered.
Limit / controlThe source defines the limit as a variable that will not be exceeded and notes it is often the same variable being controlled during inspiration.
CycleThe cycle variable ends inspiration and begins expiration. Your source teaches pressure-control breaths as time-cycled and volume-control breaths as volume-cycled.
BaselineThe baseline is the pressure level during expiration before the next inspiration. When baseline pressure is above zero, your source calls it positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP).
Do not mix up “limit” and “cycle.”

A variable can be held or prevented from exceeding a value during inspiration without being the variable that ends inspiration. The source explicitly warns that the limit is not automatically what cycles the breath.

Mandatory machine breaths are time-triggered from the set respiratory rate/total cycle time.
Assisted or spontaneous breaths are patient-triggered by a sensed pressure, flow, or volume change.
For pressure triggering in your source, −1 cmH₂O is easier for the patient to generate than −3 cmH₂O.
For flow triggering, the ventilator senses a drop in baseline/bias flow. The source notes flow triggering tends to require less patient work when set appropriately.
Too sensitive: autotriggering may occur. Not sensitive enough: patient efforts may not be recognized, causing frustration/agitation.
In the source example, pressure control set at 20 cmH₂O holds the inspiratory pressure at that limit until the breath cycles.
In volume control, the set volume/flow define delivery while a separate high-pressure safety limit may stop further pressure rise and reduce delivered volume if reached.
Pressure-control breath in the source: time-cycled by inspiratory time.
Volume-control breath in the source: volume-cycled when the set volume is reached.
Pressure-support spontaneous breath in your later transcript: patient-triggered, pressure-supported, then flow-cycled as inspiratory flow falls to a threshold.
Your source defines baseline as the pressure held during expiration before the next breath begins.
If baseline is greater than zero, the source calls it PEEP.
CPAP is presented as maintaining a positive baseline while the patient breathes spontaneously above and below that level.
A patient-triggered breath that then receives the machine’s mandatory pressure- or volume-controlled breath is described in your source as an assisted breath.
A machine-triggered breath based on time is mandatory.
Your source says continuous spontaneous ventilation uses patient-triggered breaths.
For pressure support, pressure rises above baseline to the support level and the breath is flow-cycled when inspiratory flow drops to a set level.
