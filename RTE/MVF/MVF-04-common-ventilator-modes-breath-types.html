Common Ventilator Modes & Breath Types

Do not memorize mode names alone. First classify the breath: who starts it, who controls delivery, and who ends it. Then identify how the mode combines mandatory, assisted, synchronized, and spontaneous breaths.

Breath-type framework

Mandatory • Assisted • Spontaneous

Mandatory breathYour PulmoLearn source defines this as a breath the ventilator starts and ends. Typical pattern: time-triggered and ventilator-cycled.
Assisted breathThe patient triggers the breath, but the ventilator controls delivery and determines when inspiration ends. Your source example uses a patient trigger followed by a preset volume breath.
Spontaneous breathThe patient starts and ends the breath. The ventilator may add pressure support, but the patient retains control over timing.
Pressure supportYour source describes pressure support as support for spontaneous breaths rather than a guaranteed rate or tidal volume. The patient controls rate and breath depth; support is effort-dependent.
Mode names are combinations—not the definition of the breath itself.

The canonical registry specifically says to differentiate control, assist, spontaneous, mandatory, synchronized, and support without relying on brand-specific labels.

Assist/control therefore combines time-triggered mandatory breaths with patient-triggered assisted breaths that receive the preset controlled breath.

The “synchronized” feature means mandatory breaths are coordinated with patient effort when possible.
LindseyJones notes that SIMV allows smaller natural breaths between mechanical breaths in its comparison question.
Your source describes pressure support as patient-driven: set the pressure-assist level and cycling criterion, while rate and volume remain effort-dependent.
A neonatal question in your source explicitly asks what PS supports during PCV-SIMV: spontaneous breaths.
Do not confuse support with a guaranteed mandatory rate. Pressure support assists a patient-generated spontaneous breath.
Your earlier ventilator source describes CPAP as a positive baseline while the patient breathes spontaneously.
The weaning competency changes ventilator settings to CPAP for a spontaneous-breathing trial method.
Mode concept: spontaneous timing with a maintained positive baseline is different from a mandatory breath mode.
A breath can be spontaneous and pressure-supported. “Pressure support” describes how the ventilator assists the spontaneous breath; it does not replace the mandatory/assisted/spontaneous breath classification used in your PulmoLearn source.
SIMV is explicitly the synchronized intermittent mandatory ventilation mode named in your source material.
The point is not that every breath becomes mandatory; spontaneous breaths can still occur between the mandatory breaths.
The registry’s required practice angle is to select, initiate, adjust, or reassess support of oxygenation and/or ventilation.
Mode choice should therefore reflect how much mandatory support versus spontaneous participation the patient can safely provide—not a preference for a label alone.
Detailed disease-specific mode selection comes later: MVF-06, DVM lessons, and liberation lessons own the deeper disease and weaning decisions.
