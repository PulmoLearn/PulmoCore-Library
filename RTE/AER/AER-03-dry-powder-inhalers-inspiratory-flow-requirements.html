Dry-Powder Inhalers & Inspiratory-Flow Requirements

Recognize that the patient—not a propellant—creates the aerosol. Select DPIs only when inspiratory flow is adequate, protect powder from moisture, teach fast/forceful inhalation, and troubleshoot loading and technique failures.

Core technique

The patient’s inspiratory flow is the aerosol generator

No propellant pushes the medication out of a DPI.

Load the dose according to the specific device.
Exhale fully away from the DPI.
Seal the lips around the mouthpiece.
Inhale fast, forcefully, and deeply.
Hold the breath as tolerated.
Close/store the device correctly; rinse the mouth after an inhaled corticosteroid when applicable.
MDI vs DPI

MDI = slow inhalation. DPI = fast/forceful inhalation. A spacer can help MDI coordination, but it does not fix inadequate DPI inspiratory flow.

Slow/weak inspiration leaves clumped powder and wastes dose.
Source selection rule: severe acute obstruction, very weak inspiratory effort, or inability to follow loading/inhalation instructions makes DPI delivery unreliable.
Exhale away from the mouthpiece so humid exhaled gas does not enter the powder chamber.
Moisture can promote powder clumping and interfere with dispersion.
Technique clue: exhaling into a DPI is a device/technique error even if the subsequent inhalation is forceful.
A patient with RR 32, accessory-muscle use, hypoxemia, and inability to speak in full sentences may not generate a reliable DPI maneuver.
The source scenario recommends an acute bronchodilator via SVN rather than relying on a DPI in severe distress.
DPIs do not create and store an aerosol cloud in a chamber like an MDI can.
A spacer does not supply the inspiratory flow needed to disperse powder.
The lab competency includes determining whether the patient can achieve sufficient flow with rapid inhalation.
Observe return demonstration and reassess symptoms, breath sounds, vital signs, and adverse reactions after therapy.
RTE move: if the patient cannot generate the required maneuver, change the delivery approach rather than repeating ineffective DPI doses.
Technique failure

The patient exhales into the device

Scenario: A patient loads a DPI correctly, exhales through the mouthpiece, then inhales forcefully.
Inspiratory-flow failure

The powder is loaded—but the patient is too weak

Scenario: A patient with neuromuscular weakness cannot produce a rapid, deep inhalation.

Single-dose capsule DPI
