Critical source rule

Remove the HME before inline aerosol therapy. Aerosol can clog the HME element, sharply increasing circuit resistance. If aerosol treatments are frequent or ongoing, the source lesson recommends a heated-humidifier circuit instead.

The source lesson calls vibrating mesh the first-choice inline nebulizer because it adds no gas flow and does not alter tidal volume, trigger sensitivity, or pressure limits.
Source placement: inspiratory limb about 15 cm from the patient Y-piece.
Why placement matters: the circuit acts as part of the aerosol pathway; incorrect placement can increase losses before medication reaches the artificial airway.
An MDI can be used inline with a dedicated adapter in the inspiratory circuit.
The source lesson requires actuation timed with the ventilator breath.
Benefit: no added gas flow, but delivery depends on correct adapter position and timing.
Medication and water particles can clog the hygroscopic/filter element.
Clogging can dramatically increase circuit resistance.
Source action: remove the HME for treatment and reinstall afterward; if treatments are frequent, use heated humidification instead.
The source lesson states that a decelerating inspiratory flow waveform can increase aerosol residence time/deposition compared with a square waveform.
Slower respiratory rate and longer inspiratory time also favor deposition in the source material.
Exam cue: do not change ventilator settings solely to chase aerosol deposition if doing so compromises ventilation or safety; patient support remains primary.
The source lesson prefers VMN because it adds no extra gas flow, so HFNC flow and FiO₂ are not changed by the nebulizer itself.
Source placement: HFNC gas inlet, between humidifier and patient cannula.
Flow effect: higher HFNC flows can dilute/sweep aerosol and reduce deposition; source text specifically flags flows above 40 L/min as potentially less efficient.
An endotracheal or tracheostomy tube creates additional surfaces, bends, and narrowing before aerosol reaches the lower airway.
Patient/circuit factors therefore matter even when the nebulizer itself is working.
RTE move: evaluate the whole path when response is poor—do not assume “visible aerosol” means adequate distal dose.
The 2027 NBRC outline lists endotracheal instillation separately from aerosolized therapies.
