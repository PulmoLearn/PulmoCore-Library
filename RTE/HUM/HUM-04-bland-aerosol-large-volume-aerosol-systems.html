Core distinction

Bland aerosol is not just “more humidification”

HumidityInvisible molecular water vapor in gas. Heated humidifiers, bubble humidifiers, passover systems, and HMEs address humidity.
Bland aerosolVisible liquid droplets suspended in gas. The droplets contact the upper airway or artificial-airway interface and can hydrate mucosa or assist secretion mobilization.
Large-volume nebulizer (LVN)Uses a Bernoulli/Venturi jet. High-velocity gas creates low pressure that draws liquid up a siphon tube; a baffle helps size the droplets.
Course drive flowThe source lesson teaches roughly 6–15 L/min. Total aerosol flow must still meet or exceed patient inspiratory demand.
High-yield RTE trap

If total flow from the LVN system is too low for the patient’s inspiratory demand, the patient entrains room air around the interface and the delivered aerosol—and potentially delivered FiO₂—becomes diluted.

No aerosol

Scenario: Gas is flowing into the LVN, but no aerosol exits the device.
Inspiratory-demand trap

Why can FiO₂ fall when aerosol flow is insufficient?

Scenario: A patient on an aerosol mask from a large-volume nebulizer has inspiratory demand greater than the total flow supplied by the system.
Rainout + FiO₂ trap

Set 40%, measured 55% at the trach collar

Scenario: A tracheostomy collar is connected to a Venturi-type large-volume nebulizer set at 40%. Excess water has accumulated in the aerosol tubing and measured FiO₂ is unexpectedly 55%.
