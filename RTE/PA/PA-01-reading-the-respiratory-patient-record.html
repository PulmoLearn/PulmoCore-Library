What to look for
  • Onset and duration
  • Progression or severity
  • Triggers and relieving factors
  • Associated symptoms
  • Response to treatment
Quick clues
  • “Started yesterday…”
  • “Worsening for 3 days…”
  • “Occurs with exertion…”
  • “No longer relieved by albuterol…”
Exam move
Separate the current problem from chronic history. Ask: what changed, and how quickly?
What to look for
  • Known pulmonary/cardiac disease
  • Prior intubation or admissions
  • Home oxygen or PAP
  • Usual SpO₂ and activity tolerance
Quick clues
  • “At baseline…”
  • “Normally uses 2 L/min…”
  • “Previous intubation…”
  • “Usually walks independently…”
Exam move
Baseline → now → difference. The change is often more important than the isolated number.
What to look for
  • Active respiratory orders
  • Discontinued orders
  • Date/time of changes
  • Consultant recommendations
Quick clues
  • “Recommend…” ≠ active order
  • “Discontinued” = do not follow old order
  • Newer valid order usually supersedes older one
Exam move
Distinguish a recommendation from an active order. Check the most current valid instruction.
What to look for
  • Home vs current medications
  • Scheduled vs PRN use
  • Recent frequency changes
  • Allergies and discontinued meds
Quick clues
  • Home list says BID; patient says q2h
  • PRN use suddenly frequent
  • Medication appears in one list but not another
Exam move
Conflicting data → verify. Do not silently choose the source you prefer.
What to look for
  • Timestamps and sequence
  • Oxygen/support changes
  • Respiratory rate and mental status
  • Response to treatment
  • Secretion amount/character
Quick clues
  • Same SpO₂ on more oxygen
  • Increasing RR over several hours
  • More support for the same result
  • New somnolence or agitation
Exam move
Trend the support as well as the number. “Stable value” can hide deterioration.
What to look for
  • Current code-status order
  • Documented treatment limitations
  • Conflicting or outdated entries
Quick clues
  • DNR does not automatically mean “do not treat”
  • Routine appropriate respiratory care may still be indicated
Exam move
Verify the current order and follow the documented goals of care.
What to look for
  • Previous ABGs, PFTs, imaging, cultures
  • Prior sleep/exercise testing
  • Respiratory vaccination status
Quick clues
  • “Previous spirometry…”
  • “Prior CT showed…”
  • “Vaccination status…”
Exam move
Use prior data as context. Detailed interpretation belongs in its own testing lesson.

PA-01 takeaways

The goal is not to memorize chart tabs. It is to find what matters quickly and recognize when the record changes what you should do next.

Baseline → now
Compare the patient's usual status with the current status.
Trend support
A stable number on increasing support may represent deterioration.
Verify conflicts
When credible sources disagree, confirm before acting.
What this lesson does not replace: ABG interpretation, imaging interpretation, PFT interpretation, detailed pharmacology, or full physical assessment. Those skills have their own canonical lessons.
