Turning Assessment Findings Into the Next Step

Use what you already know about the patient to decide what information is still missing, which test can answer the question, and when the care plan must change.

2027 RTEI.A.8 Determine conditionI.E Recommend diagnosticsIII.E Change care plan
1. What do I know?Use the chart, history, exam, and current response.
2. What is unanswered?Name the missing clinical question.
3. What answers it?Choose the test, reassessment, or change that directly addresses that gap.
Socratic diagnostic routing

What information do you need next?

Do not order a test because it sounds respiratory. Choose the test that answers the unresolved question.

Fast routing framework

Match the clinical question to the information source

You are not learning every test here. You are learning when another type of information is needed.

Laboratory testsUse when the unanswered question involves blood counts, electrolytes/renal function, coagulation, metabolic stress, cardiac biomarkers, pathogens, or specialty blood testing.
ImagingUse when the unanswered question is structural or anatomic: infiltrate, pleural process, air leak, vascular/structural concern, or device position.
Pulmonary function testingUse when you need objective physiologic characterization of airflow, lung volume, gas transfer, respiratory muscle function, or exercise response.
Noninvasive monitoringUse when continuous or repeated bedside trending can answer the question without an invasive sample.
Blood gas / hemoximetryUse when you need direct information about ventilation, acid-base status, oxygenation, or dyshemoglobin measurement.
ECGUse when rhythm, rate, conduction, or a cardiac electrical problem may explain the cardiopulmonary presentation.
Hemodynamic monitoringUse when pressure, flow, preload/filling-pressure, or perfusion information is needed to explain instability.
Reassessment / care-plan changeIf the problem is already identified and the patient is not responding—or develops a severe complication—the next step may be to stop, modify, or escalate treatment rather than order more data.
RTE shortcut

Before choosing an answer, finish this sentence: “I still need to know ______.” Then choose the option that answers that question most directly.

Stop, change, or escalate

Do not keep testing when the treatment itself is causing harm

The registry specifically maps this lesson to terminating treatment for a severe complication and recommending care-plan changes.

Scenario: During a respiratory treatment, the patient develops a new severe adverse response and is clinically worsening.
