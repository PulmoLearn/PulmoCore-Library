Know what to look for

Open each area once. Focus on the clue that tells you what the finding means.

0 of 7 reviewedReview all areas to continue.
  • Respiratory rate and pattern
  • Accessory-muscle use
  • Retractions or paradoxical movement
  • Chest symmetry
  • Positioning and ability to speak
  • Tripod posture
  • Nasal flaring
  • Unequal chest rise
  • One-word speech
  • Visible fatigue
Inspection answers “What is the patient doing right now?” Work of breathing, symmetry, posture, and mental status are often apparent before equipment is used.
  • Alertness and orientation
  • Agitation
  • Somnolence
  • Ability to cooperate
  • Overall appearance
  • New agitation
  • Confusion
  • Drowsiness
  • Cannot stay awake
Do not dismiss a mental-status change as “just behavior.” In a respiratory patient, a new change can be a meaningful clinical finding.
  • Chest expansion symmetry
  • Crepitus
  • Tenderness
  • Tactile rhonchi/fremitus
  • Tracheal position
  • Pulse rhythm/intensity
  • One side expands less
  • Crackling sensation under skin
  • Trachea not midline
  • Vibration felt through chest wall
If the question says “feel,” think palpation. Palpation confirms movement, position, tenderness, and transmitted vibration.
  • Resonance
  • Dullness
  • Hyperresonance
  • Side-to-side differences
  • Dull = increased density
  • Hyperresonant = increased air
  • Compare symmetric areas
Percussion estimates density; it does not identify a diagnosis by itself. Combine the percussion note with the rest of the assessment.
  • Air movement
  • Symmetry
  • Wheezes
  • Crackles
  • Rhonchi
  • Stridor
  • Wheeze = musical airflow limitation
  • Crackles = discontinuous popping sounds
  • Rhonchi = coarse/low-pitched secretions
  • Stridor = upper-airway sound
First ask whether air movement is present and symmetric. Then identify added sounds. A sound is not useful if you ignore the amount of airflow.
  • Respiratory rate
  • Heart rate
  • Blood pressure
  • Temperature
  • SpO₂ in context
  • Rising RR
  • Tachycardia with distress
  • Fever with new respiratory symptoms
  • Same SpO₂ on more support
Trend + clinical context beats a single “normal” value. PA-04 will go deeper into deterioration.
  • Inspect
  • Palpate
  • Percuss
  • Auscultate
  • Reduces missed findings
  • Supports side-to-side comparison
  • Builds from visible to tactile to acoustic data
Use the method that directly answers the question. If asked about asymmetrical movement, palpate. If asked about airflow, auscultate.

Integrated physical assessment

Which finding combination is most meaningful?

Combine methods instead of treating findings as isolated facts.

A patient is assessed at the bedside:
  • Respiratory rate 30/min with visible accessory-muscle use
  • Right chest expands less than the left
  • Percussion is dull at the right base
  • Breath sounds are diminished at the right base
  • Patient is alert but increasingly anxious
