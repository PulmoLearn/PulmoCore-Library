Question 1
Three rules that unlock the patterns

Use the physics instead of memorizing seven rows

These three distinctions do most of the work.

1. Percussion: air vs densityHyperresonant suggests more air. Dull suggests more density, fluid, or tissue.
2. Fremitus: vibration transmissionConsolidated lung transmits vibration better. Pleural air or fluid reduces transmission to the chest wall.
3. Focal vs diffuseFocal/unilateral findings suggest a localized process. Diffuse/bilateral findings suggest a generalized process.
Fast discriminator

Decreased fremitus + hyperresonance → think pleural air. Decreased fremitus + dullness → think pleural fluid. Increased fremitus + dullness → think consolidated lung.

Tracheal position

Use direction of shift to separate pressure from volume loss

Tracheal position is especially useful when the rest of the chest findings are unilateral.

MidlineExpected with normal findings and many bilateral lung diseases, because there is no strong one-sided force pulling or pushing the mediastinum.
Shift away from the affected sideThink pressure or volume outside the lung, such as a large pneumothorax or large pleural effusion, pushing the mediastinum away.
Shift toward the affected sideThink volume loss, especially atelectasis, which pulls the mediastinum toward the affected side.
Fast rule

Push = away. Pull = toward. Pleural air/fluid can push the trachea away when large enough; atelectatic volume loss pulls it toward the affected side.

Quick check: A patient has markedly diminished right-sided breath sounds, hyperresonance on the right, and the trachea is shifted to the left.
Deterioration trap

When “less wheezing” is not improvement

Trend the whole patient, not one sound.

Severe asthma:
  • 20 minutes ago: loud diffuse wheezing, RR 30/min, speaking short phrases
  • Now: wheezing is barely audible, RR 34/min, marked accessory-muscle use, increasingly fatigued
Integrated RTE-style scenario

Recognize the condition from the physical pattern

Use the cluster; do not anchor on a single abnormality.

A patient develops sudden dyspnea after a procedure.
  • Right chest expands less than the left
  • Tactile fremitus is decreased on the right
  • Percussion is hyperresonant on the right
  • Breath sounds are markedly diminished on the right
