Calculation toolkit

Match the equation to the physiologic question

P/F = PaO₂ ÷ FiO₂

Quickly expresses arterial oxygenation relative to inspired oxygen. Enter FiO₂ as a decimal.

S/F = SpO₂ ÷ FiO₂

A noninvasive oxygenation ratio when an arterial PaO₂ is not available. Interpret with pulse-oximetry limitations in mind.

PAO₂ = FiO₂(PB − PH₂O) − PaCO₂/R

The alveolar gas equation estimates alveolar oxygen. At sea level, PB is about 760 mmHg, PH₂O 47 mmHg, and R is commonly approximated as 0.8.

A–a gradient = PAO₂ − PaO₂

Compares estimated alveolar oxygen with measured arterial oxygen to help characterize impaired oxygen transfer.

CaO₂ ≈ (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)

Arterial oxygen content depends mainly on hemoglobin and saturation; dissolved oxygen contributes only a small amount.

DO₂ = CO × CaO₂ × 10

Oxygen delivery combines oxygen content with cardiac output. Adequate PaO₂ does not guarantee adequate tissue oxygen delivery.

Vᴅ/Vᴛ = (PaCO₂ − PĒCO₂) ÷ PaCO₂

The Bohr relationship estimates the fraction of each tidal breath that does not participate effectively in CO₂ elimination.

V̇A = (Vᴛ − Vᴅ) × RR

Alveolar ventilation—not minute ventilation alone—drives CO₂ elimination.

Clinical interpretation

Calculate → sanity-check → interpret

1. Verify the inputs

Confirm FiO₂, oxygen device, timing, hemoglobin, saturation source, and whether values were obtained under the same conditions.

2. Check units and decimals

FiO₂ of 40% is 0.40 in P/F and S/F calculations. A decimal error can change the answer by a factor of 100.

3. Ask what changed

A worsening P/F ratio may reflect lower PaO₂, higher FiO₂ requirement, or both. A falling CaO₂ may occur with anemia even when PaO₂ is unchanged.

4. Connect the number to physiology

Use calculations with the patient assessment, trend, disease process, and treatment response. A calculated value is evidence—not a diagnosis by itself.
