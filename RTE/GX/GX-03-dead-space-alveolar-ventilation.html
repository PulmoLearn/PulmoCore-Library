Socratic dead-space string

Which kind of dead space is this?

Classify the problem before calculating anything.

Core framework

Only part of each breath reaches gas-exchanging alveoli

Minute ventilation counts all inhaled and exhaled volume. Alveolar ventilation subtracts dead space first.

Anatomic dead spaceThe conducting zone: gas in the nose, pharynx, trachea, bronchi, and other non–gas-exchanging airways does not directly participate in alveolar gas exchange.
Alveolar dead spaceGas reaches alveoli, but those alveoli receive too little perfusion to exchange CO₂ effectively.
Physiologic dead spaceTotal wasted ventilation = anatomic dead space + alveolar dead space.
Why it mattersAs dead space rises, more of each tidal volume is wasted. The patient may need more total ventilation to maintain the same alveolar ventilation.
V̇A = RR × (VT − VD)
Memory rule

Dead space comes out before you multiply. Use RR × (VT − VD), not RR × VT − VD.

Alveolar ventilation & PaCO₂

Predict what happens to PaCO₂

When CO₂ production is unchanged, PaCO₂ moves inversely with alveolar ventilation.

V̇A decreasesLess CO₂ is cleared → PaCO₂ tends to rise.
V̇A increasesMore CO₂ is cleared → PaCO₂ tends to fall.
Dead space increasesEffective ventilation falls unless RR or VT rises enough to compensate.
Same minute ventilation ≠ same alveolar ventilationA larger fraction of each breath can be wasted as dead space even when total minute ventilation looks unchanged.
Fast exam move

If PaCO₂ rises unexpectedly, ask whether alveolar ventilation fell because tidal volume, rate, or the dead-space fraction changed.

Same minute ventilation, different effective ventilation

Do not stop at RR × VT

Patient A: RR 12, VT 500 mL, VD 150 mL
Patient B: RR 20, VT 300 mL, VD 150 mL

Both have minute ventilation of 6 L/min.
Clinical dead-space clue

What happens when perfusion falls?

Scenario: Ventilation reaches an alveolar region, but pulmonary blood flow to that region falls sharply.
