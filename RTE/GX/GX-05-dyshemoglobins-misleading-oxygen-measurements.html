Dyshemoglobins & Misleading Oxygen Measurements

Recognize when a reassuring SpO₂ or PaO₂ does not mean hemoglobin is carrying oxygen normally—and know when co-oximetry is the test that answers the question.

Socratic discrepancy string

When the oxygen numbers do not tell the same story

Use the mismatch between symptoms, SpO₂, PaO₂, and measured hemoglobin species to decide what is actually happening.

Core framework

Know what each measurement actually measures

The safest interpretation begins by asking whether the device can distinguish abnormal hemoglobin species.

Standard pulse oximetryEstimates saturation using two wavelengths. It is excellent for trending under normal conditions but cannot reliably distinguish all hemoglobin species.
PaO₂Measures dissolved oxygen tension in plasma. It does not tell you which molecules are occupying hemoglobin binding sites.
Co-oximetryUses multiple wavelengths on a blood sample to measure species such as oxyhemoglobin (O₂Hb), carboxyhemoglobin (COHb), and methemoglobin (MetHb).
Clinical mismatchWhen the patient looks sicker than SpO₂ or PaO₂ suggests, do not force the patient to fit the number—question the measurement and hemoglobin species.
High-yield rule

Normal PaO₂ does not prove normal oxygen carriage. PaO₂ measures oxygen dissolved in plasma, not whether hemoglobin is carrying oxygen normally.

Co-oximetry to measure COHb and O₂Hb. Do not use a reassuring standard pulse-ox reading to exclude CO exposure.
  • Persistent saturation abnormality that does not fit the PaO₂
  • SpO₂ commonly trending toward the mid-80s
  • Poor response of the displayed SpO₂ to oxygen
  • PaO₂ can remain normal
  • Standard SpO₂ cannot quantify MetHb
Co-oximetry to directly quantify MetHb.
  • Oxyhemoglobin (O₂Hb)
  • Carboxyhemoglobin (COHb)
  • Methemoglobin (MetHb)
  • Other hemoglobin fractions depending on analyzer
  • CO exposure suspected
  • Methemoglobinemia suspected
  • Pulse ox and clinical presentation disagree
Co-oximetry is not the same as standard pulse oximetry. It is the hemoglobin-species measurement you need when dyshemoglobinemia is suspected.
  • Poor perfusion
  • Motion
  • Probe/site problems
  • Ambient light or nail products
  • Severe anemia can leave SpO₂ normal while total oxygen content is critically low
Assess the patient, verify signal quality and device conditions, then obtain ABG/co-oximetry when the unresolved question requires it.
