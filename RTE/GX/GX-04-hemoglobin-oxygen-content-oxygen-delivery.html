Core framework

Pressure, saturation, content, and delivery are not the same thing

The registry expects you to connect hemoglobin, saturation, PaO₂, cardiac output, oxygen content, delivery, extraction, and mixed venous oxygenation.

PaO₂Oxygen partial pressure dissolved in arterial plasma. It helps describe oxygenation but contributes only a small amount to total oxygen content.
SaO₂Percentage of hemoglobin oxygen-binding sites occupied by oxygen.
HemoglobinThe major oxygen carrier. Severe anemia can sharply reduce oxygen content even with normal PaO₂ and saturation.
Cardiac outputDetermines how much oxygenated blood moves to tissues each minute. Normal content cannot rescue critically low flow.
CaO₂ = (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)
DO₂ = Cardiac Output × CaO₂ × 10
Memory rule

Content = what each dL carries. Delivery = content × flow. A normal oxygen pressure does not guarantee either one.

Interpret the weak link

What can make oxygen delivery fail?

0 of 5 reviewedReview all transport patterns.
  • PaO₂ may be normal

  • Hb is low
  • CaO₂ falls
  • Do not call oxygen transport adequate from SpO₂ alone.
Most oxygen rides on hemoglobin, so severe anemia can cause tissue hypoxia without arterial hypoxemia.
  • PaO₂ low
  • SaO₂ may be low
  • CaO₂ falls if hemoglobin loading falls
  • Connect oxygenation failure to content, not just the PaO₂ number.
The hemoglobin-bound portion usually dominates CaO₂; dissolved oxygen contributes relatively little.
  • CaO₂ can be normal
  • Cardiac output low
  • DO₂ falls
  • Poor perfusion can persist despite good saturation.
DO₂ depends on both oxygen content and blood flow.
  • C(a-v)O₂ widens
  • Venous oxygen saturation tends to fall
  • Delivery is low, demand is high, or both
Oxygen extraction is commonly around 20–30% at rest; interpret trends with the whole clinical picture.
  • Commonly about 60–80% in a stable adult
  • Represents oxygen remaining after systemic extraction
  • Low delivery
  • High metabolic demand
  • Or both
