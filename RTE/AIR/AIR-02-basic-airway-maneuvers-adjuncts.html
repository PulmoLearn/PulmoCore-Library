Manual airway maneuvers

Head-tilt chin-lift vs jaw thrust

Head-tilt chin-liftSource use: unconscious patient with no suspected cervical-spine injury. Extend the head and lift the bony chin to move the tongue away from the posterior pharynx.
Jaw thrustSource use: suspected or confirmed cervical-spine injury. Lift the angles of the mandible forward without intentionally extending or rotating the neck.
Sniffing positionSource use: optimize laryngoscopic visualization for intubation; not the basic rescue maneuver. Avoid with unstable cervical spine.
Patient-first ruleDo not leave a trauma patient obstructed while waiting for imaging. Open the airway with jaw thrust and cervical stabilization while definitive support is prepared.
Sequence

Open the airway → add the appropriate basic adjunct → create an effective mask seal → ventilate → verify chest rise and response → escalate if ventilation remains inadequate.

The source uses the oropharyngeal airway for an unconscious patient without an intact gag reflex.
It helps prevent the tongue from obstructing the pharynx.
Avoid: conscious or semiconscious patient with an intact gag reflex.
Source sizing: corner of mouth to angle of mandible.
Too small may push the tongue backward.
Too large may traumatize the airway or displace the epiglottis.
The source uses the NPA in conscious or semiconscious patients with an intact gag reflex.
It is also useful when mouth access is limited, such as trismus.
Avoid in this lesson: suspected basilar skull fracture or severe midface/nasal trauma; also use caution with significant epistaxis/coagulopathy.
Source sizing: nostril/tip of nose to earlobe or tragus region.
Lubricate with a water-soluble product and advance gently along the floor of the nose rather than aiming upward.
Thumb and index finger form the “C” around the mask.
The remaining fingers lift the bony mandible to form the “E.” Avoid pressing into soft tissue and worsening obstruction.
Goal: pull the jaw into the mask while maintaining a seal rather than simply pushing the mask down onto the face.
If one-person ventilation gives poor chest rise, first reassess airway position and mask seal.
The source recommends a two-person technique when seal is difficult: one rescuer maintains jaw lift/seal while the second squeezes the bag.
Beard/facial hair, facial trauma, poor mask size, or poor positioning can all impair the seal.
The source self-inflating bag refills on its own and can ventilate even if compressed gas is disconnected.
With oxygen and reservoir, the source describes high-FiO₂ emergency/manual ventilation.
Troubleshoot poor chest rise: airway position → mask seal → adjunct → obstruction/device issue → need for advanced airway.
