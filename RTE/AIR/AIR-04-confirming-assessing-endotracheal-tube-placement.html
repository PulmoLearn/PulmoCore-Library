Confirming & Assessing Endotracheal Tube Placement

Use continuous exhaled CO₂, bilateral assessment, tube-depth checks, ventilator data, and imaging together—then recognize esophageal, mainstem, high, or migrated tube positions before they become catastrophic.

Placement verification

Immediate confirmation becomes continuous confirmation

Waveform CO₂Persistent exhaled CO₂ provides strong immediate evidence of tracheal placement when perfusion is present.
Clinical assessmentSymmetric chest rise, equal bilateral breath sounds, improving oxygenation, and appropriate tube depth support the placement assessment.
Depth & imagingAdult ETT tip is generally targeted about 3–5 cm above the carina with the head neutral.
ReconfirmationExternal depth mark, securement, pressures/volumes, capnography, and bedside assessment must be repeated after movement or deterioration.
Critical distinction

No CO₂ immediately after placement and sudden loss of a previously normal waveform are not the same problem. The first strongly suggests esophageal placement or a disconnected sampling setup; the second raises displacement, circuit disconnection, apnea, obstruction, loss of perfusion, or monitor failure.

Chest movement or a temporarily high SpO₂ after preoxygenation does not rule out esophageal placement.

Source clue: markedly diminished/absent LEFT breath sounds with normal RIGHT breath sounds after intubation or movement.
Capnography may remain normal because the tube is still in the airway.
Corrective source case: withdraw the tube to the appropriate depth, re-auscultate, reassess oxygenation, secure, and reconfirm with imaging as indicated.
A tube positioned too high in the trachea may migrate out with head/neck movement.
Use external depth marking plus chest imaging and clinical assessment; do not interpret “tracheal CO₂” as proof that the depth is safe.

Sudden deterioration after movement: think displacement, obstruction, mainstem intubation, or circuit/device failure.
If a previously normal capnogram suddenly becomes flat, consider tube displacement, disconnection, apnea, severe obstruction, cardiac arrest/profound low perfusion, or sampling-line/monitor failure.
The source directs simultaneous patient assessment and equipment troubleshooting, with manual ventilation when indicated.
Do not wait for SpO₂ to fall. Saturation may remain high briefly, especially after preoxygenation.
Portable chest radiography provides rapid bedside confirmation of tube position and can identify major pulmonary complications.
Imaging supports—not replaces—capnography, bilateral chest rise/breath sounds, tube-depth marking, pressures/volumes, oxygenation, and hemodynamic assessment.
After any tube adjustment, repeat breath sounds, chest rise, capnography, oxygenation, pressures and exhaled volumes.
Repeat imaging when required and document the new depth.
