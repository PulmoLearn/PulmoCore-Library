Pre-intubation sequence

Prepare to succeed—and prepare to fail forward

Your source lesson organizes safe intubation around oxygen reserve, equipment readiness, and a defined backup plan.

1
Assess the airway and indication.
Recognize predicted difficulty and whether the patient still has time for controlled preparation.
2
Preoxygenate.
Maximize oxygen reserve before the attempt. Your source calls this non-negotiable.
3
Prepare primary equipment.
Appropriate ETT, laryngoscope/video device as planned, stylet, suction, BVM, monitoring, CO₂ confirmation, and securement.
4
Prepare backup equipment.
Backup ETT size, supraglottic airway, alternate intubation aid, and a named failed-airway plan.
5
Define team roles.
Primary operator, backup operator, airway assistant, medication role, monitoring, attempt limit, and escalation plan.
6
Prepare for immediate verification.
CO₂ detector/capnography, chest assessment, depth/securement checks, and post-intubation support must be ready before the tube goes in.
Exam move

If a question asks what is missing from an intubation setup, prioritize equipment or actions that protect oxygenation and rescue failure: preoxygenation, suction, BVM, backup airway, CO₂ confirmation, and a defined backup plan.

Your source intubation checklist requires suction immediately available before the attempt.
Blood, vomitus, and secretions can obstruct visualization and threaten ventilation.
Setup check: having a suction catheter in the room is not enough if the regulator, tubing, canister, or suction source is not functional.
The source pediatric airway lesson says preoxygenation maximizes oxygen reserve and buys time if the first attempt fails.
BVM must be ready for backup ventilation.
Shared airway-patency angle: if oxygenation/ventilation cannot be maintained during preparation, effective BVM takes priority over pushing forward with an unready intubation attempt.
Source preparation: define an attempt limit, name a backup operator, and know the failed-airway pathway before the first attempt.
A supraglottic airway is specifically included as an alternative/rescue device.
Safety principle: repeated traumatic attempts without changing the plan are not a backup strategy.
Your pharmacology source identifies succinylcholine as a rapid-onset depolarizing neuromuscular blocker used for rapid-sequence intubation.
Rocuronium is a nondepolarizing paralytic used in intubation/ventilation contexts.
Critical carryover from PHARM-05: paralysis does not provide sedation or analgesia. Medication roles must not be confused during preparation.
Have pulse oximetry and the relevant cardiorespiratory monitoring in place before the attempt.
Have exhaled CO₂ confirmation immediately available for post-placement verification.
Boundary: detailed interpretation of tube position belongs to AIR-04. AIR-03 is responsible for making sure verification tools are ready.
Your ET-tube exchange competency requires the replacement tube, appropriately sized tube exchanger, water-soluble lubricant, cuff-check syringe, securement, suction, CO₂ detector, pulse oximeter, BVM, and emergency-airway equipment prepared first.
The competency also has the therapist record current tube depth and preoxygenate/suction before exchange.
Principle: exchanging an established airway is another high-risk airway procedure—prepare rescue oxygenation and confirmation before removing the existing tube.

Integrated RTE-style intubation setup

Find the dangerous omissions before attempt #1

Patient:
  • Progressive respiratory failure requiring intubation
  • Preoxygenation started
  • Primary ETT and laryngoscope ready
  • Suction present but untested
  • No backup ETT or supraglottic airway at bedside
  • No CO₂ detector/capnography ready
  • No backup operator or failed-airway plan identified
