Difficult-Airway & Rescue-Airway Strategies

Recognize when an airway plan is failing, restore oxygenation before repeating ineffective attempts, use supraglottic and intubation-assist devices appropriately, recognize when an emergency surgical airway is required, and prevent airway-fire hazards.

Is the current airway strategy still working?

The exam move is often recognizing failure early enough to preserve oxygenation.

Failed-airway decision framework

Escalation is driven by failure—not by a rigid device sequence

1. Can the airway be opened?Reposition, optimize jaw lift, suction, and use the appropriate basic adjunct.
2. Can you oxygenate/ventilate?If BVM is ineffective despite correction, stop treating repeated bagging as progress.
3. Can a definitive ETT be placed?Use the planned visualization/assist strategy and avoid repeated identical failed attempts.
4. Can a supraglottic airway rescue gas exchange?An LMA or other supraglottic device can bridge a failed intubation when ventilation must be restored quickly.
5. Cannot intubate + cannot oxygenate/ventilateThe source identifies the cricothyroid membrane as the emergency surgical-airway site and cricothyrotomy as the emergency pathway after less invasive methods fail.
Patient-first safety

Repeated failed intubation attempts while saturation is falling are not progress. Restore oxygenation with the best available rescue method and move to the next airway strategy.

The source describes the LMA as a supraglottic rescue device for failed intubation or difficult airway.
It does not require direct vocal-cord visualization and can restore ventilation quickly.
Limitation: it is not a definitive aspiration-protective airway in the source. It is a rescue bridge while the definitive plan is developed.
The 2027 NBRC outline explicitly lists the video laryngoscope as a device that assists with intubation.
Direct laryngoscopy uses the selected blade to create a direct view of the glottis; video laryngoscopy provides a camera-assisted view.
Exam principle: when anatomy makes direct visualization difficult, changing the visualization strategy may be safer than repeating the same unsuccessful attempt.
The source describes the bougie as a thin flexible guide inserted into the trachea when the glottic view is limited.
The ETT is then advanced over the bougie into the trachea.
2027 NBRC: bougie is specifically named as an example of a device that assists with intubation.
The NBRC outline specifically names the endotracheal tube exchanger under intubation-assist devices.
Your competency source requires the replacement tube, appropriately sized exchanger, suction, BVM, CO₂ confirmation, monitoring, and emergency-airway equipment before an exchange begins.
Safety: exchanging an airway is not routine removal; rescue oxygenation and failed-exchange planning must be ready first.
The source identifies the cricothyroid membrane as the emergency surgical-airway site.
Cricothyrotomy is the emergency pathway when the patient cannot be oxygenated/ventilated by less invasive methods and a definitive airway cannot be established through the usual route.
Do not confuse recognition with routine tracheotomy. The registry shares AIR-05 with III.H.5 because learners need to recognize emergency airway escalation and procedure support, not perform elective surgical technique from this micro-lesson.
Your bronchoscopy source warns that thermal airway procedures create an airway-fire risk when FiO₂ is high.
The source safety rule is to use the lowest safe FiO₂ per protocol before thermal energy is activated.
Exam cue: heat/ignition source + oxygen-enriched airway = fire hazard. Cold-based or purely mechanical techniques do not carry the same thermal ignition risk.
Your source failed-airway pathway emphasizes preserving oxygenation over accumulating attempts.
A meaningful next attempt should change the strategy: operator, visualization device, adjunct, rescue airway, or pathway as clinically appropriate.
RTE logic: if oxygenation is worsening, restore gas exchange before pursuing another attempt unless the definitive rescue procedure is now required.
