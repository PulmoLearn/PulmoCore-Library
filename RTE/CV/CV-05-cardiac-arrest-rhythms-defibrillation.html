Cardiac Arrest Rhythms & Defibrillation

In adult cardiac arrest, the rhythm determines whether a shock is indicated. Ventricular fibrillation (VF) and pulseless ventricular tachycardia (pVT) are shockable. Pulseless electrical activity (PEA) and asystole are not. Start high-quality cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), support breathing, check the defibrillator setup, and keep interruptions brief.

Arrest equipment and rhythm framework

Pulse → CPR → shock decision → immediate CPR

Confirm the arrest

Check responsiveness, breathing, and a pulse promptly. Activate the emergency response and begin CPR if no definite pulse. Do not decide from the ECG tracing alone: PEA has organized electrical activity but no palpable pulse.

Classify the rhythm

VF is chaotic without organized QRS complexes; pVT has a fast wide ventricular rhythm without a pulse. Both use unsynchronized defibrillation. PEA and asystole use the nonshockable pathway with CPR and treatment of causes.

Check the defibrillator

Apply pads firmly in the appropriate positions, check cable and pad contact, select defibrillation mode rather than synchronization for VF/pVT, and follow the device’s biphasic energy recommendation. If unknown, the current algorithm uses the maximum available setting.

Protect CPR and breathing

Keep compressions going while pads and device are prepared. Coordinate a brief clear call before shock, then resume compressions immediately. Provide oxygen and effective breaths without overventilating or delaying defibrillation for a difficult airway.

Core rule: shock VF or pVT, not PEA or asystole. Fix a device setup problem while CPR continues and resume compressions immediately after a shock.
Energy cue: biphasic defibrillation follows the manufacturer’s recommendation, often an initial 120–200 joules. When the recommendation is unknown, use the maximum available setting under the current adult algorithm; do not invent one energy for every device.
Rhythm, device, and breathing

Review each concept

A monitor rhythm cannot confirm circulation. Assess responsiveness and breathing, check the pulse promptly, activate the arrest response, and begin compressions when no definite pulse is present.
Ventricular fibrillation (VF) shows chaotic electrical activity without organized QRS complexes. With no pulse it is a shockable cardiac-arrest rhythm requiring CPR and prompt defibrillation.
Pulseless ventricular tachycardia (pVT) has a fast wide rhythm on the monitor without a pulse. It is shockable. Ventricular tachycardia with a pulse requires a separate patient-focused tachycardia assessment.
Pulseless electrical activity (PEA) may show an organized rhythm but no effective pulse. Do CPR and look for reversible causes; do not deliver a shock for PEA.
Asystole is absence of organized ventricular electrical activity. Check lead and cable connections and confirm the rhythm without delaying CPR. It is a nonshockable rhythm.
Pads must adhere to skin in a suitable position and connect to the defibrillator. A disconnected cable, loose pad, or poor contact can block effective shock delivery; correct it while compressions continue.
Synchronized cardioversion times shocks to a QRS complex. In VF or pVT arrest, use unsynchronized defibrillation. Confirm the selected device mode before delivering a shock.
For a biphasic device, use the manufacturer-recommended initial defibrillation energy; the current adult algorithm gives 120–200 joules as examples. If the recommendation is unknown, use the maximum available setting.
Give oxygen and effective ventilation with the team while minimizing pauses and avoiding excessive breaths. An advanced airway should not interrupt compressions or postpone an indicated shock.
After an indicated shock, resume CPR immediately and reassess the rhythm at the next planned pause. A displayed organized rhythm requires a pulse and perfusion check before calling it return of spontaneous circulation.
Integrated RTE-style arrest case

VF and a device mode problem

An adult is unresponsive, not breathing normally, and has no definite pulse:
  • CPR is underway and the monitor shows ventricular fibrillation
  • Adhesive pads are connected but the defibrillator shows SYNC mode
  • The biphasic manufacturer’s initial shock recommendation is available
  • The team is supporting ventilation and preparing to clear for shock
