Tidal Volume, Respiratory Rate & Minute Ventilation

Tidal volume and respiratory rate work together to create minute ventilation. Choose tidal volume from the correct body-size reference, calculate the total ventilation correctly, then decide whether changing volume or rate is the safer way to change ventilation.

Which number matters—and what does it mean?

Core relationships

Choose VT safely, then use VT × rate to understand total ventilation

Minute ventilation
V̇E = VT × RR

Use compatible units. 500 mL = 0.5 L. At 14/min: 0.5 L × 14 = 7 L/min.

Reverse check
VT = V̇E ÷ RR

Your teaching guide recommends reversing the calculation to catch decimal or unit errors.

VT uses predicted/ideal body weight

Your PulmoLearn calculator and teaching guide explicitly state that lung-protective tidal volume selection uses predicted/ideal body weight derived from height and sex—not actual body weight.

Source reference range

Your calculator displays 4, 6, and 8 mL/kg PBW targets, with 6 mL/kg identified as a common lung-protective target and 8 mL/kg as an upper reference depending on context.

Calculation is not the final clinical decision.

Your source repeatedly says to verify the correct weight type, height/sex inputs, prescribed mL/kg target, units, disease process, and clinical goal before placing a calculated value on the ventilator.
