Inspiratory Flow, Inspiratory Time & I:E Ratio

Respiratory rate sets the total time available for each breath. Inspiratory time uses part of that cycle; the rest becomes expiratory time. Flow can shorten or lengthen inspiration in volume control, changing how much time remains for exhalation.

Where did the available breath-cycle time go?

Timing framework

Rate sets the cycle; Ti and Te divide it

Total cycle time
TCT = 60 ÷ RR

At RR 15/min, the total cycle time is 4 seconds.

Expiratory time
Te = TCT − Ti

At RR 15/min and Ti 1.0 sec, Te is 3.0 sec.

I:E ratio

Compare Ti and Te. With Ti 1 sec and Te 3 sec, the ratio is 1:3. With Ti 0.5 sec and Te 3.5 sec, divide both sides by 0.5 → 1:7.

Find Ti from I:E
Ti = TCT ÷ total I:E parts × inspiratory parts

At RR 15/min and I:E 1:3, TCT = 4 sec and Ti = 4 ÷ 4 = 1 sec.

Timing changes can be indirect.

Your source specifically warns that exam questions may not say “shorten Ti.” They may instead offer increase inspiratory flow or increase expiratory time. Understand the relationship rather than memorizing one phrase.
