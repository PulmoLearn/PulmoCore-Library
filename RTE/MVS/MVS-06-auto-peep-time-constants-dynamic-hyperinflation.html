Auto-PEEP, Time Constants & Dynamic Hyperinflation

Auto-PEEP develops when the next breath starts before the previous breath has fully emptied. Resistance, compliance, respiratory rate, tidal volume, inspiratory timing, bronchospasm, and secretions can all determine whether enough expiratory time is available.

Why did the lungs fail to empty?

Core framework

Long emptying time + too little expiratory time = dynamic hyperinflation

Time constant
Time constant = Resistance × Compliance

Your lecture source specifies compliance must be expressed in liters for the calculation.

What one time constant means

Your source teaches about 63.2% filling after one time constant, 86.5% after two, and about 95% after three.

Long time constant

Higher resistance and/or higher compliance lengthen filling and emptying. Your source specifically connects increased airway resistance with slower lung emptying.

Auto-PEEP

Your PulmoLearn graphics source defines it as incomplete exhalation: gas remains trapped, end-expiratory lung volume rises, and the patient must overcome intrinsic pressure before triggering the next breath.

High PaCO₂ does not automatically mean “increase the rate.”

Your source calls this a common new-graduate mistake. In a patient already air trapping, a faster rate shortens expiratory time and can worsen auto-PEEP, hypotension, and CO₂ retention.
