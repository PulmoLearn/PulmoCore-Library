PEEP, FiO₂ & Oxygenation Strategy

FiO₂ raises the oxygen concentration delivered. PEEP helps keep recruitable alveoli open. Use them together, then reassess oxygenation, compliance, overdistention, blood pressure, and whether the patient still needs the same level of support.

More oxygen, more PEEP—or neither?

Oxygenation framework

Identify the problem → choose FiO₂/PEEP → reassess benefit and harm

FiO₂ raises delivered O₂.

Use enough inspired oxygen to meet the ordered oxygenation goal, then reduce excess exposure when the patient is stable enough to do so.

PEEP supports recruitment.

Your oxygenation source pairs FiO₂ with PEEP in ARDS strategies rather than treating FiO₂ as the only way to improve oxygenation.

Recruitment can help—but more PEEP is not always better.

Your graphics source warns that excessive PEEP in already-open alveoli can contribute to overdistention.

Hemodynamics matter.

Your ARDS oxygenation source says to reassess sooner when a change causes a drop in blood pressure, oxygen saturation, or static compliance.

Do not chase SpO₂ with one setting in isolation.

If hypoxemia reflects recruitable collapsed/flooded alveoli, adding only FiO₂ may be less effective than an ordered strategy that also uses PEEP. If PEEP causes hypotension or worsening compliance, the “oxygenation improvement” may come at an unsafe cost.
