Compliance, Resistance & Pressure Calculations

Peak and plateau pressures answer different questions. Use an inspiratory hold to obtain plateau pressure, then combine pressure, volume, PEEP, and flow to separate a resistance problem from a compliance problem.

Pressure map

Use the right pressure for the right calculation

Dynamic compliance
Cdyn = VT ÷ (PIP − PEEP)

Measured while gas is moving, so airway resistance contributes to the pressure requirement.

Static compliance
Cstat = VT ÷ (Pplat − PEEP)

Your lab competency notes that total PEEP may be used in the denominator when available for a more accurate Cstat.

Airway resistance
Raw = (PIP − Pplat) ÷ Flow (L/sec)

If flow is provided in L/min, convert it to L/sec first.

Driving pressure
ΔP = Pplat − PEEP

Your PulmoLearn pediatric ventilation source defines driving pressure this way and says to interpret it with delivered VT and mechanics.

The key separator is the gap between PIP and Pplat.

If PIP rises but Pplat stays similar, the resistive pressure gap widens. If PIP and Pplat rise together, the problem points more toward reduced compliance. Trend both rather than interpreting peak pressure alone.

Your lab competency measures Pplat by performing an inspiratory pause/hold.
Your pediatric source explains why: plateau pressure is measured during a no-flow period and more closely approximates alveolar pressure than peak pressure.
Testing angle: a displayed PIP is not a substitute for plateau pressure when the question asks about static mechanics.
PulmoLearn calculator formula:
Cdyn = VT ÷ (PIP − PEEP)
Your calculator states that Cdyn reflects the pressure needed while airflow is occurring, so increased airway resistance can make Cdyn fall even when static compliance is unchanged.
PulmoLearn calculator formula:
Cstat = VT ÷ (Pplat − PEEP)
Your lab competency allows total PEEP in place of set PEEP when available to calculate static compliance more accurately.
Raw = (PIP − Pplat) ÷ Flow (L/sec)
Example from your calculator: PIP 35, Pplat 25, flow 60 L/min = 1 L/sec → Raw 10 cmH₂O/L/sec.
Common source error: using L/min directly instead of converting to L/sec.
Driving pressure = Pplat − PEEP
Your pediatric source says to interpret driving pressure with delivered tidal volume and chest-wall mechanics rather than as an isolated number.
Source boundary: MVS-05 teaches the source-supported calculation and trending relationship. It does not invent a universal adult threshold where the current adult source set does not provide one.
A widening PIP−Pplat gap means more pressure is being spent overcoming resistance during flow.
Your dynamic-compliance calculator lists increased resistance, secretions, bronchospasm, kinked tubing, and high flow-related pressure as reasons Cdyn may fall relative to Cstat.
If more pressure is needed even during the no-flow plateau, the elastic/static load has increased.
Your Week 1 lecture specifically asks students to connect increasing pressure needed for a set VT with worsening compliance.
One high PIP does not identify the cause. Compare prior PIP, Pplat, VT, PEEP, flow, Cdyn/Cstat, airway findings, and the patient.
Your Unit 2 exam demonstrates this explicitly: after suctioning/draining tubing, PIP falls while Pplat remains 20, dynamic compliance improves, and the interpretation is decreased airway resistance.
