Medical Gas Sources, Regulators & Safety Systems

Follow the gas from source to patient: identify the correct source and connection, reduce pressure safely, meter flow accurately, verify the delivered mixture, and troubleshoot the system without trusting a single gauge or color cue.

Source-to-patient framework

Never let one “normal” component end the troubleshooting process

The source lesson repeatedly teaches that a displayed pressure or flow does not prove gas is reaching the patient.

1. Identify the gasRead the cylinder or outlet label. Color is a secondary cue and should not be the sole method of gas identification.
2. Verify a compatible connectionPISS and DISS are gas-specific mechanical safeguards designed to prevent wrong-gas connections.
3. Control pressure and flowThe regulator reduces high cylinder pressure; the flowmeter controls or indicates flow delivered downstream.
4. Verify actual deliveryTrace the system through tubing, interfaces, blenders, and analyzers, then confirm the patient response.
Safe troubleshooting path

Patient → source → pressure/regulator → flowmeter → tubing/connections → delivery interface/analyzer → patient response.

Analyzer & blender troubleshooting

Do not trust the dial when the gases disagree

Scenario: A blender is set to a specific FiO₂, but a calibrated analyzer reads substantially lower. One high-pressure source gas has become disconnected.
