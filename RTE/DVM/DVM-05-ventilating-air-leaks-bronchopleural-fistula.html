Ventilating Patients With Air Leaks & Bronchopleural Fistula

Air can leave the airway or lung instead of returning through the ventilator. The goal is to maintain adequate gas exchange while avoiding unnecessary distending pressure that can drive more gas through the leak. Reassess the patient, ventilator, airway, and chest drainage system together.

Air-leak ventilation framework

Confirm the leak → identify the source → minimize pressure injury → reassess

1. Confirm volume loss.

Your graphics source says the volume-time waveform should return to baseline after exhalation. Failure to return means some delivered volume did not come back through the expiratory limb.

2. Separate external from intrathoracic leak.

Source-listed causes include cuff leak, loose circuit connection, bronchopleural fistula, and chest-tube air leak.

3. Reassess the chest drainage system.

Persistent bubbling can indicate ongoing air leak depending on system and respiratory phase. Reassess chest tube setup and function rather than assuming the tube “fixed” the problem permanently.

4. Reduce excessive distending pressure.

Your air-leak case specifically says to use the least distending pressure that maintains acceptable gas exchange and warns that more pressure can worsen the leak.

Do not force the leak closed with more pressure.

The supplied source repeatedly warns that aggressive PIP/distending pressure can worsen an air leak. Support oxygenation and ventilation, but avoid unnecessary pressure while monitoring CO₂, oxygenation, pressures, exhaled volume and chest-tube behavior.

Source boundary: the retrieved source supports a pressure-limiting strategy but does not prescribe one universal VT, PEEP, inspiratory-time, or respiratory-rate formula for bronchopleural fistula. This lesson therefore teaches the supported principle—minimize unnecessary distending pressure while maintaining acceptable gas exchange—without inventing numeric targets.

Integrated RTE-style air-leak case

Keep gas exchange adequate without feeding the leak

Ventilated patient after pneumothorax drainage:
  • Chest tube is in place
  • Continuous bubbling persists
  • Volume-time waveform does not return to baseline
  • Delivered VT exceeds exhaled VT substantially
  • SpO₂ is acceptable
  • PaCO₂ is mildly elevated but stable
  • PIP was recently increased and bubbling became more vigorous
