Ventilating Restrictive & Low-Compliance Lungs

Stiff lungs require more pressure for the same volume. The safest strategy is lung-protective ventilation: use PBW-based lower tidal volumes, limit pressure exposure, apply PEEP intentionally for recruitment/oxygenation, and reassess hemodynamics every time support changes.

What changes when the lungs become stiff?

Low-compliance ventilation framework

Protect the lung while supporting oxygenation

1. Confirm low compliance.

Your source defines stiff lungs as needing more pressure to deliver the same volume. Static compliance falls in ARDS, pneumonia, pulmonary edema, atelectasis, and fibrotic disease.

2. Use lung-protective VT.

Your low-compliance source explicitly says to use lower, PBW-based tidal volumes rather than large breaths that increase stretch and pressure exposure.

3. Limit pressure exposure.

Plateau pressure reflects static/alveolar load during a no-flow hold. Use it with VT and PEEP to judge whether the lung is being overdistended.

4. Use PEEP intentionally.

PEEP may improve alveolar recruitment and oxygenation, but too much pressure can overdistend already-open units and worsen hemodynamics.

5. Reassess oxygenation and compliance together.

A better SpO₂ is not enough if plateau pressure rises sharply or static compliance worsens.

6. Reassess hemodynamic tolerance.

Your source repeatedly reminds learners that positive-pressure changes can reduce venous return and perfusion.

Stiff lungs are not “fixed” by forcing a normal tidal volume.

If pressure rises disproportionately, the safer response is to protect the lung and optimize recruitment/oxygenation rather than simply increasing volume.
