Positive Pressure, PEEP & Hemodynamic Effects

Positive-pressure ventilation changes pressure inside the chest as it moves air into the lungs. Positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) keeps some pressure between breaths. These settings can improve oxygenation, yet they can also change blood returning to the heart and the load on each ventricle. Read the oxygen and circulation trends together.

Did the ventilator change blood flow?

Heart–lung interaction framework

Chest pressure → filling and load → flow → oxygen delivery

Chest pressure and venous return

Positive pressure raises pressure inside the chest. This can reduce the pressure difference driving blood back to the right heart, lowering right-ventricular filling (preload) and cardiac output, especially when circulating volume is low.

Lung volume and right-heart load

PEEP may reopen collapsed lung areas and improve oxygenation. Excessive lung stretch can compress pulmonary vessels and increase the load against which the right ventricle pumps (afterload). Right-heart dysfunction makes this effect more consequential.

Left ventricle and circulation

Higher chest pressure can reduce the pressure the left ventricle must pump against. In some patients with left-sided heart failure and lung edema, positive pressure improves breathing and cardiac performance. The net effect depends on the patient.

Measure benefit and harm

After a pressure change, compare oxygen saturation, blood gases when available, blood pressure, heart rate, cardiac output, and signs of tissue perfusion. Also check lung inflation and the cause of deterioration before choosing the next adjustment.

Core rule: an oxygenation gain after increasing PEEP must be weighed against blood pressure and cardiac output. A lower output can reduce oxygen delivery even when oxygen saturation rises.
Pattern cue: low volume or right-ventricular failure increases the risk of a pressure-related fall in output. Positive pressure may help some patients with left-sided pulmonary edema. Avoid assuming that every PEEP increase has the same circulatory effect.
Reading the ventilator response

Review each concept

Mechanical inspiration increases pressure within the chest. That pressure affects the heart and major veins as well as the lungs.
PEEP maintains pressure at the end of exhalation. It may keep unstable air spaces open and improve oxygenation, but too much may overdistend lung regions.
Higher chest pressure can reduce venous return and right-ventricular preload. Blood pressure and output may fall when the heart has less blood to pump forward.
A patient with blood or fluid loss may rely heavily on venous return. Starting positive pressure or raising PEEP can then cause an abrupt fall in blood pressure.
Overdistended lung units can squeeze pulmonary vessels and raise right-ventricular afterload. A strained right ventricle may pump less blood through the lungs.
PEEP that recruits collapsed lung can improve oxygenation and sometimes ease pulmonary vessel constriction from low oxygen. Evaluate both lung response and circulation rather than judging the setting by a single value.
Positive chest pressure can reduce left-ventricular afterload. In left-sided heart failure with pulmonary edema, it may improve breathing and, in some cases, forward flow.
After a ventilator pressure increase, falling blood pressure can reflect reduced filling or greater right-heart load. Verify measurements and assess other causes of shock at the same time.
Oxygen delivery depends on blood oxygen content and cardiac output. Improved saturation with falling output is not enough to conclude that tissue oxygen delivery improved.
Trend oxygenation, pressure, output when measured, skin, mental status, urine output, and ventilator pressures. Report the tradeoff and work with the team on the safest setting.

Better saturation, falling output

A ventilated adult with right-heart strain has PEEP raised to improve low oxygen:
  • Oxygen saturation improves from 86% to 92%
  • Blood pressure falls from 106/68 to 82/48 mm Hg
  • Cardiac output falls from 4.8 to 3.5 L/min; skin becomes cool
