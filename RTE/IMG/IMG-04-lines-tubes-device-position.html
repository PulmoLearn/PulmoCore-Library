Lines, Tubes & Device Position

Every visible tube and line needs its own path and tip check. A device can appear on a chest radiograph yet end in the wrong place or create a new complication. Compare the image with the patient, recent procedure notes, and earlier films before it is used or adjusted.

Where does the device actually end?

Device-position framework

Identify → follow course → confirm tip → assess patient

Airway tubes

The endotracheal tube (ETT) tip should sit in the trachea above the carina, not enter a main bronchus or lie so high it may slip out. Check neck position and current depth as well as lung expansion. A tracheostomy tube should follow the tracheal airway; compare the image with ventilation and the stoma.

Enteric tubes

A nasogastric or feeding tube must follow the esophagus and cross the diaphragm toward the intended gastrointestinal location. Trace its entire visible course, tip, and any side port. A tube entering a bronchus or ending above the diaphragm must not be used for feeds or medication.

Vascular lines

Trace a central venous line from the insertion site toward the expected central vein and its tip. A sharp turn, unexpected vessel, deep position, or a new pneumothorax after placement requires prompt review. The appropriate tip position depends on device and purpose.

Chest drains and other devices

A chest tube intended to drain the pleura should have its drainage openings within the pleural space and no obvious kink. Judge function with the clinical exam and drainage system; tip direction alone does not prove failure. Inspect other visible catheters or devices for changes and complications.

Core rule: check every device after placement or an unexpected clinical change. If an enteric tube follows an airway path, stop use and alert the team; if ventilation or circulation worsens, assess the patient immediately while verifying the device.
Reading cue: seeing a tip below the diaphragm alone does not fully confirm enteric placement. Trace the full path and confirm the intended target according to local policy. A functioning chest drain with side holes inside the chest need not be moved solely because its tip points in a surprising direction.
Following every line and tube

Review each concept

Identify each visible ETT, tracheostomy tube, central line, enteric tube, chest tube, and other device. Compare device count with the chart so no line is overlooked.
Follow the ETT down the trachea to its tip above the carina. A tip in a main bronchus can cause one-sided ventilation and opposite-lung collapse; a high tip may risk dislodgement.
Head or neck movement can shift the ETT tip relative to the carina. Compare insertion depth, image, breath sounds, and ventilation after repositioning.
The tube should enter the tracheal airway rather than lie in soft tissue. If ventilation fails after a tracheostomy change, assess the patient, airway patency, and tube position promptly.
An enteric tube should follow the esophagus, cross the diaphragm, and reach the intended target. A tube tracking into a bronchus is unsafe for feeding or medication.
For a gastric tube, both the tip and side openings need to be below the gastroesophageal junction. A tip just below the diaphragm with a side hole in the esophagus may still be unsafe to use.
Trace the catheter from entry to its intended central venous tip. Unexpected looping or deviation warrants team review; also check for a new pleural air collection after insertion.
A drain should be in the intended pleural space with its side holes inside the thorax. A kink, retraction, worsening air or fluid, or poor drainage needs assessment; tip direction alone is insufficient.
Compare pre- and post-placement images for new pneumothorax, collapse, bleeding, or other change. Match radiographic findings with breath sounds and blood pressure.
State device type, unusual path or tip, patient symptoms, and any interval imaging change. Hold unsafe use, notify the appropriate team, and confirm position before the device is used.
