Brain-Death Apnea Testing

Apnea testing is one part of determining brain death, also called death by neurologic criteria (BD/DNC). A trained, authorized clinician leads the determination; the respiratory therapist helps prepare oxygenation and ventilation, records arterial blood gas (ABG) data, watches for breaths and instability, and restores support when the test ends. Follow the current institutional protocol and applicable law.

Can the apnea test proceed safely?

Apnea-testing framework

Prerequisites → safe stimulus → observed effort → blood gas

Ready before testing

The determining clinician confirms catastrophic permanent brain injury and excludes confounders such as hypothermia, drug effects, paralysis, and major metabolic disturbances. Confirm adequate blood pressure, oxygenation, volume status, and the baseline ABG; defer if safety prerequisites are unmet.

Preoxygenate and provide oxygen

The 2023 consensus guideline calls for 100% oxygen for at least 10 minutes with a target arterial oxygen pressure (PaO2) above 200 mm Hg before testing. The clinician chooses a protocol-approved method of apneic oxygenation, such as continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), while mandatory breaths are stopped.

Watch for a true breath and instability

Observe the chest and abdomen for spontaneous respiratory effort, not just ventilator-triggered numbers. Continuously watch oxygen saturation, blood pressure, and rhythm. Stop for any breath or for guideline-defined unsafe hypoxemia, hypotension, or unstable arrhythmia; promptly restore ventilation.

Apply all completion criteria

For a patient without known chronic hypercarbia, an apnea response consistent with BD/DNC requires no breaths, arterial carbon dioxide pressure (PaCO2) at least 60 mm Hg and at least 20 mm Hg over the pretest baseline, and arterial pH below 7.30. A known chronic elevated baseline changes the comparison; unknown chronic baseline may require ancillary testing.

Core rule: no observed breaths alone is insufficient. The target PaCO2 rise and acidemia must be documented, and the apnea test is only one component of the full BD/DNC evaluation.
Safety cue: an unsafe or incomplete test is not a positive test. Restore ventilation, document what occurred, and defer further evaluation to the authorized team and institution’s current protocol.
Preparation, observation, and interpretation

Review each concept

The authorized clinician determines BD/DNC. The therapist helps check ventilator and oxygen setup, gathers baseline and final ABGs, observes respiratory effort and physiologic trends, and acts with the team if the test must stop.
A known catastrophic permanent brain injury must be established, and the team must address drugs, neuromuscular blockers, hypothermia, and severe metabolic problems that could make the examination unreliable.
Do not begin in a patient with unresolved hypotension or hypovolemia. For adults, the 2023 guidance uses systolic pressure at least 100 mm Hg and mean arterial pressure at least 75 mm Hg, accounting for a substantially different chronic baseline.
Normally target a pretest PaCO2 of 35–45 mm Hg and pH 7.35–7.45. In known chronic hypercarbia, use the known chronic PaCO2 baseline or the protocol-directed estimate, and tell the team if the chronic baseline is unknown.
Provide 100% oxygen for at least 10 minutes and document adequate oxygenation; the guideline targets PaO2 above 200 mm Hg. Confirm the chosen oxygen-delivery method and a rapid way to restart ventilation.
The team may use an approved CPAP-based approach or an adult oxygen insufflation catheter. The point is to supply oxygen while observing for a spontaneous breath as carbon dioxide rises. Technique and catheter size must match protocol.
Look for chest or abdominal respiratory effort. A ventilator alarm or apparent trigger alone may be artifact, but any actual spontaneous breath means the patient does not meet BD/DNC criteria and the test must stop.
With no breath, PaCO2 must reach at least 60 mm Hg and rise at least 20 mm Hg above the appropriate baseline, with arterial pH below 7.30. A value exactly 7.30 does not meet the pH threshold.
Stop and restore support for a breath, progressive oxygen saturation below 85%, adult systolic pressure below 100 mm Hg or mean below 75 mm Hg despite support, or an arrhythmia with instability. Follow pediatric age-specific thresholds when applicable.
If instability forces termination before targets are reached, report an incomplete test rather than a determination. The authorized clinician decides whether safe repeat testing or a guideline-accepted ancillary test is needed; unknown chronic PaCO2 baseline has special implications.
