Home PAP, Aerosol & Airway-Clearance Equipment

Successful home therapy depends on a setup the patient can use every day. Positive airway pressure (PAP) supports breathing during sleep. A nebulizer turns prescribed medicine into an inhaled mist. Airway-clearance devices help move mucus. For each one, check setup, fit, cleaning, maintenance, barriers to regular use, and what the patient or caregiver can demonstrate.

Home equipment teaching framework

Demonstrate → observe → troubleshoot → repeat

Positive airway pressure

Connect the PAP device, tubing, and mask as prescribed. Check mask comfort and unintended leak while the machine is running. Ask about dry mouth, nasal symptoms, pressure discomfort, and sleep routine. Use the device's care instructions and follow up on use data when available.

Nebulized aerosol therapy

Confirm the medicine, dose, device, and treatment schedule. Show how to place medicine in the cup, attach the prescribed mouthpiece or mask, and breathe through treatment. Clean the parts that contact medicine and breath after use as directed, then allow them to dry before storage.

Airway clearance

For a prescribed positive expiratory pressure (PEP) device or chest-wall oscillation vest, show the correct fit and the ordered breathing or treatment sequence. Ask the patient to demonstrate, including any instructed huff cough to move loosened mucus. Clean and maintain the equipment according to its instructions.

Maintenance and adherence

Check worn masks, tubing, filters, cups, valves, and batteries against the device-specific replacement plan. Ask what prevents regular use and solve the practical cause. Do not change prescribed pressures, medicine doses, or clearance settings without the care plan.

Core teaching rule: use the actual device, have the patient or caregiver demonstrate the process, correct missed steps, and repeat the demonstration.
Cleaning caution: follow manufacturer and disease-specific instructions. Do not substitute an ozone or ultraviolet PAP cleaner for the cleaning method recommended by the device maker.
Home device checkpoints

Review each concept

Confirm the prescribed device mode and interface, connect tubing and humidifier if used, then run the device. Show how to start therapy and identify the normal display and alarm messages. PAP means positive airway pressure.
Seat the mask while PAP is running. A comfortable seal matters; do not overtighten headgear to compensate for an incorrect size. Ask about air blowing into the eyes, noise, skin pressure, and dry mouth.
Ask when and why PAP comes off, then target the cause: mask fit, dryness, congestion, pressure discomfort, or routine. Review use reports if available and arrange supplier or clinician follow-up when needed.
Teach cleaning of the mask, tubing, and humidifier chamber as directed by their makers. Use the recommended water and drying method. Avoid unapproved ozone or ultraviolet cleaning products.
Verify the prescribed medicine and dose. Assemble the cup, tubing, compressor or other power source, and mouthpiece or mask for that device. Check for mist and proper fit without adding unprescribed medicine.
Watch the patient breathe through the mouthpiece or mask as instructed for the full treatment. If little mist appears, inspect connections, power, and the medicine cup rather than assuming the dose was delivered.
Wash reusable medication cups and patient interfaces after each use according to the instructions; disinfect when required by the device and care plan. Let parts dry fully before storing. Follow any stricter infection-control plan for the patient's disease.
For prescribed PEP or oscillating PEP, watch the exhalation technique and the instructed huff cough. For a vest, check fit, session settings, and how the patient clears loosened mucus. Follow the prescribed sequence.
Show where the patient finds filter, mask, tubing, cup, valve, and battery instructions. Replace worn or damaged parts on the device-specific schedule and contact the supplier for service rather than improvising repairs.
Ask the patient or caregiver to demonstrate setup, treatment, cleaning, and one likely troubleshooting step. Correct gaps, repeat the demonstration, and document remaining barriers and follow-up.
Integrated RTE-style home-care case

Find the cause of missed therapy

A patient reports using PAP for only one hour nightly and missing nebulizer treatments:
  • Air leaks toward the eyes and the mask leaves painful marks
  • The nebulizer cup is stored damp and produces little mist
  • The patient says a friend recommended an ozone cleaner
  • The patient wants help fitting treatment into a normal day
