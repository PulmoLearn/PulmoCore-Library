Core framework

HFNC has two major knobs: flow and FiO₂

Do not treat them as if they do the same thing.

Flow mainly changes supportHigher flow better meets inspiratory demand, reduces room-air entrainment, can wash out nasopharyngeal dead space, and may reduce work of breathing. It can also create a small amount of distending pressure, but it is not the same as set CPAP.
FiO₂ mainly changes oxygen concentrationIncrease FiO₂ when oxygenation remains below target. Once oxygenation improves, reduce excessive FiO₂ while maintaining adequate support.
Heat and humidity are essentialHigh gas flows must be actively heated and humidified. Loss of humidification can cause discomfort, mucosal drying, and poor tolerance.
Cannula fit mattersThe prongs should fit comfortably without completely occluding the nares. The system depends on an open nasal interface rather than a sealed pressure mask.
Fast exam rule

Hypoxemia problem? Think FiO₂. High inspiratory demand / tachypnea / work of breathing? Think flow—while reassessing whether HFNC is still enough support.

Why HFNC works

Know the mechanism behind the bedside response

0 of 6 reviewedReview all concepts.
  • A tachypneic patient may draw inspiratory flow well above what a conventional low-flow cannula supplies.
  • High total flow better matches demand
  • Less uncontrolled room-air entrainment
  • Delivered FiO₂ is more stable
A device can deliver a set FiO₂ only if total flow is adequate for the patient’s demand.
  • Continuous high flow flushes exhaled gas from the upper airway
  • May improve ventilatory efficiency by reducing rebreathing of CO₂-rich gas
  • Respiratory rate and effort may decrease when support is effective
HFNC is not a substitute for NIV or invasive ventilation when ventilation is failing.
  • High flow through the nasal interface may generate a modest positive pharyngeal pressure
  • The amount is variable and affected by flow, cannula fit, mouth position, and patient anatomy
  • You cannot prescribe a specific CPAP level by simply setting HFNC flow
If the patient needs reliable positive airway pressure, choose an actual CPAP/NIV strategy rather than assuming HFNC provides the same support.
  • Improves comfort
  • Reduces mucosal drying
  • Supports secretion mobilization and mucociliary function
  • Check water supply/chamber
  • Check heater function and temperature
  • Inspect circuit for excessive condensate or disconnection
Heated high-flow appears in both device troubleshooting and support-of-oxygenation sections of the 2027 outline.
  • Hypoxemia persists on conventional oxygen
  • Inspiratory demand is high
  • Patient has increased work of breathing but is still protecting the airway and participating
  • Worsening mental status
  • Progressive fatigue
  • Rising CO₂/acidemia when ventilation is failing
  • Persistent severe hypoxemia despite increasing support
O2-05 owns the full escalation framework to NIV or invasive support.
  • SpO₂ reaches target
  • Respiratory rate falls
  • Work of breathing improves
  • Heart rate and distress improve
  • Patient is comfortable and alert
  • Escalating flow/FiO₂ requirement
  • Persistent tachypnea/distress
  • New fatigue or mental-status change
  • Oxygenation remains inadequate
Do not call HFNC successful just because SpO₂ rises if the patient is tiring, becoming more tachypneic, or deteriorating clinically.

Recognize HFNC failure

Do not let a device delay needed escalation

Scenario: Despite increasing HFNC support, the patient has worsening tachypnea, increasing accessory-muscle use, new somnolence, and persistent hypoxemia.

Find the setup error before changing therapy

Patient:
  • HFNC ordered for heated high-flow oxygen
  • Flow and FiO₂ are set as ordered
  • SpO₂ is falling
  • Cannula is displaced from the nares
  • Water chamber is full and heater is functioning
