Transport Oxygen, Cylinders & Power Planning

A transport plan must cover the trip, delays, and a protected reserve. Read the cylinder gauge, subtract the reserve pressure, convert usable pressure to liters with the correct cylinder factor, and divide by total gas use per minute. Check battery runtime and backup supply separately. If either runs short, change the plan before moving the patient.

Will the supply last the trip?

Transport resource framework

Pressure → liters → minutes → safe plan

Calculate usable gas

Read the gauge in pounds per square inch (psi). Subtract the chosen residual pressure reserve. Multiply the remaining pressure by the cylinder factor, measured in liters per psi. Use the factor for the actual cylinder model and local reference; a commonly taught E-cylinder factor is 0.28 L/psi.

Convert to time

Usable duration in minutes = (gauge psi − protected reserve psi) × cylinder factor ÷ total oxygen use in liters per minute (L/min). Example: an E cylinder at 1,800 psi with a 200-psi reserve has (1,800 − 200) × 0.28 = 448 usable liters; at 8 L/min, that is about 56 minutes.

Compare against the whole trip

Include setup, travel, procedure, return, handoff, and plausible delays. Use the equipment's actual oxygen use. A transport ventilator may consume oxygen for its operation as well as for patient delivery; follow its manual or measured consumption rather than assuming it equals a simple mask flow.

Protect power and backup

Confirm battery runtime for the ventilator, monitor, and pumps under current settings. Bring an appropriate backup oxygen source and manual ventilation equipment. If estimated oxygen or power time is shorter than the planned interval plus contingency, obtain more supply or change the transport plan.

Core calculation: usable minutes = [(gauge psi − reserve psi) × cylinder factor] ÷ total gas use in L/min. Check oxygen and power independently.
Planning limit: duration is an estimate. Rising patient demand, leaks, ventilator drive gas, and delays can shorten actual runtime; recheck gauge and battery during transport.
Usable time & backup decisions

Review each concept

The gauge shows pressure remaining now. Use that reading, not a nominal full-cylinder capacity, to estimate available gas. Recheck the valve, regulator, and pressure before leaving.
The factor converts gauge pressure to approximate liters of oxygen for a specific cylinder. A common training value for an E cylinder is 0.28 L/psi; confirm the factor for the cylinder in use.
Subtract a stated reserve pressure before calculating usable liters. The example in this lesson protects 200 psi; follow facility and device requirements when they call for a different minimum. Do not plan to use the protected gas.
At 1,800 psi with a 200-psi reserve, an E cylinder provides (1,800 − 200) × 0.28 = 448 usable liters by the example factor. Keep units visible: psi × L/psi = liters.
At 8 L/min, 448 usable liters lasts about 56 minutes. For a device with several oxygen demands, use its verified total gas consumption, not just a displayed patient flow.
Include departure, travel, the procedure, return, reconnection, and a plausible delay. A 35-minute procedure may require far more than 35 minutes of oxygen supply.
Higher flow shortens duration in inverse proportion if the gas supply is unchanged. Circuit leaks and a pneumatically driven ventilator may increase consumption; use the device instructions and a conservative estimate.
An oxygen cylinder cannot keep an electric ventilator, monitor, or infusion pump running after battery power is exhausted. Confirm charge, expected runtime, and backup power for each essential device.
A spare oxygen source, working regulator, and manual resuscitation bag can protect against supply or device failure. Verify that the backup can connect to the patient's equipment and is within reach.
If usable oxygen or battery time is less than the trip plus contingency, delay a nonurgent trip and obtain enough supply. For urgent movement, mobilize extra oxygen, power, staff, and a rescue plan while treatment continues.
Integrated RTE-style transport case

Decide whether the supply is enough

Patient needs 10 L/min for a nonurgent scan:
  • E cylinder reads 1,800 psi; use factor 0.28 L/psi
  • Protect a residual 200 psi in the cylinder
  • Estimated time away is 30 minutes plus a 20-minute delay allowance
  • Portable monitor and ventilator batteries each have at least 75 minutes
