Alarm Settings, False Alarms & Safe Limits

Alarm limits should be close enough to detect a clinically meaningful change, but not so tight that every normal fluctuation creates a nuisance alarm. Start from the patient’s current stable values, individualize by mode and condition, and reset alarms whenever support or patient needs change.

High pressure
Practical board-style start: about PIP + 10 cmH₂O

Your source says this should detect a meaningful rise without alarming on every normal breath-to-breath change.

Low pressure / disconnect
Practical start: about 5–10 cmH₂O below observed PIP

Keep it high enough to detect a major leak or disconnection.

Low exhaled VT
Board-style start: about 10% below expected/set VT

Useful when appropriate for the selected mode to detect leak, inadequate breaths, or lost delivered volume.

High exhaled VT
Learning start: about 10–15% above expected VT

Your source notes this is especially useful in pressure-targeted or spontaneous modes where VT can vary.

High/low minute ventilation
Common practical start: about ±10–15% around expected baseline V̇E

Especially useful when spontaneous rate or VT changes may signal deterioration.

Low PEEP / CPAP
Common textbook start: about 2–4 cmH₂O below set baseline pressure

Helps detect leak or failure to maintain baseline pressure.

These are starting points—not universal standards.

Your source explicitly distinguishes textbook/board-style rules from current-practice guidance: the guideline requires alarm assessment and documentation, but does not prescribe one universal numeric formula. Individualize to the patient, mode, ventilator, and facility policy.
