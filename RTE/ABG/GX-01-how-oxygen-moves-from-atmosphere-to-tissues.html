The oxygen pathway

Think step-by-step instead of “low oxygen = give oxygen”

Oxygenation can fail at several different points, and the treatment depends on where the problem is.

1. Inspired oxygenEnough oxygen must reach the patient’s airway. Low inspired oxygen or inadequate oxygen delivery can limit the entire pathway from the start.
2. Alveolar ventilationFresh gas must reach the alveoli. Severe hypoventilation can lower alveolar oxygen and raise PaCO₂.
3. Gas exchangeOxygen must move from alveoli across the alveolar-capillary membrane into pulmonary capillary blood.
4. Hemoglobin loadingOnce in blood, most oxygen must bind to hemoglobin. A normal PaO₂ does not guarantee normal oxygen content if hemoglobin is low or abnormal.
5. Circulatory deliveryOxygenated blood must be pumped forward. Low cardiac output can reduce oxygen delivery even when saturation looks acceptable.
6. Tissue delivery/useTissues must actually receive enough oxygen for metabolic demand. Delivery depends on both oxygen content and blood flow.
Fast mental model

Get O₂ in → get it to the alveoli → move it into blood → load hemoglobin → move the blood → deliver it to tissues.
