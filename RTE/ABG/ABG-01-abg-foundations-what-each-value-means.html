Core reference

Know the normal ranges and the job of each value

This lesson teaches the individual values. Full acid-base classification comes in ABG-02.

ValueTypical adult arterial rangeWhat it tells you
pH7.35–7.45Overall acidity or alkalinity of the blood.
PaCO₂35–45 mmHgVentilation. CO₂ rises when alveolar ventilation falls and falls when alveolar ventilation increases.
HCO₃⁻22–26 mEq/LMajor metabolic/renal buffer component.
PaO₂80–100 mmHgPartial pressure of oxygen dissolved in arterial plasma; used to assess arterial oxygenation.
SaO₂≈95–100%Percentage of hemoglobin binding sites occupied by oxygen.
Base excess−2 to +2 mEq/LDirection and magnitude of the metabolic acid/base contribution; negative suggests base deficit, positive suggests excess base.
Fast mental map

pH = overall direction. PaCO₂ = ventilation. HCO₃⁻ and base excess = metabolic side. PaO₂ and SaO₂ = oxygenation—but they describe different things.

Quick recognition

Know the clue that identifies each value

Open each value once to continue.

0 of 6 reviewedReview all values.
  • < 7.35 = acidemia
  • > 7.45 = alkalemia
  • pH tells you the overall direction, not the cause.
Do not fully classify the disorder here. ABG-02 teaches respiratory vs metabolic disorders and compensation.
  • High PaCO₂ = hypoventilation relative to CO₂ production
  • Low PaCO₂ = increased alveolar ventilation
  • Think “CO₂ clearance.”
  • PaCO₂ moves inversely with alveolar ventilation.
If the question asks whether the patient is ventilating, look first at PaCO₂.
  • Low bicarbonate = less base
  • High bicarbonate = more base
  • Think “metabolic side.”
HCO₃⁻ helps identify the metabolic contribution. Compensation patterns come later.
  • Measured in mmHg
  • Reflects oxygen dissolved in arterial plasma
  • Think “oxygen pressure,” not hemoglobin percentage.
PaO₂ and SaO₂ are related but not interchangeable. PaO₂ is pressure; SaO₂ is hemoglobin saturation.
  • Reported as a percentage
  • Represents oxygen-bound hemoglobin sites
  • Think “percent of hemoglobin sites occupied.”
This does not tell you total oxygen content by itself. Hemoglobin concentration and dyshemoglobins are handled in later lessons.
  • Negative = base deficit direction
  • Positive = excess-base direction
  • Think “metabolic magnitude.”
Use base excess as another clue to the metabolic component. Full mixed-disorder interpretation belongs in ABG-02.
