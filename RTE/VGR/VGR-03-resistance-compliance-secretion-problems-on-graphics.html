Resistance, Compliance & Secretion Problems on Graphics

Pressure trends tell you where to look. If peak pressure rises while plateau stays similar, think increased resistance. If both peak and plateau rise, think reduced compliance. Then use breath sounds, suction catheter passage, circuit inspection, chest symmetry, hemodynamics, and imaging to identify the actual cause.

Resistance or compliance?

Pressure-trend framework

Use PIP and Pplat together

Resistance pattern

PIP rises while Pplat remains similar. Your source says to assess for secretions, bronchospasm, tube obstruction, biting, kinked tubing, water, or a narrowed artificial airway.

Compliance pattern

PIP and Pplat both rise. Your source lists ARDS, edema, atelectasis, pneumothorax, abdominal distention, positioning, or reduced chest-wall compliance.

Plateau pressure must be measured correctly

Use an inspiratory pause/hold to obtain the no-flow pressure needed to distinguish static load from resistive load.

Pattern narrows the differential—it does not finish it

Your source explicitly says breath sounds, suction catheter passage, chest symmetry, hemodynamics, and imaging determine the cause.

Never treat the number alone.

A sudden pressure rise is a signal to assess the patient and ventilator. The same pressure pattern can come from several causes that require different interventions.

Visual pressure comparison

Three patterns to recognize immediately

Baseline / expected

PIP and plateau are near the patient's usual trend.

Resistance problem

PIP rises; plateau stays near prior value → larger resistive pressure gap.

Compliance problem

PIP and plateau rise together → increased static/elastic pressure requirement.
