Flow & Cycling Dyssynchrony

Once the breath is triggered, the ventilator still has to match the patient through inspiration and end the breath at an appropriate time. The registry specifically requires recognition of flow starvation, double triggering, breath stacking, and premature or late cycling.

Primary: III.C.4 Recognize & correct patient-ventilator dyssynchronyNo shared mapping in current crosswalk
Flow mismatchThe patient wants inspiratory flow the ventilator is not delivering.
Double triggeringTwo closely linked assisted breaths occur during one continuing patient demand.
Premature cyclingThe ventilator ends inspiration before the patient is finished.
Late cyclingVentilator inspiration continues after the patient is ready to exhale.

Dyssynchrony framework

Trigger → flow delivery → cycle → exhalation

Flow starvation

Your current mechanical-ventilation source directly supports this pattern: patient inspiratory demand exceeds delivered inspiratory flow. A concave/scooped pressure contour and increased patient effort are used as clues.

Double triggering / breath stacking

The registry explicitly requires recognition of these patterns. In the available equipment registry they also appear as assessed dyssynchrony topics.

Premature cycling

The registry requires recognition of the ventilator ending inspiration too early relative to patient demand.

Late cycling

The registry requires recognition of inspiratory support continuing too long relative to the patient’s effort.

Correct the mismatch that is actually present.

Do not respond to every dyssynchrony by increasing sedation or changing multiple settings at once. Determine whether the problem is flow delivery, repeated triggering/breath stacking, or inspiratory timing, make the targeted change, and reassess the patient and graphics.

Source boundary: the authoring registry explicitly names premature and late cycling as required VGR-05 content, but the retrieved detailed PulmoLearn source material does not provide device-specific cycling-percentage thresholds. This lesson therefore teaches recognition and direction of correction without inventing manufacturer-specific numeric targets.
Pattern reference

Three high-yield graphics ideas

Flow starvation clue

Conceptual rendering of the source-supported concave/scooped inspiratory pressure contour when demand exceeds delivered flow.

Double-trigger / stacked-breath clue

Two breaths occur with little expiratory separation. The registry requires recognition of double triggering and breath stacking.

Cycling mismatch concept

Conceptual comparison: patient demand and ventilator inspiratory timing should end together. A mismatch produces premature or late cycling.

Find the mismatch before changing the ventilator

Ventilated patient:
  • Patient is awake and making strong inspiratory efforts
  • Inspiratory pressure contour shows repeated concavity
  • Two assisted breaths occasionally occur back-to-back with minimal expiratory separation
  • No circuit leak or condensation is present
  • Expiratory flow returns to baseline between ordinary breaths
