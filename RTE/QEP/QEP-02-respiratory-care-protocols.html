Respiratory Care Protocols

An approved respiratory therapist (RT)-driven protocol can authorize defined assessments, treatment adjustments, and reassessments without a new order for each step. Oxygen, aerosol, airway-clearance, and ventilator-liberation pathways share one safety pattern: confirm the patient meets entry criteria, act only within authorized limits, measure the response, and escalate when the patient worsens or leaves the pathway.

RT-driven protocol framework

Confirm entry → act within limits → record response → escalate when needed

Start with authorization

An RT-driven protocol has a defined indication, order or authorization, inclusion and exclusion criteria, allowed actions, monitoring, and escalation triggers. Verify the current version and your role before changing therapy.

Oxygen and aerosol pathways

An oxygen protocol may allow titration toward an ordered saturation target while tracking work of breathing and delivered support. An aerosol protocol may guide a bronchodilator trial when indicated; document breath sounds, symptoms, vital signs, response, and adverse effects.

Airway clearance and liberation

Airway-clearance plans should match secretion burden and ability to clear it, then be reassessed for benefit. A ventilator-liberation pathway screens readiness, performs a spontaneous breathing trial (SBT) when appropriate, and defines intolerance and stop criteria.

Escalation is part of the protocol

A change beyond authorized limits, falling oxygenation, rising distress, mental-status change, hemodynamic instability, or SBT intolerance calls for immediate assessment and team communication. Do not keep repeating a step that is failing.

Core rule: protocol authority is bounded by its eligibility, permitted steps, and stop criteria. Worsening physiology needs clinical reassessment and escalation, not automatic continuation.
Practice cue: exact oxygen targets, adjustment ranges, bronchodilator frequency, clearance techniques, and SBT failure thresholds vary by approved local protocol and patient population. Use the active plan rather than memorizing one universal number.
Four pathways, one safety cycle

Review each concept

Find the approved protocol, required order, patient group, and therapist-authorized actions. A current order for “per RT protocol” does not allow treatment outside the protocol’s defined scope.
Before adjusting therapy, assess oxygenation, respiratory rate and effort, breath sounds, secretions, vital signs, and goals relevant to the pathway. A comparable baseline makes the later response meaningful.
Titrate within the prescribed target and protocol range, then reassess SpO₂, work of breathing, device, and patient status. Increased oxygen need or distress may require a clinician review even if SpO₂ briefly improves.
Verify indication, medicine order, previous response, contraindications, and appropriate device. After delivery, compare symptoms, airflow or breath sounds, and adverse effects; do not give repeated treatments without benefit assessment.
Consider secretion burden, cough effectiveness, contraindications, and patient tolerance. Choose a permitted technique, assess sputum clearance and breathing, and adjust or stop based on response.
In a ventilated patient, use the local readiness screen before an SBT. Assess oxygenation, hemodynamics, alertness, and cause improvement according to protocol; do not force a trial in a patient who is unstable.
A patient who develops marked distress, worsening gas exchange, or instability during an SBT needs the trial stopped under protocol, support restored, and the team notified. Reassess why the trial was not tolerated.
If needed therapy exceeds the authorized oxygen range, medication frequency, or ventilator change, contact the responsible clinician and follow emergency escalation procedures. A protocol is not an unlimited standing order.
Record entry criteria, baseline, protocol step, delivered therapy, measured response, adverse findings, and any clinician communication. This supports continuity and shows why a change was made.
If treatment is ineffective or the clinical picture changes, review the diagnosis, adherence, technique, and current goals. Continue, modify, discontinue, or escalate only within the approved process.

Beyond the oxygen protocol

An adult is being managed under an authorized oxygen-titration protocol:
  • SpO₂ falls below the ordered target despite the highest support allowed by the pathway
  • Breathing effort is increasing and the patient is newly drowsy
  • The current protocol requires clinician notification at its limit
  • Additional support may be needed now
