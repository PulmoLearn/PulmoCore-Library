Spontaneous Breathing Trials

A spontaneous breathing trial (SBT) tests whether a patient who has passed the readiness screen can tolerate a period of substantially reduced ventilatory assistance. The RTE task is to choose an appropriate trial method, gather the right observations during the trial, and determine whether the response supports continued liberation or a return to support.

SBT framework

Method → monitoring → interpretation

Choose the method.

Your PulmoLearn equipment registry specifically includes T-piece, tracheostomy collar, CPAP, and pressure-support trial methods.

Reduce assistance enough to test spontaneous breathing.

The purpose is to observe whether the patient can sustain breathing with little or no ventilatory assistance, depending on the method and protocol.

Monitor the patient continuously.

Observe breathing pattern, work of breathing, respiratory rate trend, oxygenation, heart rate/blood pressure, comfort, mental status, and overall clinical appearance.

Interpret the response.

Stable or improving findings support continued liberation. Increasing distress, deteriorating gas exchange, instability, fatigue, or mental-status change indicates intolerance and should prompt return to support and reassessment.

An SBT is a clinical stress test—not a single-number test.

LIB-03 will teach RSBI, VC, MIP/NIF, VE, VT, and respiratory rate as supporting data. LIB-02 focuses on conducting the trial and recognizing tolerance or failure.

Source boundary: the retrieved PulmoLearn sources name the common SBT methods and require stop-criteria practice, but they do not provide one universal trial duration, pressure-support level, CPAP level, or numeric stop threshold. This lesson therefore teaches procedure logic and clinical intolerance without inventing universal numbers.

Review each topic

These methods allow spontaneous breathing without ventilator inspiratory pressure assistance. The patient still receives the ordered oxygen through the trial setup. Use the facility protocol and monitor the full clinical response.
The patient breathes spontaneously while a continuous baseline pressure is maintained. The purpose is still to evaluate whether spontaneous breathing can be sustained with minimal ventilator assistance.
The patient initiates spontaneous breaths and receives limited inspiratory assistance. The registry includes pressure-support trial selection, but the retrieved source does not define one universal pressure-support setting.
Before and during the trial, gather method/setup, breathing pattern, RR trend, work of breathing, oxygenation, hemodynamics, comfort, mental status, and any protocol-specific measurements.
A tolerated trial shows a stable overall trajectory: spontaneous breathing is sustained without marked distress, oxygenation and hemodynamics remain acceptable, mental status is maintained, and work of breathing does not progressively worsen.
Progressive tachypnea or visibly worsening breathing pattern, increased accessory-muscle use/distress, deteriorating oxygenation, hemodynamic instability, fatigue, diaphoresis, anxiety/altered mental status, or inability to sustain adequate spontaneous ventilation are clinical failure clues.
Do not call a trial successful because one value remains acceptable. Integrate the whole response. Likewise, a failed trial does not mean permanent ventilator dependence; it identifies unresolved causes that should be treated before another trial.
If the patient tolerates the SBT, proceed to the next liberation/extubation-readiness decision. If the patient does not tolerate it, restore appropriate support, identify the reason for failure, correct reversible problems, and reassess later.
