TherapyWhat the source says it doesHigh-yield safety/use point
Acetylcysteine (Mucomyst)Breaks disulfide bonds in mucoproteins, reducing mucus viscosity.Direct airway irritation can cause bronchospasm; source recommends having a SABA available and considering pretreatment. Strong sulfur/“rotten egg” odor is expected.
Dornase alfa (Pulmozyme)Recombinant DNase that breaks down extracellular DNA from dead neutrophils in CF mucus.CF-specific in the source; delivered by nebulizer and supports secretion mobilization.

Source indication: thick, retained secretions; source examples include COPD, CF, and retained-secretion complications.
Mechanism: breaks disulfide bonds in mucoproteins → lower mucus viscosity.
Safety clue: reactive-airway patients may bronchospasm. Have albuterol available, consider SABA pretreatment, monitor breath sounds, SpO₂, and work of breathing.
The source knowledge check says to stop acetylcysteine if bronchospasm occurs and administer the SABA.
Increasing nebulizer flow does not remove the drug’s airway-irritation risk.
Breaks extracellular DNA released from dead neutrophils—a major contributor to CF mucus viscosity.
Source effects: reduces mucus viscosity, supports mucociliary clearance, and decreases infection frequency in CF.
Boundary: it is not a bronchodilator and the source does not use it for asthma or routine non-CF disease.
The CF lesson describes hypertonic saline as a secretion-hydrating therapy.
It can provoke bronchospasm, so tolerance must be assessed rather than assuming “more saline is always better.”
RT goal: improve secretion mobilization, then evaluate cough/clearance response.
The source says exact sequence varies by order set and patient plan.
Its common logic is: bronchodilator when ordered → secretion-hydrating/mucolytic therapy → airway clearance → therapies such as inhaled antibiotics that should remain in the airway afterward when appropriate.
Do not memorize a rigid universal order. Use the prescribed plan and the physiologic purpose of each treatment.
The CF source defines CFTR modulators as medications that improve CFTR protein function for patients with eligible genetic variants.
The 2027 NBRC example is elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (Trikafta).
Exam distinction: CFTR modulators treat the underlying CFTR dysfunction; dornase/hypertonic therapy manage airway secretions.
CF source priorities include sputum amount/quality, cough effectiveness, breath sounds/air entry, respiratory rate, work of breathing, SpO₂, treatment tolerance, and oxygen need.
Poor response to intensified airway clearance should trigger provider reassessment/escalation.
Primary PHARM-04 task: choose/administer the therapy, then decide whether response or adverse effects require a care-plan change.
