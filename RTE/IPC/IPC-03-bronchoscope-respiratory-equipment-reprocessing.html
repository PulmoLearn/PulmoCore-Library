Bronchoscope & Respiratory Equipment Reprocessing

A flexible bronchoscope has narrow channels that can retain soil and moisture. Use the device-specific instructions from point of use through leak testing, manual cleaning, high-level disinfection or sterilization, rinsing, drying, and protected storage. Verify that every channel was treated, document each cycle, and stop a device with a failed check from reaching a patient.

Bronchoscope reprocessing framework

Point-of-use care → leak test → clean → disinfect → rinse and dry → release

Protect and inspect

At the point of use, keep soil from drying as directed and contain the used scope for transport. In the processing area, perform the manufacturer's leak test if required before immersion or manual cleaning. A failed test means remove the scope from service and arrange repair.

Clean every surface and channel

Disassemble removable parts as instructed. Manually clean outer surfaces and brush and flush all accessible channels with compatible supplies. High-level disinfection cannot make up for skipped manual cleaning.

Deliver the full process

Use a validated high-level disinfection or sterilization method for this device. When an automated endoscope reprocessor is used, connect each required channel correctly; confirm chemical contact and full cycle completion. Do not release a scope after an alarm or disconnected channel.

Finish and trace

Rinse and dry the scope and internal channels by the approved instructions. Store clean equipment protected from recontamination and damage. Record scope identification, processing cycle, checks, and patient or procedure linkage as policy requires.

Stop rule: a failed leak test, uncleared visible soil, missing channel connection, failed cycle, or incomplete drying/storage step means do not release the scope. Correct the problem and repeat the required validated steps.
Respiratory device cue: not every respiratory item is a bronchoscope. Follow the classification and manufacturer instructions for the exact reusable component; single-use items are not reprocessed for reuse unless specifically authorized by an approved program.
Device-specific processing safeguards

Review each concept

Perform initial care as the instructions direct so secretions do not dry in channels. Transport the used scope in a designated, contained route separate from clean equipment.
Follow the specific scope's leak-test instructions before immersion and manual cleaning. Remove a failed scope from clinical use; sending a damaged scope into liquid processing can harm it and leave channels unprocessed.
Manually clean external surfaces and every accessible channel with the correct brush and solution. Check removable valves and accessories against their own instructions. Flushing only the outside leaves internal soil behind.
A bronchoscope contacting mucous membranes needs at least high-level disinfection after cleaning, unless the device and procedure require sterilization. Follow compatible device and chemical instructions for full exposure time.
In an automated endoscope reprocessor, attach all required channel connectors correctly. If a channel is not perfused, the cycle cannot be assumed to disinfect the complete scope even if the outside looks clean.
After chemical processing, flush channels with the approved water and method to remove disinfectant. Follow the device and facility instructions rather than guessing a rinse volume or skipping the step.
Dry external surfaces and channels using the validated method. Trapped moisture supports microbial growth; store the processed device in a way that protects it from recontamination and damage.
Maintain the facility's scope identifier, processing record, cycle results, and patient or procedure linkage. These records allow the team to identify affected patients and items if a processing failure is discovered.
Quarantine a scope when leak testing fails, a connector is missing, a cycle alarms, or required steps cannot be confirmed. Tell the processing lead, troubleshoot under policy, and use another approved scope if care cannot wait.
Staff need training and repeated competency checks for each device and reprocessor. Use the current manufacturer's instructions and approved checklists; a new model should not be processed from memory of an older one.

Stop the incomplete cycle

A flexible bronchoscope is being prepared for an urgent procedure:
  • The scope passed leak testing and was manually cleaned
  • After an automated cycle, a required channel connector is found detached
  • The outer surface appears clean, but channel exposure cannot be confirmed
  • Another processed scope is available
