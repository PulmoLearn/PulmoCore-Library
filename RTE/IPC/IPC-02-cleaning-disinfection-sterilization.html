Cleaning, Disinfection & Sterilization

Reusable respiratory equipment needs the right reprocessing level before it reaches another patient. First contain and clean the used device. Then select low-level disinfection, high-level disinfection, or sterilization based on what tissue the item contacts and its validated manufacturer instructions. Rinse, dry, store, and document as the approved process requires.

When is this device safe to reuse?

Reprocessing decision framework

Contain → clean → classify → process → verify

Clean first, every time

Wear protection appropriate for the contaminated item. Transport it in a designated, contained route and remove visible and hidden soil according to the device instructions. Soil can interfere with both disinfection and sterilization; a wet wipe alone does not make a dirty device ready for use.

Noncritical: intact skin

A blood pressure cuff or the external surface of shared equipment touches intact skin, not a mucous membrane. Clean and use an appropriate low-level disinfectant between patients according to the product and facility instructions.

Semicritical: mucous membrane

Many reusable parts contacting the respiratory tract or an endoscope contacting mucous membranes need at least high-level disinfection after cleaning; sterilize when the device and approved process call for it. Follow validated rinse, drying, and storage steps.

Critical: sterile tissue

An item that enters sterile tissue or the vascular system must be sterile before use. Clean it, then use its validated sterilization method and required monitoring. If processing fails or the sterile package is compromised, do not use the item.

Decision rule: classify by intended tissue contact, not how dirty an item looks. The manufacturer's instructions for use and facility policy set the approved method, concentration, contact time, rinsing, drying, and storage.
Safety cue: high-level disinfection is not sterilization. A chemical left on a device or moisture trapped after processing can also harm the next patient; complete every validated step before release.
Levels and workflow

Review each concept

Protect staff from secretions and splashes, separate used devices from clean supplies, and transport reusable items to the designated processing area in approved containment. Follow local rules for regulated waste.
Remove organic and inorganic soil, including from channels or removable parts, exactly as the instructions describe. Residual material can block the action of a disinfectant or sterilizing process.
Items touching intact skin, such as a reusable blood pressure cuff, generally require cleaning and low-level disinfection between patients. Follow the product label and device compatibility instructions.
Items contacting mucous membranes or nonintact skin need at least high-level disinfection. Many reusable respiratory therapy items are in this category; verify the specific device's intended use and approved process.
Items that enter sterile tissue or the vascular system require sterilization before each use. A surface wipe or high-level disinfection alone cannot substitute for a validated sterile process.
High-level disinfection removes nearly all microorganisms but may not destroy large numbers of bacterial spores. Sterilization destroys all forms of microbial life when the process is effective.
Confirm the device and chemical are compatible. Follow the approved concentration, contact time, temperature, rinse, and processing steps; do not invent a shortcut based on the item looking clean.
When the process calls for rinsing, use the specified water and method; dry as directed and store so a processed item cannot become wet or contaminated before use.
Use required chemical tests, sterilization indicators, logs, and package checks according to the process. If a control fails, quarantine the affected items and follow the facility investigation and reprocessing plan.
Label and contain used items, perform hand hygiene after handling them, and do not put a dirty item on a clean cart. Communicate a processing failure so no one releases the device by mistake.
Integrated RTE-style equipment case

Stop an unsafe release

A reusable airway item that contacts mucous membranes is needed for another patient:
  • It was used and returned with visible secretions
  • Someone offers to soak it immediately without cleaning
  • The manufacturer lists an approved high-level disinfection process
  • The item must be rinsed and dried as directed before reuse
