Standard Precautions, Transmission-Based Precautions & PPE

Start with Standard Precautions for every patient. Add Contact, Droplet, or Airborne Precautions when the suspected or confirmed infection calls for them. Before a respiratory procedure, identify the likely exposure to blood, secretions, or aerosols; select personal protective equipment (PPE), protect the environment, and handle used items safely.

Precautions decision framework

Standard for all → add the route → assess the task → prevent spread

Standard Precautions: every patient

Perform hand hygiene, use safe injection and sharps practices, and choose gloves, gown, mask, and eye or face protection when exposure to blood, body fluids, or respiratory secretions is expected. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.

Transmission-Based Precautions: add on

Use Contact Precautions for organisms spread by direct or environmental contact; Droplet Precautions for infections such as seasonal influenza; Airborne Precautions for suspected pulmonary tuberculosis. More than one category may be needed. Follow the current facility and organism-specific policy.

Match PPE to the procedure

During open suction or another splash-prone task, protect hands, clothing, and face or eyes as indicated. A patient on Airborne Precautions needs appropriate room placement and a fit-tested N95 or higher respirator for staff. Other respiratory procedures may require additional protection under disease-specific policy.

Remove and handle safely

Follow the posted sequence for putting on and removing PPE, avoid touching its contaminated surfaces, and clean hands at the required points. Contain soiled disposables, transport reusable devices for reprocessing, and put used sharps directly in approved containers.

Core rule: Standard Precautions never disappear when isolation is added. Select both the transmission category and task-specific protection before entering or starting a procedure.
Respiratory procedure cue: aerosol-generating procedures may require higher-level respiratory protection and room controls under current pathogen-specific guidance. Check the patient’s precautions and local procedure policy; do not assume every nebulizer or suction task has one universal isolation category.
Precautions and PPE decisions

Review each concept

Use them for every patient regardless of known diagnosis. Hand hygiene, respiratory hygiene, safe injection and sharps handling, and PPE selected for likely exposure remain the foundation.
Ask whether the task could soil hands or clothing or splash eyes, nose, or mouth. Gloves, gown, and facial protection depend on that risk, even when no extra isolation category is ordered.
For a contact-spread organism, follow room-placement and gown-and-glove rules, use dedicated equipment when indicated, and clean shared equipment before another patient.
Seasonal influenza is a common example. Wear a medical mask according to the posted precautions and add other PPE for anticipated contact or splashes; mask the patient during necessary transport if tolerated and indicated.
Suspected pulmonary tuberculosis calls for an airborne infection isolation room when available and a fit-tested N95 or higher respirator for staff. Limit unnecessary transport and follow source-control instructions.
A suspected infection or syndrome may call for combined precautions. Check current organism-specific guidance rather than assuming that Contact, Droplet, and Airborne are mutually exclusive.
For open airway suction with expected splashes, choose gloves, gown, and appropriate eye or face protection, plus the mask or respirator indicated by the patient's infection status and procedure policy.
Keep contaminated surfaces away from skin and clean areas. Follow the facility doffing sequence, perform hand hygiene after glove removal, and clean hands again whenever contaminated.
Place used sharps immediately in a puncture-resistant sharps container; never leave them on a cart. Contain infectious materials and dispose of regulated waste according to policy rather than assuming all ordinary waste is regulated.
Do not carry a used respiratory device into another patient's room without approved cleaning or reprocessing. Contain and transport soiled reusable items according to local policy and separate them from clean supplies.

Prepare before bronchoscopy

A patient with suspected pulmonary tuberculosis needs an urgent provider-performed bronchoscopy:
  • The patient is under Airborne Precautions
  • Airway secretions and splashes may occur
  • A reusable respiratory device and used sharps must be handled afterward
  • The care team will follow its procedure and isolation policies
