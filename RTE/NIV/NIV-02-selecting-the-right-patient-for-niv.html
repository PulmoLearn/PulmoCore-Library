Selecting the Right Patient for NIV

NIV can prevent intubation when the patient has a reversible ventilatory or oxygenation problem and can safely use a mask. It becomes dangerous when the airway is unprotected, secretions cannot be managed, vomiting is active, cooperation is impossible, or instability demands immediate invasive support.

NIV candidacy framework

The ABG is only one part of the decision

Good candidate traits

Alert enough to cooperate, can protect the airway, can manage secretions, tolerates the interface, and is sufficiently hemodynamically stable for a closely monitored trial.

Major danger signs

Active vomiting, apnea, arrest, severe or rapidly worsening altered consciousness, unmanageable secretions, facial trauma preventing a seal, severe hemodynamic instability, or inability to cooperate.

Airway protection overrides the ABG.

An obtunded or vomiting patient may require intubation even before profound hypercapnia develops because mask-based support cannot secure the airway.

Match support to the failing function.

Oxygen treats isolated hypoxemia; CPAP provides continuous distending pressure for recruitment; bilevel NIV adds inspiratory assistance; invasive ventilation secures the airway and supports/replaces breathing.

NIV candidacy = physiology + airway safety + behavior + secretion control + stability.

Do not select NIV from PaCO₂ alone.
