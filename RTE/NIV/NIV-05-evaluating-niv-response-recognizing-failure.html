Evaluating NIV Response & Recognizing Failure

NIV success is a trend, not a single number. Reassess pH and PaCO₂, oxygenation, respiratory rate, returned tidal volume, work of breathing, mental status, synchrony, hemodynamics, interface tolerance, and overall trajectory. If these worsen despite appropriate support—or if a severe complication develops—do not delay invasive escalation.

Is NIV actually working?

Response framework

Improvement should appear in several domains—not just SpO₂

ImprovingpH rises, PaCO₂ falls or stabilizes appropriately, RR/effort fall, VT/air movement improve, alertness is maintained or improves.
ConcerningLittle change despite appropriate support, increasing pressure needs, persistent distress, poor tolerance, or marginal gas exchange.
FailingWorsening acidemia/PaCO₂, falling VT/air movement, persistent hypoxemia, fatigue, somnolence, apnea, shock, emesis/aspiration risk, or inability to tolerate/protect.
Do not let one reassuring value hide deterioration elsewhere.

A patient can have an acceptable SpO₂ and still be developing progressive ventilatory failure, fatigue, hypercapnia, or loss of airway protection.

Failure is a clinical trend. Your pediatric source explicitly says increasing NIV indefinitely is an error. Worsening CO₂/acidemia, fatigue, hypoxemia, or mental status is failure.
