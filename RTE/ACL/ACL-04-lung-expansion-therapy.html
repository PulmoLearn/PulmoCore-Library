Lung Expansion Therapy

Choose hyperinflation therapy when the main problem is poor lung expansion or alveolar collapse. Choose airway clearance when the main problem is retained secretions or obstruction. Sometimes the patient needs both—but they are not the same goal.

Hyperinflation framework

Identify the cause → choose the expansion strategy → verify setup → reassess response

Postoperative / low-volume atelectasisYour PulmoLearn atelectasis lesson identifies incentive spirometry as a lung-expansion strategy for postoperative or low-volume atelectasis prevention/treatment.
Poor inspiratory effort / persistent low volumeThe same source lists PEP, CPAP and IPPB as positive-pressure options that can help recruit alveoli when indicated.
Mucus plug / retained secretionsAirway clearance, suctioning and possible bronchoscopy escalation address obstruction. Oxygen or hyperinflation alone does not remove a mucus plug.
Manual hyperinflationThe ACL-04 registry explicitly requires the concept, but the retrieved source set does not provide a detailed bagging technique, pressure target, or maneuver sequence. This lesson therefore keeps manual hyperinflation at the selection/recognition level.
Core distinction

Alveolar recruitment opens underinflated/collapsed lung units. Secretion clearance moves or removes retained mucus. If collapse is caused by obstruction, reopening the lung requires addressing the obstruction—not just adding oxygen or pressure.
