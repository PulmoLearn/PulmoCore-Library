Choosing Airway-Clearance Therapy

Pick the therapy that matches the secretion problem, cough strength, diagnosis, patient ability, and precautions. “Airway clearance” is not one treatment.

Primary: III.B.3 Mechanical secretion-clearance devicesShared: III.B.1 Postural drainage, percussion, vibration
Manual CPTSelected patients with retained secretions or focal drainage needs.
PEP/OPEPCooperative patients who can actively exhale through a device.
HFCWOChronic secretion disorders; mobilizes mucus but does not replace cough.

Socratic selection string

Which therapy best matches the problem?

Selection framework

Secretion burden → cough → cooperation → pattern → precautions

Secretion burdenIs the problem retained mucus, recurrent plugging, focal drainage, or little/no secretion burden?
Cough effectivenessA therapy that mobilizes mucus is incomplete if the patient cannot clear what reaches the central airway.
Patient abilityPEP/OPEP requires active participation and technique. The pediatric source specifically says exercise/active-cycle breathing suits cooperative patients.
Pattern of diseaseYour source links HFCWO to chronic secretion disorders such as cystic fibrosis or neuromuscular disease.
Manual/focal drainage needManual percussion/postural drainage can be selected for retained secretions or focal drainage needs.
Contraindications/precautionsThe registry requires them to affect selection. The retrieved source set does not provide a complete contraindication list for every modality, so this lesson uses only source-supported patient-selection cautions.
RTE decision rule

Select the therapy that fixes the dominant clearance problem and that the patient can safely and effectively perform. Then reassess whether secretion movement and clearance improved.

The pediatric source describes PEP for mobile secretions and mild-to-moderate airflow obstruction in cooperative patients.
Positive pressure during exhalation helps splint airways and move gas behind secretions.
Patient ability matters: this is an active technique, not a passive therapy for a patient unable to follow the breathing maneuver.
OPEP combines expiratory resistance with oscillation to mobilize secretions.
Your lab competency requires technique, a tight seal, slow exhalation through the device, repeated breaths, and huff cough/expectorating afterward.
Device examples in source: Acapella and Flutter. Detailed setup belongs to ACL-03.

Your PulmoLearn neuromuscular/pediatric source uses MI-E when respiratory muscle weakness leads to weak cough and recurrent mucus plugging.
Selection logic: choose cough augmentation when the key failure is expiratory cough strength, not merely mucus mobilization.
