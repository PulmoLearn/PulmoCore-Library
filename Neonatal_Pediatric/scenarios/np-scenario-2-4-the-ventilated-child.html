Separate Oxygenation From Ventilation

Oxygenation

Think FiO₂ + recruitment + mean airway pressure. PEEP helps maintain end-expiratory lung volume.

Ventilation

Think effective minute ventilation: breath size × frequency, while avoiding overdistention.

Pressure concepts: plateau pressure approximates alveolar pressure during no flow. Driving pressure = plateau pressure − PEEP. Both should be interpreted with delivered volume and chest-wall mechanics.

Obstruction Changes the Timing Strategy

Patient A

8-year-old intubated for status asthmaticus

Severe airflow obstruction persists. Peak pressures are high. Expiratory flow has not returned to baseline before the next breath begins.

Obstructive strategy: lower rate, shorter inspiratory time, longer expiratory time, cautious tidal volume, and tolerance of some hypercapnia when appropriate. The major danger is dynamic hyperinflation and auto-PEEP.

Now the Child Develops Pediatric ARDS

Patient B

Child with septic shock

New bilateral infiltrates, poor compliance, and severe hypoxemia develop. Pulmonary edema is not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload. Tidal volume is 10 mL/kg and plateau pressure is rising.

PARDS lung protection: use smaller tidal volumes when compliance is poor, limit excessive pressure, optimize PEEP, consider permissive hypercapnia when appropriate, and avoid unnecessary positive fluid balance after stabilization.

Track Severity With Oxygenation Index

OI = (FiO₂ × Mean Airway Pressure × 100) ÷ PaO₂

FiO₂ 0.70 · MAP 16 cmH₂O · PaO₂ 56 mmHg

Calculate OI

Trend severity, not just one number. A rising OI suggests worsening oxygenation failure and may support escalation when combined with the child’s overall clinical condition.

Rescue Therapies Should Solve a Specific Problem

Prone positioningMay improve dorsal recruitment, secretion drainage, and V/Q matching in selected moderate-to-severe PARDS.
Neuromuscular blockadeMay be used briefly when severe dyssynchrony prevents lung-protective ventilation despite adequate sedation.
High-frequency ventilationMay be considered when conventional ventilation cannot provide gas exchange without injurious pressures or volumes.
ECMOConsidered for potentially reversible refractory cardiorespiratory failure despite optimized conventional and rescue therapy.
Conservative fluid strategyAfter stabilization, avoid unnecessary positive fluid balance while maintaining organ perfusion.
ReassessmentEvery rescue intervention needs a measurable response target and harm check.

Sudden Deterioration and Liberation Are Both Ventilator Decisions

Emergency

After repositioning

The intubated child suddenly desaturates. Peak pressure rises, exhaled tidal volume falls, and breath sounds are absent on the left.

Patient first → airway → circuit → ventilator → disease. Sudden deterioration should trigger a structured airway-and-circuit check before blindly increasing pressure.
Two days later

The child improves

Underlying disease is resolving, gas exchange is stable on modest settings, spontaneous respiratory drive is consistent, cough and secretion handling are acceptable, and a postextubation support plan is ready.
