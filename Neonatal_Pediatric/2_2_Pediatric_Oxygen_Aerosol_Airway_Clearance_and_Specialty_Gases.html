Match Device to Demand

Oxygen Therapy Begins With a Target and a Reassessment Plan

Oxygen is a medication. Select the device according to the ordered saturation range, oxygen requirement, inspiratory flow demand, airway anatomy, comfort, and need for humidification.

DeviceBest useImportant limitation
Low-flow nasal cannulaMild oxygen need in a stable child who can breathe through the nose.Delivered FiO₂ varies with flow, size, mouth breathing, and inspiratory demand.
Simple maskShort-term moderate oxygen need when a nasal interface is not tolerated.Requires enough flow to limit CO₂ rebreathing and prevents eating or speaking normally.
Nonrebreather maskRapid delivery of a high oxygen concentration during acute hypoxemia.Needs adequate flow and reservoir inflation; seal and cooperation affect performance.
Oxygen hoodControlled humidified oxygen around an infant’s head.Requires sufficient flow to prevent CO₂ accumulation and careful temperature monitoring.
HFNCHeated humidified high flow for hypoxemia and increased work of breathing.Does not replace ventilation when apnea, fatigue, or severe hypercapnia is present.

High Flow Is More Than Oxygen

HFNC Conditions Gas and May Reduce Work of Breathing

HFNC delivers heated humidified gas at flows intended to meet or exceed inspiratory demand. It can reduce nasopharyngeal dead space and may generate variable distending pressure.

Reassuring response
Lower respiratory rate, reduced retractions, improved comfort, lower oxygen need, and stable CO₂.
Failure signs
Worsening retractions, apnea, rising CO₂, altered mental status, persistent hypoxemia, or hemodynamic instability.
Cannula fit matters: Prongs should not completely occlude the nares. Excessive occlusion can increase unintended pressure.
Medication Deposition

Technique Often Matters More Than the Device Name

DeviceStrengthCommon barrier
pMDI with spacer or valved holding chamberPortable, fast, reproducible, and effective when technique is correct.Poor seal, poor coordination, crying, or insufficient breaths through the chamber.
Jet nebulizerFamiliar and useful for continuous or intermittent treatment.Residual volume, long treatment time, noise, and variable output.
Vibrating mesh nebulizerLow residual volume and efficient output; can be used in ventilator circuits.Cost, cleaning requirements, and medication/device compatibility.
Breath-actuated nebulizerReduces medication loss during exhalation in cooperative patients.Requires enough inspiratory flow and patient cooperation.
Dry-powder inhalerPortable and breath-actuated.Requires adequate inspiratory flow and is unsuitable for many young or acutely ill children.

For infants and young children, use a valved holding chamber with a properly fitted mask. Calm tidal breathing improves deposition; crying substantially reduces lower-airway delivery.

When Cough Is Not Enough

Mobilized Secretions Still Must Leave the Airway

Assess cough strength, bulbar function, secretion volume, airway patency, oxygen reserve, and ability to cooperate. Suction only when secretions are present or obstruction is suspected rather than on an automatic schedule.

Mechanical insufflation-exsufflation
Applies positive pressure followed by negative pressure to simulate a cough in patients with respiratory muscle weakness.
Airway suction
Removes secretions from an artificial airway or accessible upper airway when the patient cannot clear them.

Lower Gas Density

Heliox Can Reduce Turbulent Resistance in a Narrowed Airway

Heliox is a helium-oxygen mixture. Its lower density may improve flow and reduce work of breathing when turbulent resistance is prominent, such as selected upper-airway obstruction or severe obstructive disease.

Bridge, not cure: Heliox does not remove a foreign body, reduce edema directly, reverse bronchospasm by itself, or replace airway stabilization. Benefit decreases as the required oxygen concentration rises.
Selective Pulmonary Vasodilation

Inhaled Pulmonary Vasodilators Redirect Blood Toward Ventilated Lung

TherapyPrimary effectImportant monitoring
Inhaled nitric oxideSelective pulmonary vasodilation in ventilated lung regions.Oxygenation response, NO/NO₂ concentration, methemoglobin, delivery-system function, and rebound during weaning.
Inhaled epoprostenolInhaled prostacyclin that may reduce pulmonary vascular resistance.Clinical response, aerosol delivery, circuit deposition, systemic blood pressure, platelet effects, and interruption risk.

Neither therapy replaces lung recruitment, ventilation, correction of acidosis, or treatment of the underlying disease. Use echocardiographic and hemodynamic information when available.

Key Takeaways

Oxygen devices are not interchangeable
Match FiO₂ need, flow demand, humidification, tolerance, and ventilation status.
Aerosol success depends on technique
Seal, coordination, calm breathing, device position, and cleaning determine deposition.
Mobilization and removal are different
Airway-clearance devices move secretions; cough augmentation or suction may still be needed.
Specialty gases are targeted bridges
Heliox reduces gas-density effects; inhaled vasodilators target pulmonary vascular resistance.
