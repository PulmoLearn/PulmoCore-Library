Advanced Support

High-Frequency Ventilation Separates Lung Recruitment From Small-Volume Ventilation

ModeCore conceptPrimary controls
HFOVVery small oscillatory volumes are delivered around a constant mean airway pressure.Mean airway pressure controls lung volume and oxygenation; amplitude and frequency influence CO₂ clearance.
HFJVRapid high-velocity pulses are delivered through a specialized adapter while exhalation remains passive.Jet PIP, rate, inspiratory time, and background PEEP affect ventilation and lung volume.
General principle: Oxygenation depends largely on lung recruitment and mean airway pressure plus FiO₂. Ventilation depends on delivered oscillatory or jet volume, frequency, amplitude or pressure, and the infant’s mechanics.
HFOV may help when
Conventional ventilation cannot maintain oxygenation without excessive pressure or volume.
HFJV may help when
There is severe air leak, pulmonary interstitial emphysema, or a need for very short inspiratory pulses with passive exhalation.

Severity Tracking

Oxygenation Index Combines Oxygen Need, Pressure, and PaO₂

OI = (FiO₂ × Mean Airway Pressure × 100) ÷ PaO₂

Use FiO₂ as a decimal. A rising OI suggests worsening oxygenation failure or increasing support burden. Always interpret the trend with blood pressure, lactate, ventilation, imaging, and disease trajectory.

Protect the Fragile Lung

Air-Leak Strategies Minimize Distending Pressure and Trapped Gas

When pulmonary interstitial emphysema or recurrent pneumothorax develops, reduce unnecessary tidal volume and pressure, avoid gas trapping, optimize ETT position, and consider positioning or a high-frequency strategy that permits effective ventilation at lower distending forces.

Lower injury load
Reduce excessive PIP, tidal volume, inspiratory time, and rate when feasible.
Promote exhalation
Allow adequate expiratory time and monitor for hyperinflation.
Reassess the cause
Check airway depth, secretion burden, unilateral disease, circuit resistance, and synchrony.

Prematurity and Respiratory Support Affect Multiple Organ Systems

ComplicationRisk relationshipRespiratory relevance
BPDExtreme prematurity, inflammation, oxygen exposure, pressure/volume injury, infection, and prolonged support.Leads to chronic oxygen need, variable airway reactivity, impaired growth, and pulmonary hypertension risk.
ROPPrematurity and excessive or unstable oxygen exposure.Requires careful saturation targeting and avoidance of unnecessary hyperoxia.
IVHFragile cerebral vessels, abrupt hemodynamic changes, severe illness, and extreme prematurity.Avoid large swings in CO₂, blood pressure, oxygenation, and handling.
NECPrematurity, intestinal immaturity, inflammation, and perfusion instability.Abdominal distention can impair ventilation; worsening acidosis and apnea may be early signs.

Neurologic Protection

Therapeutic Hypothermia Requires Respiratory and Metabolic Vigilance

Eligible term or near-term infants with moderate-to-severe hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy may receive controlled whole-body or selective head cooling according to protocol. Cooling alters metabolism, heart rate, blood-gas interpretation, medication clearance, and oxygen consumption.

Monitor closely
Ventilation, oxygenation, blood pressure, glucose, electrolytes, coagulation, seizures, and skin integrity.
Avoid overventilation
Reduced CO₂ production during cooling can produce hypocapnia if ventilator support is not adjusted.
Move Only After Stabilization

Transport Adds Risk to Every Airway, Line, and Monitor

Before transport, stabilize temperature, airway, ventilation, oxygenation, circulation, glucose, and vascular access. Anticipate changes in altitude, battery life, gas supply, vibration, ambient temperature, and access to the infant.

Airway
Confirm depth, securement, patency, backup equipment, suction, and manual ventilation capability.
Equipment
Verify battery, oxygen reserve, alarms, humidification, monitor leads, and transport ventilator performance.
Communication
Share diagnosis, trends, medications, lines, ventilator settings, risks, and contingency plans.
