Competency Priorities

What You Must Be Able to Apply

Choose support intentionally
Match the mode and controls to airway resistance, compliance, respiratory drive, and gas-exchange goals.
Protect the lung
Use appropriate tidal volume, pressure limitation, PEEP, and synchrony while avoiding repeated collapse and overdistention.
Recognize PARDS
Use timing, pulmonary edema not fully explained by cardiac failure, imaging, and oxygenation impairment.
Escalate and liberate safely
Know when to recruit, prone, consider high-frequency support or ECMO, and when to begin weaning.
Why Intubate?

Mechanical Ventilation Supports a Failing System

Primary indicationExamples
Ventilatory failureRising PaCO₂ with acidemia, fatigue, apnea, or neuromuscular weakness.
Oxygenation failurePersistent hypoxemia despite optimized noninvasive support.
Airway protectionAltered mental status, uncontrolled seizures, aspiration risk, or severe upper-airway disease.
Excessive work of breathingImpending fatigue despite treatment of the underlying cause.
Procedural or hemodynamic supportTransport, surgery, shock, or need for deep sedation and paralysis.
Intubation does not treat the disease. It creates time to reverse the underlying process while protecting oxygen delivery and reducing respiratory muscle load.

Start With a Strategy

Initial Settings Must Fit the Disease

Use the child’s ideal or predicted body size, mechanics, disease pattern, blood gas, and hemodynamics. Reassess immediately after connection.

Low-compliance diffuse disease
Use lung-protective tidal volume, adequate PEEP, pressure limitation, and recruitment assessment.
Obstructive disease
Use lower rate, adequate expiratory time, careful tidal volume, and permissive hypercapnia when appropriate.
Neuromuscular weakness
Support tidal volume and cough while avoiding excessive sedation and unnecessary oxygen.
Hemodynamic instability
Anticipate reduced venous return and closely monitor perfusion when positive pressure begins.

Pediatric ARDS

PARDS Is Acute Diffuse Lung Injury With Oxygenation Impairment

PALICC-2 provides pediatric-specific diagnostic and management guidance. The syndrome develops acutely, is associated with a known clinical insult, produces pulmonary edema not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload, and causes impaired oxygenation with new imaging abnormalities.

Management priorityClinical application
Lung-protective tidal volumeUse smaller tidal volumes in poor compliance and avoid excessive stretch.
Pressure limitationMonitor plateau and driving pressure alongside delivered volume and chest-wall mechanics.
PEEP optimizationBalance recruitment against overdistention and hemodynamic compromise.
Permissive hypercapniaAccept a higher PaCO₂ when needed to reduce injury, provided pH and contraindications are considered.
Conservative fluid strategy after stabilizationAvoid unnecessary positive fluid balance while preserving organ perfusion.

Track Severity

Oxygenation Index Includes FiO₂, Mean Airway Pressure, and PaO₂

OI = (FiO₂ × Mean Airway Pressure × 100) ÷ PaO₂

Oxygen saturation index substitutes SpO₂ when an arterial PaO₂ is unavailable. Trends are more informative than isolated values.

Escalation Options

Adjuncts Are Selected for Refractory Disease, Not Used Automatically

Prone positioning
May improve dorsal recruitment, secretion drainage, and V/Q matching in selected moderate-to-severe PARDS.
Neuromuscular blockade
May be used briefly when severe dyssynchrony prevents lung-protective ventilation despite adequate sedation.
High-frequency ventilation
May be considered when conventional ventilation cannot provide gas exchange without injurious pressures or volumes.

ECMO is considered for potentially reversible, refractory cardiorespiratory failure despite optimized conventional and rescue therapy.

Key Takeaways

Mode is only the starting point
Outcomes depend on the actual tidal volume, pressure, timing, PEEP, synchrony, and reassessment.
Separate oxygenation from ventilation
FiO₂ and recruitment affect oxygenation; effective minute ventilation controls CO₂.
PARDS requires lung protection
Avoid excessive tidal volume, pressure, derecruitment, and unnecessary fluid burden.
Weaning starts when stabilization begins
Daily assessment of disease, drive, strength, airway, and postextubation support prevents delay.
