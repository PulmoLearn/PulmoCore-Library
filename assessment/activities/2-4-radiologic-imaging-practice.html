Imaging Decisions: Choose, Validate, Interpret & Act

Select the study that answers the immediate question, protect the patient during imaging, verify chest-radiograph quality before interpretation, recognize major cardiopulmonary patterns, and act quickly when a device problem or tension physiology is more urgent than another image.

Start with the clinical question—not the fanciest modality

Competency focus: Match chest radiography, CT/CTPA, MRI, TTE/TEE, PET, or V/Q imaging to the question while accounting for stability, contrast, renal function, pregnancy, implants, and transport risk.

Portable CXR

Rapid bedside overview, device position, pneumothorax, edema, effusion, consolidation.

CT / CTPA

Detailed thoracic anatomy; CTPA specifically opacifies pulmonary arteries with iodinated contrast.

MRI

Soft-tissue detail without ionizing radiation, but strict implant/equipment compatibility is required.

TTE / TEE

Cardiac structure and function; TEE is invasive and adds sedation/airway implications.

PET

Metabolic activity such as malignancy or treatment response; not an acute tube-position study.

V/Q

Regional ventilation and perfusion; useful when CTPA is unsuitable and the clinical context fits.
