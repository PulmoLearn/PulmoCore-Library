Move from preliminary information to definitive data

Competency focus: Distinguish Gram reaction and morphology from culture identification and antimicrobial susceptibility.

Gram-positive cocci

Purple suggests Gram-positive; cocci describes spherical morphology. This is preliminary information.

Gram-negative bacilli

Pink/red suggests Gram-negative; bacilli describes rod-shaped morphology. This is preliminary information.

Sequence: specimen quality → Gram reaction and morphology → culture identity → susceptibility → clinical correlation → treatment reassessment.

Classify pneumonia from timing, setting, and ventilation status

Competency focus: Classify CAP, HAP, and VAP using the full clinical pattern rather than the diagnosis label alone.

CAP

Begins outside the hospital or early in admission and is not attributable to the current hospitalization.

HAP

Develops ≥48 hours after admission and is not associated with mechanical ventilation.

VAP

Develops after endotracheal intubation/mechanical ventilation; a common operational threshold is ≥48 hours after intubation plus a compatible clinical pattern.

VAE ≠ VAP. Ventilator-associated event surveillance can reflect sustained worsening oxygenation from infectious or noninfectious causes. VAP is a clinical infection diagnosis.

Procedures: recognize the complication before it becomes the diagnosis

Competency focus: Prepare for and monitor bronchoscopy and thoracentesis, recognize procedure-specific hazards, and respond rapidly to deterioration.

Bronchoscopy safety

Verify indication, consent status, NPO status per policy, allergies, anticoagulation/coagulation concerns, baseline oxygen support, airway risk, IV access, and monitoring. Watch for hypoxemia, bronchospasm, bleeding, dysrhythmia, sedation-related hypoventilation, and post-biopsy pneumothorax.

Thoracentesis safety

Monitor respiratory status and hemodynamics. Sudden dyspnea, hypoxemia, tachycardia, or unilateral breath-sound loss afterward should raise concern for pneumothorax and prompt immediate support/escalation.

Prevent infection by interrupting the pathway

Hands & PPEHand hygiene, standard precautions, and transmission-based isolation when indicated.
Oral careReduce oral pathogen burden and support comfort according to the prevention bundle.
Ventilator durationReduce unnecessary ventilation and support readiness for liberation.
Circuit integrityAvoid routine circuit changes when clean and functioning; unnecessary disconnections may increase contamination and physiologic instability.
CondensateDrain away from the patient into the designated trap while minimizing circuit disruption.
Equipment hygieneUse clean/appropriately processed respiratory equipment and safe nebulizer handling.
