Hypoxemia and tissue hypoxia are not synonyms

Competency focus: Distinguish an arterial oxygen problem from a tissue oxygen-delivery or utilization problem.
OxygenIs oxygen loading into arterial blood?
CarrierIs there enough functioning hemoglobin?
PumpIs enough blood reaching the tissues?
CellCan the tissues use delivered oxygen?
PaO₂ reflects dissolved arterial oxygen tension. SpO₂ estimates the percentage of available hemoglobin binding sites occupied by oxygen. Neither one, by itself, proves that tissue oxygen delivery is adequate.

Early versus late warning signs

RAT: restlessness, anxiety, tachycardia/tachypnea are early compensatory clues. BED-C: bradycardia, altered behavior/mentation, severe or declining breathing effort, and cyanosis are high-risk late findings.

Use the A-a gradient to localize gas-exchange failure

Competency focus: Calculate the alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient and distinguish extrapulmonary causes of hypoxemia from impaired pulmonary oxygen transfer.
Room air at sea level: PAO₂ ≈ 150 − (PaCO₂ ÷ 0.8)
P(A-a)O₂ = PAO₂ − PaO₂
A small or expected gradient supports causes such as hypoventilation or low inspired oxygen. A widened gradient supports impaired pulmonary transfer such as V/Q mismatch, diffusion limitation, or shunt.

Calculate arterial oxygen content

Competency focus: Compare patients whose PaO₂ or SpO₂ values look similar but whose hemoglobin concentrations make their actual oxygen content very different.
CaO₂ = (1.34 × Hgb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)
The hemoglobin-bound component dominates oxygen content. Severe anemia can produce critically low CaO₂ even when PaO₂ and saturation look reassuring.

Affinity changes unloading—but does not replace content or perfusion assessment

Competency focus: Interpret right and left shifts of the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve and recognize when normal monitor values are misleading.

Right shift

Lower affinity → easier tissue unloading.

Associated with increased temperature, increased CO₂, increased H⁺ / lower pH, and increased 2,3-DPG.

Left shift

Higher affinity → tighter binding and less tissue unloading.

Associated with lower temperature, lower CO₂, lower H⁺ / higher pH, and some abnormal hemoglobin situations.
