Purpose before wording

Every question should answer a clinical need

A respiratory interview is not a memorized list. Begin broadly, listen to the answer, then use focused follow-up questions only when the answer makes them relevant.

Open-ended

Purpose: Let the patient describe the problem in their own words.

“Tell me what has changed with your breathing.”

Closed-ended

Purpose: Verify or clarify a specific fact.

“Do you use oxygen at home?”

Focused follow-up

Purpose: Add detail after a positive or important answer.

“When did the shortness of breath begin?”

Verification

Purpose: Confirm charted information with the patient.

“I see asthma listed in your history. Is that correct?”

Clarifying

Purpose: Replace vague wording with usable detail.

“When you say ‘often,’ about how many times per week?”

Baseline comparison

Purpose: Determine whether the current finding is new or usual.

“Is that different from what you can normally do?”

Safety / priority

Purpose: Identify instability that may require the interview to stop.

“Are you having severe trouble breathing right now?”

Final competency review

A complete respiratory interview is not the longest interview. It is the interview that gathers the information needed to describe the patient's current respiratory status, compare it with baseline, recognize missing or conflicting data, and communicate what needs to happen next.

Can you now…

  • Choose open versus focused questions?
  • Let patient answers determine follow-up?
  • Verify charted respiratory history?
  • Clarify home respiratory medications?
  • Gather smoking/vaping and exposure history?
  • Compare current symptoms with baseline function?
  • Separate patient-reported from measured data?
  • Recognize missing or contradictory information?

Core interview rule

Ask with a purpose. Do not collect details until you have established that the topic is relevant. When an answer changes the respiratory story, follow it.
