Cardiac Performance Depends on Rate, Filling, Resistance, and Contractility

The kidneys strongly influence cardiac function by regulating fluid balance, acid–base balance, and electrolytes. They do not directly maintain oxygen balance, although renal function can affect oxygen delivery through erythropoietin and volume status.

Right Heart to Lungs; Left Heart to Body

The right ventricle ejects blood into the pulmonary circulation. The left ventricle ejects blood into the systemic circulation. The sinoatrial node is the normal pacemaker.

Beta-1 receptors are concentrated primarily in the heart. Their stimulation increases heart rate, conduction, and contractility.

The refractory period is the interval during which cardiac cells cannot be restimulated normally. Sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium all influence cardiac conduction and arrhythmia risk.

Clinical correction: Magnesium does affect cardiac conduction and is especially important in torsades de pointes. It should not be taught as irrelevant to the conduction system.

Heart Failure Therapy Targets Volume, Resistance, Rate, and Contractility

Heart failure can cause cardiomegaly, ankle edema, pulmonary edema, and reduced renal perfusion. The kidneys tend to retain sodium and water rather than increase fluid removal.

Drug classPrimary goalExamples
DiureticsReduce excess fluid and preloadLoop and thiazide diuretics
ACE inhibitorsReduce systemic vascular resistance and afterloadLisinopril, enalapril
Beta blockersReduce chronic sympathetic stress in stable systolic heart failureMetoprolol succinate, carvedilol
VasodilatorsReduce preload and/or afterloadNitrates, hydralazine
InotropesTemporarily improve contractility in selected unstable patientsDobutamine, dopamine

Pulmonary Hypertension Can Progress to Cor Pulmonale

Chronic pulmonary hypertension increases right-ventricular afterload. Untreated, it can lead to right-sided heart disease known as cor pulmonale.

Recognize Cor Pulmonale

A patient with longstanding pulmonary hypertension develops peripheral edema, jugular venous distention, and right-ventricular enlargement.

Identify the condition.

Acute Coronary Therapy Preserves Myocardium and Left-Ventricular Function

Nitroglycerin reduces myocardial oxygen demand through venodilation and may relieve ischemic chest pain. The preferred emergency route is commonly sublingual because of rapid onset.

Safety first: Check blood pressure and recent phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use before administering nitrates. Hypotension can be severe.

Clopidogrel is an antiplatelet medication used in acute coronary syndrome. The overall goal of myocardial infarction treatment is to limit ischemic injury, reduce workload and oxygen demand, restore perfusion when possible, and preserve left-ventricular function.
