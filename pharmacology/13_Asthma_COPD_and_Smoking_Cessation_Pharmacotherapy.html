Asthma and COPD Share Obstruction but Differ in Pattern

FeatureAsthmaCOPD
Airflow limitationVariable and often substantially reversiblePersistent, though some reversibility may occur
InflammationOften eosinophilic or type-2, but phenotypes varyOften neutrophilic and exposure-related
Typical patternEpisodes of wheeze, cough, chest tightness, nocturnal or early-morning symptomsProgressive dyspnea, chronic cough, sputum, exposure history
Structural hallmarkAirway hyperresponsiveness and inflammationSmall-airway disease and/or emphysematous destruction

Loss of elastic recoil and alveolar-wall destruction are features of emphysema, not uncomplicated asthma. Hyperinflation may occur during an asthma exacerbation and may be chronic in COPD, but it is not literally present in every patient at all times.

Do not rely on wheezing alone. Severe asthma can produce markedly diminished breath sounds or a “silent chest,” which is more concerning than loud wheezing.

Assess Current Control and Future Risk

Older severity labels such as intermittent, mild persistent, moderate persistent, and severe persistent remain useful for understanding untreated disease patterns. Current asthma management places greater emphasis on symptom control, exacerbation risk, lung function, adherence, technique, and response to treatment.

Symptoms suggesting poor control
Frequent daytime symptoms, night waking, activity limitation, and frequent reliever use.
High-risk clues
Prior severe exacerbation, low lung function, medication underuse, incorrect inhaler technique, smoking, or eosinophilic inflammation.

Escalate Treatment When Repeated Bronchodilators Are Not Enough

Acute asthma treatment may include oxygen, repeated rapid bronchodilator therapy, inhaled ipratropium for severe exacerbations, and early systemic corticosteroids. Reassess respiratory rate, work of breathing, mental status, oxygenation, peak flow or FEV₁ when feasible, and response after each intervention.

Prevention Requires More Than Writing a Prescription

Patients need a written action plan, correct inhaler technique, adherence assessment, trigger identification, and clear instructions for worsening symptoms. Annual influenza vaccination, hand hygiene, and reducing viral exposure can decrease infection-associated exacerbation risk.

Allergen immunotherapy may help selected patients with allergic asthma, but it does not prevent viral transmission.

COPD Diagnosis Requires Post-Bronchodilator Obstruction

COPD is confirmed by a post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC below 0.70 in the appropriate clinical context. FEV₁ percent predicted grades airflow limitation, but symptoms and exacerbation history guide treatment decisions.

Smoking Cessation Is Disease-Modifying Therapy

Pack-years equal packs smoked per day multiplied by years smoked. Tobacco cessation slows COPD progression and reduces cardiovascular, cancer, and exacerbation risk.

Nicotine replacement therapy
Patch, gum, lozenge, inhaler, or nasal spray reduce withdrawal without delivering combustion products.
Other medications
Varenicline and bupropion are options when clinically appropriate.

NRT generally provides slower, more controlled nicotine delivery than smoking. Combining a long-acting patch with a short-acting form can improve craving control.

COPD Pharmacotherapy Is Based on Symptoms and Exacerbation Risk

Short-acting bronchodilators such as albuterol and ipratropium are used during acute exacerbations. Long-acting bronchodilators are central to maintenance therapy. Inhaled corticosteroids are added selectively, particularly for patients with exacerbation risk and evidence suggesting steroid responsiveness, rather than automatically based only on FEV₁ stage.

Long-term oxygen is not prescribed by “stage” alone. It is based on documented chronic severe resting hypoxemia and qualifying clinical criteria after optimal treatment.

Key Takeaways

Asthma is variable and inflammatory
Controller therapy reduces future risk; rescue bronchodilation alone is not enough.
Technique and education are treatment
Action plans, trigger recognition, and inhaler mastery prevent avoidable exacerbations.
COPD treatment is multidimensional
Use spirometry to confirm obstruction, but base therapy on symptoms and exacerbations.
Smoking cessation changes outcomes
Medication plus behavioral support is one of the highest-value COPD interventions.
