Empiric Therapy Comes Before the Exact Organism Is Known

Empiric therapy is selected from the likely pathogens, disease severity, patient age, allergies, local resistance patterns, and infection site. Once culture and susceptibility data are available, therapy should be narrowed whenever possible.

Stewardship principle: Broad coverage may be appropriate initially in severe infection, but unnecessary duration and excessive spectrum increase adverse effects and resistance.

Aerosolized anti-infectives have specialized indications, including selected cystic fibrosis infections and certain opportunistic infections. They are not routine treatment for every COPD exacerbation.

Upper-Airway Infection Patterns Guide Treatment

Acute otitis media often follows a viral upper-respiratory infection. When antibiotics are indicated, amoxicillin is commonly used unless patient factors or local resistance patterns require another option.

Sinusitis is inflammation of the paranasal sinus mucosa. Most uncomplicated cases are diagnosed clinically; CT is generally reserved for complications, atypical disease, surgical planning, or chronic/recurrent problems rather than routine acute diagnosis.

Untreated group A streptococcal pharyngitis can lead to acute rheumatic fever and subsequent valvular damage.

Epiglottitis Is an Airway Emergency; Croup Is Usually Viral

Drooling, dysphagia, dysphonia, distress, tripod positioning, and a toxic appearance should raise concern for epiglottitis. Do not agitate the child or force an oral examination outside a controlled airway setting.

When bacterial epiglottitis is suspected, airway protection comes first. Third-generation cephalosporins such as ceftriaxone or cefotaxime are commonly included in empiric treatment, with additional coverage selected according to local guidance and patient factors.

Croup commonly presents with a barking cough, hoarseness, and inspiratory stridor. Dexamethasone is standard therapy, and nebulized epinephrine may be used for moderate to severe disease.

The most likely pathogen depends on age, comorbidities, exposure, severity, and local epidemiology. Avoid teaching one organism as universally “the” cause of community-acquired pneumonia.

VAP Prevention Requires Consistent Bedside Practice

Hand hygiene, oral care according to protocol, aspiration-risk reduction, appropriate cuff management, head-of-bed elevation when not contraindicated, early mobility, sedation minimization, and daily readiness-to-wean assessment all reduce risk.

Do not change ventilator circuits routinely every 24 hours. Change them when visibly soiled, malfunctioning, or according to infection-prevention policy.

Suspected Pulmonary TB Requires Airborne Precautions

Weight loss, night sweats, fever, prolonged cough, and hemoptysis should prompt evaluation for tuberculosis. Place the patient in airborne isolation and use a fit-tested respirator while diagnostic testing is obtained.

Tuberculin skin-test interpretation depends on the patient’s risk category. A 5 mm induration can be positive in high-risk patients, while 10 mm or 15 mm thresholds apply to other groups. A value below 5 mm is not universally the only “negative” result.

Active drug-susceptible TB is treated with multiple medications, commonly including isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol initially. Latent-TB regimens vary and may use shorter rifamycin-based options or isoniazid-based alternatives.
