The Lung Uses Innate and Adaptive Defenses

Macrophages, lymphocytes, antibodies, mucus, cilia, and cough help protect the respiratory tract. An antigen is the foreign substance that triggers the response; it is not itself a defense mechanism.

Humoral response
B lymphocytes differentiate into plasma cells and produce antibodies.
Cell-mediated response
T lymphocytes coordinate and directly participate in immune defense.

Allergic Asthma Is an IgE-Mediated Airway Response

Common triggers include dust mites, mold, animal dander, pollens, and selected food additives such as sulfites. Allergic asthma has historically been called atopic or extrinsic asthma.

IgE
The immunoglobulin most associated with immediate allergic rhinitis and allergic asthma.
Mast cells
Release histamine and other mediators after allergen-triggered activation.
Eosinophils
Support ongoing allergic inflammation and are especially important in the late-phase response.

Early and Late Responses Explain Why Rescue Therapy Is Not Enough

When an allergen cross-links IgE on mast cells, degranulation releases histamine and other mediators. This produces the early response: bronchoconstriction, vascular leak, edema, and mucus production.

The arachidonic acid cascade generates prostaglandins and leukotrienes. The later inflammatory response recruits eosinophils and other cells, prolonging airway hyperresponsiveness and edema.

Sequence the Allergic Response

Allergen cross-links IgE on a mast cell
Mast cell degranulation releases mediators
Bronchoconstriction, edema, and mucus increase

Corticosteroids Reduce Inflammation Rather Than Provide Immediate Rescue

Corticosteroids reduce inflammatory-cell activity, suppress cytokine production, and inhibit upstream portions of the arachidonic acid cascade. They decrease eosinophilic inflammation and improve airway responsiveness over time.

Allergic Rhinitis and Histamine-Driven Symptoms

Histamine causes vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, mucus production, itching, sneezing, and increased airway resistance.

Intranasal corticosteroid
Fluticasone nasal spray is effective for long-term allergic-rhinitis control.
H1 antihistamine
Fexofenadine blocks histamine at the H1 receptor.

First-generation antihistamines more readily enter the central nervous system and commonly cause drowsiness. Second-generation agents are generally less sedating, although no medication is completely free of that possibility.
