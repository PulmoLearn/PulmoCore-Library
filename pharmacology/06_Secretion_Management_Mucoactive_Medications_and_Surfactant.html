Mucus Is Protective Until It Becomes Retained

Goblet cells and submucosal bronchial glands collectively produce roughly 100 mL of mucus each day. In a healthy airway, this mucus lubricates the epithelium, traps inhaled particles, limits excessive water loss, and contributes antimicrobial protection.

Important boundary: Cilia move mucus from conducting airways toward the pharynx. They do not sweep debris directly out of the alveoli.
Healthy clearance
Adequate hydration, coordinated ciliary beating, an effective cough, and open airways.
Impaired clearance
Dehydration, thick mucus, high oxygen exposure, artificial airways, smoke, infection, sedation, weakness, and immobility.

Repeated irritant exposure can enlarge and increase bronchial glands, increase mucus production, damage cilia, and create the chronic retention pattern seen in chronic bronchitis.

The Gel Layer Rides on the Sol Layer

The mucociliary blanket is often described as two functional layers. The upper gel layer traps particles and contains mucins. The lower sol layer is a more watery space that allows cilia to beat effectively.

Gel layer problem
Mucus is too thick or chemically tenacious. A mucolytic may alter its structure or viscosity.
Sol layer problem
The watery layer is too shallow. Hydration, humidification, or saline may increase water available for transport.
Pharmacologic goals: Increase sol-layer depth, alter gel-layer consistency, and support ciliary action. Increasing the number of bronchial glands is not a treatment goal.

Bland Aerosols and Saline Solutions

Water and saline can hydrate airway secretions, but concentration, sterility, delivery device, and patient tolerance matter.

SolutionTypical roleKey caution
Sterile waterHumidification or bland aerosol in selected systemsHypotonic aerosol may provoke cough or bronchospasm
0.9% salineIsotonic diluent or hydration supportDoes not create the osmotic effect of hypertonic saline
3%–7% salineSputum induction or secretion hydration in selected patients, especially cystic fibrosisMay trigger bronchospasm; assess need for bronchodilator pretreatment

Sterile means free of viable microorganisms. Distilled describes a purification process and does not automatically mean a container is sterile. Neither term means “isotonic.” Verify the exact product label and intended use.

N-acetylcysteine can cause bronchospasm and has a sulfur odor. Bronchodilator pretreatment, airway-clearance planning, and suction readiness may be needed. Do not give a mucolytic without a plan to remove the loosened secretions.

Dornase alfa is Pulmozyme. It is disease-specific therapy for cystic fibrosis and should be delivered only with a compatible nebulizer listed in the current product instructions.

Cystic Fibrosis Therapy Is Sequenced to Open, Hydrate, Mobilize, and Treat

The exact regimen is individualized, but the logic is consistent: improve airway caliber, hydrate or alter secretions, perform airway clearance, and deliver inhaled antimicrobial or anti-inflammatory therapy when prescribed.

Do Not Suppress a Cough the Patient Needs

Antitussives reduce cough. Examples include dextromethorphan and codeine. They may be useful for a dry, irritating, nonproductive cough after the cause has been assessed.

Avoid routine suppression: A patient with thick retained secretions depends on cough for clearance. Suppressing that cough can worsen obstruction, atelectasis, and infection risk.
