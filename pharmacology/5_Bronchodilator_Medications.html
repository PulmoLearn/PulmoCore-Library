Not Every Narrow Airway Needs the Same Medication

Inhaled medications place drug at the airway target, often producing a faster local effect with a smaller dose and less systemic exposure than oral or parenteral therapy.

Bronchospasm
Active contraction of bronchial smooth muscle. Treat primarily with bronchodilators.
Inflammation
Airway edema and inflammatory-cell activity. Treat with anti-inflammatory therapy, not bronchodilator alone.
Secretions
Mucus obstruction or impaired clearance. Treat with hydration, humidification, airway clearance, and selected mucoactive therapy.
Clinical reasoning: Wheezing can occur with bronchospasm, edema, mucus, airway collapse, or a foreign body. The sound alone does not identify the medication.

Beta-2 Agonists Relax Airway Smooth Muscle

Beta-2 agonists increase intracellular cAMP, reduce calcium-driven contraction, and relax airway smooth muscle. At higher doses, selectivity becomes less complete and systemic effects become more likely.

ClassExamplesRoleKey caution
SABAAlbuterol, levalbuterol, terbutalineRapid relief of acute bronchospasmTremor, tachycardia, palpitations, hypokalemia
LABASalmeterol, formoterolLonger-duration maintenance bronchodilationNot a substitute for rapid rescue unless part of a specifically prescribed regimen
Continuous SABAContinuous nebulized albuterolSevere or refractory acute bronchospasmRequires close cardiac and electrolyte monitoring

Levalbuterol contains the R-isomer of albuterol. It may be considered when adverse effects are problematic, but it does not guarantee the absence of tachycardia or tremor.

Oral bronchodilators: Oral albuterol exists, but inhaled therapy is generally preferred because it acts more directly in the airway with less systemic exposure.

Monitor Before, During, and After Treatment

Bronchodilator administration is an assessment–intervention–reassessment cycle. Record baseline status, observe during therapy, and determine whether the medication produced meaningful benefit without unacceptable harm.

Therapeutic response
Less dyspnea, reduced work of breathing, improved air movement, increased PEFR or FEV₁, and improved ability to speak or exercise.
Adverse response
Marked tachycardia, new arrhythmia, chest pain, severe tremor, worsening distress, paradoxical bronchospasm, or clinically important hypokalemia.

Racemic Epinephrine Targets Upper-Airway Edema

Racemic epinephrine provides alpha-mediated vasoconstriction and beta-mediated bronchodilator effects. It is most commonly used to temporarily reduce upper-airway mucosal edema.

Common uses
Croup, post-extubation stridor or edema, and selected laryngeal edema.
Not routine first-line therapy
It is not the standard replacement for selective beta-2 agonists in uncomplicated lower-airway bronchospasm.

Antimuscarinics Block Cholinergic Bronchoconstriction

Antimuscarinic bronchodilators block acetylcholine at airway muscarinic receptors. This reduces bronchial smooth-muscle contraction and can decrease glandular secretion.

ClassMedicationTypical roleCommon considerations

LAMATiotropiumLong-acting maintenance bronchodilationNot a rapid rescue medication; available in device-specific formulations
Combination products: Ipratropium plus albuterol is available as DuoNeb for nebulization and Combivent Respimat as an inhalation spray.

Methylxanthines: Narrow Therapeutic Window, Many Interactions

Theophylline is a methylxanthine bronchodilator. Its use is limited by variable metabolism, drug interactions, and a narrow margin between therapeutic and toxic concentrations.

Potential benefit
Additional bronchodilation in selected patients when monitored carefully.
Toxicity risk
Nausea, vomiting, restlessness, irritability, tremor, insomnia, tachycardia, arrhythmias, and seizures.

Liver disease, heart failure, fever, age, smoking changes, and interacting medications can alter clearance. Toxicity becomes more likely as concentrations rise, especially above about 20 mcg/mL, but symptoms can occur at lower levels in susceptible patients.

Recognize Theophylline Toxicity

A COPD patient taking extended-release theophylline develops nausea, dizziness, restlessness, irritability, and palpitations after starting another medication.

Key Takeaways

Treat the cause of narrowing
Bronchodilators treat smooth-muscle constriction, not inflammation or mucus by themselves.
Rescue and maintenance are different

Reassessment proves effectiveness

Theophylline demands caution
