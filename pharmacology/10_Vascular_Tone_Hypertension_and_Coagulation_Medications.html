Arterioles Are the Main Resistance Vessels

Arterioles contain abundant vascular smooth muscle and create most of the adjustable resistance in the systemic circulation. Small changes in arteriolar radius can produce large changes in blood flow and blood pressure.

ConceptMeaningClinical implication
ComplianceHow easily a vessel expands as volume increasesHighly compliant vessels accommodate volume with less pressure rise

PreloadVentricular filling before contractionInfluenced by venous return and intravascular volume

Nitric oxide relaxes vascular smooth muscle and promotes vasodilation. Vascular smooth muscle tone is regulated largely by the autonomic nervous system and local mediators.

Blood Pressure Reflects Cardiac Output and Vascular Resistance

Blood pressure depends largely on cardiac output and systemic vascular resistance. Increased stroke volume or cardiac output tends to raise pressure, while decreased total peripheral resistance tends to lower it.

Baroreceptors are stretch receptors located in major arteries. They detect changes in vessel stretch and trigger reflex changes in autonomic tone.

Hypertension Damages Multiple Target Organs

Longstanding hypertension can contribute to left-ventricular hypertrophy, coronary and peripheral vascular disease, chronic kidney disease, stroke, and retinopathy. Prostatic hypertrophy is not a classic target-organ complication of hypertension.

For an asymptomatic patient with mildly elevated blood pressure and low immediate risk, lifestyle modification is often the first step: dietary change, sodium reduction, regular activity, weight management, limited alcohol intake, and smoking cessation. Medication decisions depend on repeated measurements, comorbidities, and current guidelines.

Loop Diuretics Are Commonly Used in Acute Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema

Loop diuretics remove sodium and water and can reduce vascular congestion. They are often used when acute pulmonary edema is caused by fluid overload or heart failure, alongside oxygenation, ventilatory support, vasodilators, and treatment of the cause.

Anticoagulants Prevent New Fibrin Clot Formation and Extension

Anticoagulants do not directly dissolve an established fibrin clot. They reduce clot formation or propagation while the body’s fibrinolytic system works.

MedicationCommon monitoringMain safety concern
Unfractionated heparinaPTT or anti-Xa, depending on protocolBleeding and heparin-induced thrombocytopenia
WarfarinPT/INRBleeding, drug and dietary interactions
Antiplatelet agentsClinical monitoring; selected laboratory testingBleeding

Potential bleeding signs include hematuria, blood in stool, petechiae, unusual bruising, prolonged bleeding, headache, or neurologic change. Nasal congestion by itself is not a classic anticoagulant adverse effect.

Thrombin Converts Fibrinogen into Fibrin

In the final clotting pathway, thrombin converts soluble fibrinogen into fibrin strands that stabilize the clot.
