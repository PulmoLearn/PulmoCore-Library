ABG Interpretation Practice

Use one repeatable process every time. First decide whether the ABG is trustworthy. Then interpret acid-base status, ventilation, and oxygenation as related—but separate—questions. The goal is not simply to name a disorder. It is to explain what the numbers mean physiologically, recognize compensation or mixed disease, and decide what the patient needs next.

Your ABG roadmap

Do the same steps in the same order.

1 · VERIFYDoes the sample fit the patient and support?
2 · pHAcidemia, alkalemia, or normal range?
3 · PRIMARYWhich component explains the pH?
4 · COMPENSATIONIs the other system responding as expected?
5 · CHRONICITYAcute, chronic, acute-on-chronic, or mixed?
6 · OXYGENAssess PaO₂/SpO₂ separately.
7 · PATIENTWhat does the patient need now?
Most important habit: A normal pH does not mean a normal ABG. PaCO₂, HCO₃⁻, and oxygenation can still be significantly abnormal.
Technical classification vs final interpretation: “Partially compensated respiratory acidosis” describes the acid-base pattern. A stronger final interpretation may add chronicity, mixed processes, oxygenation, and clinical meaning—for example, “chronic respiratory acidosis with acute worsening and hypoxemia.”
