The oxygen journey

Do not memorize these as isolated formulas.

Each value answers a different question. The clinical skill is knowing where you are in the oxygen pathway and what a normal or abnormal result means.

FiO₂ / PiO₂Did enough oxygen enter?
PAO₂Is enough O₂ available in the alveoli?
A–a / P:FDid oxygen cross into blood?
CaO₂How much O₂ is carried?
DO₂How much reaches tissues?
CvO₂ / SvO₂How much came back?
VO₂ / O₂ERHow much did tissues use?
Your recurring question: “If this value is abnormal, is the problem with inspired oxygen, alveolar ventilation, gas transfer, hemoglobin, circulation, or tissue extraction?”
About “normal” values: The ranges shown in this assignment are approximate resting-adult teaching references, not stand-alone treatment thresholds. Age, FiO₂, altitude/barometric pressure, hemoglobin, cardiac output, metabolic demand, disease state, and institutional reference ranges can change what is expected.

ValueFormulaWhat it tells you
PiO₂FiO₂ × (PB − PH₂O)Inspired oxygen pressure after accounting for barometric pressure and humidification.
PAO₂PiO₂ − (PaCO₂ / RQ)Estimated oxygen pressure available in the alveoli.
A–a gradientPAO₂ − PaO₂How much oxygen pressure is lost between alveolus and arterial blood.
P/F ratioPaO₂ / FiO₂How effectively the lungs oxygenate relative to supplied oxygen. This assignment uses >380 as normal.
CaO₂(1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)Total arterial oxygen content; most is carried on hemoglobin.
COHR × SV ÷ 1000Liters of blood pumped each minute.
DO₂CO × (CaO₂ × 10)Oxygen delivered to tissues each minute.
CvO₂(1.34 × Hb × SvO₂) + (0.003 × PvO₂)Oxygen content returning after tissue extraction.
C(a-v)O₂CaO₂ − CvO₂Arterial-to-venous oxygen content difference.
VO₂CO × [C(a-v)O₂ × 10]Oxygen consumed by tissues each minute.

Standard values used in practice: sea-level PB = 760 mmHg, PH₂O = 47 mmHg at normal body temperature, and RQ = 0.8 unless otherwise stated.
