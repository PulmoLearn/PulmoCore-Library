Oxygenation & Gas Exchange Calculations

Respiratory therapists use oxygenation calculations to understand how much oxygen reaches the alveoli, arteries, and tissues. This lesson introduces PiO₂, PAO₂, P(A-a)O₂, P/F ratio, CaO₂, DO₂, VO₂, and oxygen extraction using step-by-step clinical reasoning.

The Oxygen Journey

Before formulas make sense, students need the story. Oxygen begins as a fraction of inspired gas, becomes a pressure after humidification, enters the alveoli, diffuses into arterial blood, binds mostly to hemoglobin, gets delivered by cardiac output, and is extracted by tissues.

FiO₂
Fraction of inspired oxygen.
PAO₂
Estimated alveolar oxygen pressure.
CaO₂
Oxygen content in arterial blood.
DO₂
Oxygen delivered to tissues each minute.

PiO₂: Pressure of Inspired Oxygen

PiO₂ estimates the oxygen pressure available after inspired gas is humidified in the upper airway. Water vapor takes up some of the total pressure, so humidification matters.

PiO₂ = FiO₂ × (PB − PH₂O)
VariableMeaningCommon Value
FiO₂Fraction of inspired oxygen as a decimal.Room air = 0.21
PBBarometric pressure.Sea level ≈ 760 mmHg
PH₂OWater vapor pressure at body temperature.47 mmHg

Hint: 0.92 × (760 − 47)

PAO₂: Alveolar Oxygen Pressure

PAO₂ estimates how much oxygen pressure is present in the alveoli. It subtracts the effect of carbon dioxide because alveoli contain both oxygen entering and carbon dioxide leaving.

PAO₂ = FiO₂(PB − PH₂O) − (PaCO₂ / RQ)

PAO₂ Practice

Your patient is on 33% oxygen, at sea level, with PaCO₂ 37 mmHg and RQ 0.8. Round to the nearest whole number.

Hint: 0.33 × (760 − 47) − (37 / 0.8)

P(A-a)O₂: Alveolar-to-Arterial Gradient

The A-a gradient compares estimated alveolar oxygen pressure to measured arterial oxygen pressure. If the gap is large, oxygen is available in the alveoli but is not transferring well into arterial blood.

P(A-a)O₂ = PAO₂ − PaO₂

P/F Ratio: Oxygenation Severity

The P/F ratio compares PaO₂ to FiO₂. It helps describe how well oxygen is getting into arterial blood relative to how much oxygen the patient is receiving.

P/F Ratio = PaO₂ / FiO₂
Example: PaO₂ 80 on room air: 80 / 0.21 = 381. This is near normal. PaO₂ 80 on 60% oxygen: 80 / 0.60 = 133, which is much worse oxygenation.

CaO₂: Arterial Oxygen Content

PaO₂ tells oxygen pressure. CaO₂ estimates oxygen content in arterial blood. Most oxygen is carried by hemoglobin, while a tiny amount is dissolved in plasma.

CaO₂ = (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)

CaO₂ Practice

Hint: Use saturation as a decimal: 95% = 0.95.

DO₂ and VO₂: Delivery and Consumption

DO₂ estimates how much oxygen is delivered to tissues each minute. VO₂ estimates how much oxygen tissues consume. Both depend on oxygen content and cardiac output.

DO₂ = CO × (CaO₂ × 10)
VO₂ = CO × (C(a-v)O₂ × 10)
TermMeaning
Cardiac outputHeart rate × stroke volume; the amount of blood pumped per minute.
C(a-v)O₂Difference between arterial and venous oxygen content.
DO₂Oxygen delivery to tissues.
VO₂Oxygen consumed by tissues.

O₂ER: Oxygen Extraction Ratio

Oxygen extraction ratio estimates the percentage of delivered oxygen that tissues actually use. It rises when oxygen demand increases or oxygen delivery is not enough.

O₂ER = VO₂ / DO₂ × 100

FAQ

Common Oxygenation Calculation Questions

Why are there so many oxygen numbers?

Each number describes oxygen in a different location or form: inspired gas, alveoli, arterial pressure, oxygen content, delivery, consumption, and extraction.

Why does hemoglobin matter so much?

Most oxygen in blood is bound to hemoglobin, so low hemoglobin can reduce oxygen content even when PaO₂ looks acceptable.

What is the difference between PaO₂ and SaO₂?

PaO₂ is oxygen pressure dissolved in arterial blood. SaO₂ is the percentage of hemoglobin binding sites occupied by oxygen.

Why do we multiply by 10 in DO₂ and VO₂ formulas?

CaO₂ is usually in mL/dL, while cardiac output is in L/min. Multiplying by 10 converts dL to L for the final mL/min value.

Summary & Clinical Pearls

Oxygenation calculations follow the oxygen pathway. PiO₂ describes inspired oxygen pressure. PAO₂ estimates alveolar oxygen. The A-a gradient compares alveolar and arterial oxygen. P/F ratio describes oxygenation severity. CaO₂ estimates arterial oxygen content. DO₂, VO₂, and O₂ER describe delivery, consumption, and extraction.

Must know:
Most oxygen content comes from hemoglobin-bound oxygen.
Math pearl:
Use FiO₂ and saturation values as decimals.
Clinical pearl:
PaO₂ can look okay while CaO₂ is low if hemoglobin is low.
Common mistake:
Forgetting to multiply O₂ER by 100 when reporting a percent.
